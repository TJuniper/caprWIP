## Development-note summary

The widow note begins with a source table that already contains the main philological distinction the row still depends on. DEV_NOTES records `wuduwe ~ widuwe ~ weoduwe` from Campbell [@Campbell1959, §218], West Saxon `wuduwe` with `widuwe` explained from `widwe` in Luick [@Luick1914, §296], `WS OE wuduwe ~ widuwe, but Merc. widwe, North. widua` in Ringe-Taylor [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 322], and West Saxon `wuduwe` versus Anglian `widwe`, with Ælfrician `widewe`, in Bülbring [@Bulbring1902, §264]. From that evidence the early note already drew the essential conclusion that West Saxon `wuduwe` is the regular full form, that `widwe` represents a syncopated Anglian/Mercian form, and that `widuwe` is not the direct Lautgesetz outcome.

What changed later is the explanation of why the medial vowel survives. The earlier note still left too much room for an unfinished preservation story. The later widow canvass corrects that by ruling out the most tempting but wrong fix: `w` is **not** a blocker for medial unstressed `u > o`. DEV_NOTES preserves Campbell's wording that "By §§ 373, 385, *u* often appears as *o* and *e*, e.g. *beadowe*, *swalewan*" [@Campbell1959, §§365, 373], which directly shows lowering before `w`. It also notes that Campbell's preservation list contains `_u`, `_m`, `-uc`, and `-ung`, with no `w` [@Campbell1959, §373]; Hogg gives the same kind of list [@HoggPhonology1992, §3.3.1.3]; Brunner excludes `m` and `ng`, not `w` [@Brunner1965, §44 Anm. 7]; and Bülbring's `widewe` is direct counterevidence to any `w`-blocker claim [@Bulbring1902, §264].

The final DEV_NOTES resolution therefore keeps the earlier philological distinction but changes the mechanism. `widuwe` remains analogical, with Luick's "`widuwe nach widwe`" still doing the central work [@Luick1914, §221 Anm. 1]. `wuduwe` remains the regular West-Saxon target. But the mechanism is now progressive stem-`u` harmony, not a special `w`-blocker and not an open-syllable exception. DEV_NOTES states that Brunner and Luick explicitly support blocking unstressed `u`-lowering when the preceding stem syllable contains `u` and only one consonant intervenes [@Brunner1965, §44 Anm. 7; @Luick1914, §326.2]. That lets the OE-internal `*wi > *wu` rule feed the existing harmony exclusion and yields `wuduwe` from `*wíduwōn`. The later `bugun/sċufun` fallout is treated as a separate paradigm-cell problem in other rows, not as a reason to weaken the widow analysis.
