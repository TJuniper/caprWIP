## Reconstruction and early-stage forms

1. **Cognate-set proto:** PGmc interrogative stem *hwa-; nom.sg.masc. *hwaz
   (Goth. ƕas/hwas) [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 86].
2. **Project input form:** `*xwáz` in project transcription (x for the PGmc
   voiceless dorsal fricative, acute for stress).
3. **OE target form:** attested WS hwā, the citation cell for the English
   interrogative.

Derivation: *hwaz loses word-final *-z in a stressed monosyllable with
compensatory lengthening in the northern West Germanic dialect continuum
(SC097), giving *hwā directly [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 86]. Because the output
vowel is long back *ā created after the Anglo-Frisian fronting environment
was fixed, no fronting applies: OE hwā, never †hwǣ [@Campbell1959, §125
p. 49; @SieversBrunner1965, §137 Anm. 1 p. 129]. The executable cascade
enforces this by requiring a preceding nucleus in the long-final clause of
EAFBrighteningLongFinal.

**Alternative account (recorded, not adopted):** Campbell and Brunner allow a
generalized unaccented by-form *hwa lengthened later under stress. The
project adopts the R&T compensatory-lengthening account because it is the
regular sound-law path and matches the parallel pronoun evidence (*hiz type).

## Old English philology

WS hwā is abundantly attested as the ordinary interrogative 'who'
[@ClarkHall1960; @BosworthToller1898]. The nominative singular masculine is
the natural citation cell for the English lexeme; no unusual paradigm cell
was selected to force a rule to fire.

## Paradigm probe

Not required. The nom.sg.masc. cell is decisive on its own; oblique cells
(hwæs, hwǣm, hwone) involve short-vowel developments irrelevant to SC097.
