### Lexeme report

#### Reconstruction and early-stage alternatives

This row is designed to keep three levels apart. `PROTO = *ráukiz` is the etymological headword retained for the cognate set. `PROTOFORM = *ráukaz` is the project input chosen for the FST. The Old English target is `rēac`, explicitly treated in the row note as a reconstructed West Saxon outcome rather than as the directly attested manuscript form. The note also records the abandoned earlier target `rēc`, which is an attested Anglian smoothing relic rather than the report's present target.

#### Chronological source dossier

- `Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv`: the note says the row was retargeted from attested Anglian `rēc` to reconstructed West Saxon `rēac`, because the current FST already derives the latter regularly from `*ráukaz`.
- `Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md` §17.22 contains the earlier `rēc` dossier and is explicitly cited by the row note as the background to the final closure.
- The handbook backdrop is the ordinary development of PGmc `*au` to OE `ēa`, plus later dialect-specific smoothing issues [@RingeTaylor2014; @HoggGrammar1992].

#### Old English philology

The report must say plainly that `rēac` is reconstructed, not directly attested. The attested comparative material is the Anglian-type `rēc`, which proves inheritance of the lexeme but also shows that the textual record is filtered through dialectal smoothing and later lexical reshaping.

#### Project problem and solution

This is a `reconstructed_oe` case. The project deliberately prefers the regular West Saxon output `rēac` over the attested Anglian smoothing form because the goal of this row is to document the inherited phonological pathway, not to collapse dialectal smoothing and FST input choice into a single label. The note therefore preserves both facts: the target is reconstructed, and the attested Anglian form remains philologically real.
