The noun is a root noun whose nominative the sources print in three notations of the same morphology: Orel gives _\*bōkz_ with an explicit nominative marker, Kroonen cites the bare stem _\*bōk-_, and Kluge/Seebold print _\*bōks_ [@Orel2003, 52; @Kroonen2013, 71; @KlugeSeebold2011, 158]. Ringe notes that the consonant-stem nominative ending — zero, _\*-z_, or possibly _\*-s_ — is unrecoverable for monosyllables [@Ringe2017, 306]. The selected input _\*bōkz_ follows Orel's morphologically explicit convention.

The root-noun nominative marker was eliminated before Proto-West Germanic: no West Germanic daughter shows any ending in this class [@RingeTaylor2014, 118]. Its loss gives _\*bōk_, already the Old English form _bōc_ but for spelling; the mutated plural _bēċ_ preserves the class's umlaut alternation [@Fulk2018, 165]. This early morphological loss is distinct from the later West Germanic loss of final _\*z_ in unstressed syllables and from the northern monosyllabic loss.
