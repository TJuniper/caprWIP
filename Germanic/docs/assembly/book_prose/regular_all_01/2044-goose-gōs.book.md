The 'goose' word is a root noun with a layered nominative history. Orel prints _\*ǥansz_ with his explicit class-wide marker [@Orel2003, 126]; Kroonen reconstructs endingless _\*gans_ with Verner-voiced _z_ only in oblique _\*gunzaz_ [@Kroonen2013, 168]. Bammesberger supplies the deeper layer: Szemerényi's law gave PIE nominative _\*ǵʰanss_ > _\*ǵʰān_, after which the Proto-Germanic nominative was rebuilt as _\*ganz_ [@Bammesberger1990, 196]. The notations encode different analytical layers of one paradigm; the selected input _\*gánsz_ follows Orel's morphologically explicit convention.

The root-noun nominative marker was eliminated before Proto-West Germanic [@RingeTaylor2014, 118], giving _\*gáns_. The Ingvaeonic nasal-spirant sequence then lengthens and rounds the vowel and drops the nasal, yielding _gōs_; the mutated plural is _ġēs_ [@Fulk2018, 165].
