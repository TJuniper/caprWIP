Orel prints _\*lusz_ with his class-wide explicit nominative marker, citing Bammesberger and Griepentrog for the morphology [@Orel2003, 252]; Kroonen has no 'louse' entry [@Kroonen2013, 345]. The root-final _s_ is itself secondary: _\*luw-_ was extended with _s_ on the model of the 'mouse' word [@Bammesberger1990, 195], and as in Ringe's endingless _\*mūs_, degemination means no extra sibilant was ever audible in the nominative [@Ringe2017, 149]. The selected input _\*lūsz_ makes the inflectional marker visible without claiming a pronounced _[sz]_ cluster.

The root-noun nominative marker was eliminated before Proto-West Germanic [@RingeTaylor2014, 118], giving _\*lūs_, already the attested Old English form; the mutated plural is _lȳs_ [@Fulk2018, 165]. The root-final _s_ of _lūs_ was never eligible for the later West Germanic unstressed-final _\*z_ loss or the northern monosyllabic loss.
