# Dossier C — Later northern loss of word-final `*-z` in stressed monosyllables (2026)

Branch `sc001-sc020-chronology-audit`. Phase 1 of the three-rule *-z programme.
Research/documentation only: no production FST, protoform, TSV, metadata,
rule-ID, baseline, or binary change. This dossier proposes a historical rule but
no Foma syntax (Phase 3, after adjudication).

Treated independently of Dossier A (consonant-stem nominative `*-z`) and
Dossier B (PWGmc unstressed final `*-z` loss). Scope of implementation and
validation is the **Old English pipeline only** (per author instruction; the
Dutch/German/modern-English doculect cascades are not under active
maintenance).

## 1. Research question

What happened to word-final `*z` in **stressed monosyllables** — the class
`*maiz`, `*wiz/*wīz`, `*jiz`, `*miz`, `*þiz`, `*hiz`, `*iz`, `*hwaz`, `*kūz`
— in the ancestry of Old English? Is it a single regular sound change; what is
its geographic scope (northern WGmc, Ingvaeonic, Anglo-Frisian, narrower?);
what is its chronology relative to rhotacism; and what exactly conditions it
(stress, monosyllabicity, vowel frontness, quantity, weight, morphological
class, or interacting factors)?

CAPR context: after Dossiers A and B, this is the only remaining final-`*z`
class. The Phase 0 census found **zero** selected OE-baseline derivations
whose input ends in stressed vowel + `z`: the OE corpus rows systematically
use other paradigm cells for the relevant lexemes (cow: `*kūi` dat. sg. → cȳ,
row 1980, precisely because "nom. `*kōz`, obl. `*kū-`"; night: `*náxti` dat.
sg. → niht, row 2140; breast: `*bréustą` → brēost, row 1968). C is therefore a
candidate **presently-unwitnessed** rule in the OE baseline. (The stale
non-OE doculect rows contain nom. sg. `*kōz` etc.; they are out of scope and
are not used as witnesses here.)

## 2. The evidence to be explained

Southern WGmc (OHG) keeps a rhotacized reflex; northern WGmc (OE, OF, OS)
shows loss with compensatory lengthening. Gothic devoices (-s); ON rhotacizes
(-r). The core sets, from Ringe & Taylor (2014: 86, §3.3.1), verified against
the source:

- PGmc `*maiz` 'more' (Goth. mais, ON meir) > PWGmc `*maiz` > OE mā, OF mā
  ~ mē, OS, OHG mēr;
- PGmc `*wiz ~ *wīz` 'we' (Goth. weis, ON vér) > PWGmc `*wiz` > OE wē, OF,
  OS wī, OHG wir;
- PNWGmc `*jiz` 'you (nom. pl.)' (ON ér) > PWGmc `*jiz` > OE gē, OF jī, OS
  gī, OHG ir;
- PGmc `*miz` '(to) me' (Goth. mis, ON mér) > PWGmc `*miz` > OE mē, OF, OS
  mī, OHG mir;
- PGmc `*þiz` '(to) you (sg.)' (Goth. þus, ON þér) > PWGmc `*þiz` > OE þē,
  OF, OS thī, OHG dir;
- PGmc `*hiz` 'this (nom. sg. masc.)' > PWGmc `*hiz` > OE hē, OF hī, OS hī ~
  hē, all 'he';
- PGmc `*iz` 'he' (Goth. is) > PWGmc `*iz` → `*ez` > OHG er;
- PGmc `*hwaz` 'who?' (Goth. ƕas) > PWGmc `*hwaz` > OE, OF hwā; OS hwē, OHG
  wer "reflect a preform `*hwiz` → `*hwez` of uncertain antiquity but show the
  same pattern of development of word-final *z in monosyllables";
- PNWGmc `*kūz` 'cow' (?; ON kýr) > PWGmc `*kūz` (?) > OE cū. (R&T print the
  question marks; the reconstruction of this nominative is hedged. Cf.
  Bammesberger 1990: 192 "urg. `*kwō-z`" as an example of `*-z` after vowels;
  Fulk 2018: 167 on ON kýr, sýr, ær retaining -r < `*-z` with R-umlaut.)

Apparent counterexamples in the north, from the same page: "Nom. sg. forms
such as OE dēor 'animal' (PWGmc `*deuz`), ār 'bronze' (PWGmc `*aiz`), and gār
'spear' (PWGmc `*gaiz`) can of course owe their -r to levelling from forms
with overt endings" (R&T p. 86) — i.e. nouns with full paradigms restored -r
analogically from oblique/inflected forms where `*z` was medial and regularly
rhotacized.

OHG endingless monosyllables kuo 'cow', sū 'sow', drī 'three', zwā 'two' might
suggest the loss reached the south, but R&T (pp. 86–87) provide alternative
explanations for each (nom./acc. syncretism for kuo/sū; i-stem plural ending
or early `*-inz` > `*-ī` for drī; PIE `*dwō` endingless for zwā) and conclude:
"The certain examples of monosyllables ending in *-z did not lose that
consonant in OHG" (p. 87).

## 3. The competing analyses

### 3.1 Ringe & Taylor (2014): northern loss in all stressed monosyllables

"The development of word-final *z in monosyllables differed in the northern
and southern WGmc dialects: **in the north it was lost with compensatory
lengthening (and, in OE, lowering of *i), but in the south it survived and
became r**." (p. 86, §3.3.1; fn. 24: "This is the simplest formulation for OE,
in which *-iz always appears as -ē", with levelling caveats for hē/er after
Klein 1979: 430–44.)

Against stress-based accounts: "The suggestion that these words lost *-z
because they were unstressed (cf. e.g. Luick 1914–40: 819) **does not account
for OE mā and cū**, nor does it offer a convincing explanation for the
retention of *-z in OHG." (p. 86)

Chronology: "The most economical scenario that will give this pattern of
outcomes puts rhotacism at the end of the sequence of changes: first *z was
lost in a variety of environments in a variety of dialects, then all surviving
*z > *r across the whole WGmc area (cf. Crist 2001: 106–8). Since only the
last change was uniformly shared by all the dialects, and since it is clear
that rhotacism is a 'natural' sound change that could easily spread, it seems
advisable to assign **the entire process to the post-PWGmc period**." (p. 87)
Separation from B: pp. 44–45 ("clearly a late change, probably post-PWGmc";
"two historically separate sound changes (so Crist 2001: 107–8)" — see
Dossier B §2.1).

Environment on this analysis: word-final `*z` in a stressed monosyllable,
**any vowel quality or quantity** (i, ī, a, ai, ū all occur), northern WGmc.

### 3.2 Crist (2002): one Ingvaeonic front-vowel rule for finals and preconsonantal codas

Crist's LSA handout proposes "a regular sound change in the Ingvaeonic
dialects of West Germanic (OE, OFris, OS): `*z > ∅ / {*i,*e} _ $` **with
compensatory lengthening** of preceding vowel" (handout p. 1, §1). One rule
covers both the word-final monosyllables (`*miz`, `*wīz`, `*maiz` — "the *i
element of the diphthong triggers secondary z-deletion", p. 4 §10) and the
preconsonantal stressed codas (`*mizdō` > mēd, `*hezdōn` > heorde/hēde,
`*twiznaz` > twīn, `*liznōn` > līnon).

Supporting observations (handout p. 3, §8): "(a) Non-final *z-deletion never
occurs after back vowels in any WGmc language; not even one exception. (b)
Non-final *z-deletion never occurs outside OE, OFris, OS, with the sole
exception of OHG mēde" (which he takes as an Ingvaeonic trade loan).

Relative chronology (handout p. 4, §10): the rule **follows** the Ingvaeonic
lowering `*i > *e / _ *z`; **precedes** the Ingvaeonic `*ai > *ā` shift
("since the *i element of the diphthong triggers secondary z-deletion"); and
**precedes rhotacism** ("original *r does not delete in this environment";
`*hēr` > OE hēr, handout p. 2, §6).

Limits of Crist's account, stated precisely:

1. His data set contains **no word-final back-vowel monosyllable**: `*hwaz`,
   `*kūz`, `*sūz` appear neither in his tables (handout p. 1) nor in his Torp
   appendix (front-vowel list: `*wīz`, `*liznōn`, `*laizijaną`, `*maiz(-an)`,
   `*twizna/-ōn`, `*þrizna`, `*mizdō(n)`, `*hezdōn`, `*kizna`, `*waizda`;
   back-vowel list: only preconsonantal `*uzdaz`, `*hazdaz`, `*huzda`,
   `*hraznō`, `*gazdaz`, `*bruzdaz`, `*nazjan`, `*mazgaz`, `*razna`,
   `*razdō`, `*uzlaga`, `*walakuzjōn`). His observation (a) is explicitly
   about **non-final** position. As formulated, `{*i,*e} _` cannot generate
   OE hwā < `*hwaz` or cū < `*kūz`; the handout does not address them (it
   quotes Campbell's list including hwa only as the traditional "unaccented"
   account, p. 3 §7).
2. OE meord/heorde, leornian are exceptions he must handle with a
   northern-Jutland dialect-mixture hypothesis (handout p. 3, §9).

### 3.3 Kilday (2024): split Crist's two environments

Kilday ("Crist's Law, Smith's Law, and English wizen", 2024 draft,
`docs/references/kilday_2024_crists_law_smiths_law_wizen.txt`) explicitly
separates the two environments Crist unified:

- **Word-final stressed monosyllables** (`*miz`, `*wīz`, `*maiz`): accepted
  for OE and OS — "Old English (mē, wē, mā) deletes it, as does Old Saxon in
  the pronouns (mī; wī, we) … For the adverb 'more', Old Saxon and Old
  Frisian attest both mē and mēr, the latter easily explained by analogical
  restoration. A parallel restoration underlies the Modern English adverb
  more." (pp. 1–2)
- **Preconsonantal "Crist's Law"** (`*-(e/i)z(d/n)-` > `*-(ē/ī)(d/n)-`):
  "operated in Old Saxon and Old Frisian, **but not directly in Old English**,
  so it should not be ascribed to the Ingvaeonic stage" (abstract); it was
  "restricted to a smaller part of Ingvaeonia, and could have operated around
  the year 500", with OE mēd a Saxono-Frisian loan and **meord the regular OE
  outcome** (pp. 2–3).

CAPR has already adopted Kilday's position for the preconsonantal subcase:
corpus row 2124 (meed) selects dat. sg. `*mízdai` > meorde as the
lautgesetzlich OE derivation and treats WS mēd as a Saxono-Frisian loan
(HISTORY note on the row; full survey in
`Germanic/docs/analysis/meord_med_chronological_review.md`). Consistency
therefore requires that CAPR **not** implement Crist's unified `{i,e}`-rule
for OE; whatever rule C becomes, it must cover only the word-final
monosyllable class.

### 3.4 Luick / Campbell: unstressed pronominal loss (superseded)

Campbell (1959: 166, quoted in Crist's handout p. 3 §7): "z is lost … in the
unaccented pronominal words OE we, ġe, he me, þe, hwa"; similarly Luick
1914–40: 819. This makes the loss a function of sentence-level lack of stress
in pronouns. R&T's refutation (p. 86, quoted in §3.1): mā and cū are not
pronouns and not characteristically unstressed, and OHG retention is
unexplained. Under this account the class would also wrongly merge with
Dossier B. No modern source consulted defends it.

### 3.5 Fulk / Markey: an Ingvaeonic diagnostic feature

"Markey (1976: 36–71) identifies thirty-six features as typically Ingvaeonic,
among the most important of which is **loss of *-z in monosyllabic pronouns
like OE dat. mē (cf. OHG mir)**." (Fulk 2018: 18 n. 6, §1.10.) Fulk frames
the scope as Ingvaeonic (OE/OF/OS = North Sea Germanic), matching R&T's
"northern WGmc" extensionally. Fulk's surrounding discussion (§1.10 with
n. 7, citing Stiles 2013) notes that most Ingvaeonic innovations postdate
WGmc unity — consistent with R&T's post-PWGmc dating.

| Syllable weight | Not a condition (follows from quantity) | ibid. |
| Morphological/lexical class | Not a condition on R&T's analysis (pronouns + adverb + noun cū); pronominal-only on the superseded Luick/Campbell view | R&T p. 86 |
| Geography | Northern WGmc = Ingvaeonic (OE/OF/OS); south (OHG) retains → r | R&T p. 86; Fulk p. 18 n. 6; Crist §1 |
| Interacting factors | Analogical restoration (OS/OF mēr, ModE more; OE gār/ār/dēor levelling); paradigm levelling caveats for hē (fn. 24) | R&T p. 86 + fn. 24; Kilday p. 2 |

**Is it a single regular sound change?** Within the word-final monosyllable
class, yes on all modern accounts (R&T: regular northern change; Crist:
regular Ingvaeonic rule; Kilday: accepted subcase). The genuine dispute is
(i) whether the same rule also covers the preconsonantal codas (Crist yes;
Kilday no — OE excluded; CAPR has already sided with Kilday via the meed
row), and (ii) whether the environment mentions frontness (Crist) or not
(R&T). On (ii), R&T's formulation is preferred here because hwā and cū are
directly attested OE outcomes of back-vowel preforms and Crist's handout
simply does not treat them; but Crist's rule must be represented accurately
as a competing analysis, not silently discarded — if `*hwaz` in fact went
through `*hwez`-type fronting first (R&T p. 86 record OS hwē, OHG wer <
`*hwiz` → `*hwez` "of uncertain antiquity"), parts of the class could in
principle be reconciled with a front-vowel condition, though cū could not.

## 5. Chronology

1. **Post-PWGmc**: "clearly a late change, probably post-PWGmc" (R&T p. 44);
   "assign the entire process to the post-PWGmc period" (p. 87). After
   Dossier B (which had already removed all unstressed final `*z`), and long
   after Dossier A's class was endingless.
2. **Before rhotacism**, which "puts rhotacism at the end of the sequence"
   (R&T p. 87); deletion is `*r`/`*z`-sensitive (`*hēr` keeps r, Crist §6).
   The southern dialects rhotacize exactly what the north deleted.
3. Crist's internal ordering for his version: after Ingvaeonic `*i`-lowering
   before `*z`; before the Ingvaeonic `*ai > *ā` shift (handout p. 4, §10).
   In R&T's version no ordering against `*ai > *ā` is imposed by `*maiz`
   (loss + compensatory lengthening yields `*mai` → mā by the regular
   monophthongization either way); CAPR need not adjudicate this unless a
   witness appears.
4. Kilday's absolute anchor for the (excluded) preconsonantal subcase:
   "around the year 500", post-settlement (p. 3) — corroborating how late
   this family of changes runs.

## 6. CAPR status: unwitnessed in the OE baseline

- The Phase 0 census over the selected OE-baseline derivations found **no
  input ending in stressed vowel + `z`**: none of SC020's 114 firings belongs
  to class C, and no C-class form survives to be mishandled.
- This is not accidental: the corpus deliberately selects oblique cells for
  the would-be C witnesses (cow `*kūi` → cȳ, row 1980, whose note records
  nom. `*kōz`; night `*náxti` → niht, row 2140; breast `*bréustą` → brēost,
  row 1968) and derives meed via `*mízdai` → meorde (row 2124), the
  Kilday-consistent regular path.
- Rhotacism controls confirming that stressed/medial `*z` otherwise survives
  to rhotacize in CAPR: deer `*déuzan`, berry, hoard, learn (3 rows), meed
  (Phase 0 audit §5). R&T's dēor/ār/gār levelling note (p. 86) covers the
  nominative -r of such nouns.
- Per the programme and existing project practice, C may be **retained as a
  historically genuine but presently unwitnessed rule** in the OE pipeline.
  No protoform may be altered to manufacture a witness.
- (Aside, out of scope: stale non-OE doculect rows carry nom. sg. `*kōz`
  'cow' etc. and currently pass through the shared `OldEnglishCore`; the
  Dutch/German/English cascades are not under maintenance and are not used
  for design or validation. Phase 3 should merely avoid gratuitously breaking
  them.)

## 7. Proposed historical rule (for Phase 2 adjudication; no Foma syntax)

**Rule C (working name: northern WGmc stressed-monosyllable final `*-z` loss
with compensatory lengthening).**

- **Historical content**: loss of word-final `*z` in stressed monosyllables,
  with compensatory lengthening of the preceding vowel (and, for `*-iz`,
  OE lowering to -ē per R&T's "simplest formulation", p. 86 fn. 24).
- **Environment**: word-final `*z` immediately after a vowel in a stressed
  monosyllable. Vowel quality unrestricted (R&T formulation adopted; Crist's
  front-vowel alternative documented as a competing analysis — §4).
- **Stage/scope**: post-PWGmc, northern WGmc / Ingvaeonic (OE, OF, OS);
  not OHG (which rhotacizes), not NGmc, not Gothic.
- **Ordering**: after Dossier B's rule (feeding relationship: B has removed
  unstressed final `*z`, so C's domain is exactly the stressed residue);
  before rhotacism (which comes last, R&T p. 87). In CAPR's cascade this
  ordering is expressible even though `EAFRhotacism` executes early, because
  the FST's rhotacism is non-final by scoping — but the historically faithful
  slot is late in the pre-OE sequence; exact placement is Phase 3 design
  work, subject to the constraint that it must not capture A's or B's
  classes.
- **Witnesses**: none in the selected OE baseline (recorded as such);
  comparative witnesses mā, wē, gē, mē, þē, hē, hwā, cū (R&T p. 86).
- **Negative controls**: medial `*z` (deer, berry, hoard, learn, meed —
  must continue to rhotacism); `*hēr`-type original `*r` (untouched);
  A-class consonant-final monosyllables (book, flea, goose, louse — must be
  handled by A, not C); B-class unstressed finals.
- **Distinctness**: C ≠ B (stage, stress, geography: R&T pp. 44–45 "two
  historically separate sound changes"); C ≠ A (A is pre-PWGmc,
  post-consonantal, shared conditionally with NGmc; C is post-PWGmc,
  post-vocalic, northern-only); C ≠ Crist's Law sensu Kilday (preconsonantal
  subcase, OS/OFris only, already excluded from OE by the meed adjudication).

## 9. Conclusion

The later monosyllable development is real, late (post-PWGmc), and northern
(Ingvaeonic: OE, OF, OS): word-final `*z` after a vowel in a stressed
monosyllable was lost with compensatory lengthening, while southern WGmc kept
the segment and rhotacized it. It is historically distinct from Dossier B
(stage, stress environment, geography — R&T pp. 44–45 explicitly) and from
Dossier A (which concerns a morphological ending gone before PWGmc). The
principal live disagreement is Crist's front-vowel formulation versus R&T's
quality-neutral one; this dossier adopts R&T's (hwā, cū) while documenting
Crist's accurately, and follows Kilday — as CAPR's meed adjudication already
does — in excluding the preconsonantal "Crist's Law" subcase from Old English
entirely. In the OE baseline the rule is **presently unwitnessed**: no
selected derivation ends in stressed vowel + `z`, because the corpus already
selects oblique paradigm cells for cow, night, breast, and meed. C should
accordingly be carried as a genuine but unwitnessed historical rule, with no
protoform alterations to manufacture witnesses.
