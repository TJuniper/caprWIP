# Dossier A — Early root-noun / consonant-final nominative `*-z` (2026)

Branch `sc001-sc020-chronology-audit`. Phase 1 of the three-rule *-z programme.
Research/documentation only: no production FST, protoform, TSV, metadata,
rule-ID, baseline, or binary change. This dossier proposes a historical rule but
no Foma syntax (that is Phase 3, after adjudication).

Companion dossiers: B (PWGmc unstressed final `*-z` loss) and C (later northern
stressed-monosyllable `*-Vz` loss) are treated separately. Evidence is not
imported between dossiers without explicit argument.

Supersedes the verdicts of `sc020-final-z-monosyllable-dossier.md` (author
decision: `*bōkz`, `*gánsz`, `*lūsz` are retained in PROTOFORM; CAPR's input is
Proto-Germanic; the `*-sz` notation is morphologically explicit, not erroneous).

## 1. Research question

CAPR's corpus contains seven Proto-Germanic PROTOFORMs ending in consonant +
`z` (Phase 0 census, `sc020-three-rule-phase0-current-state.md` §5):

Stressed monosyllables (4):

- book:  `*bōkz`   → OE bōc
- flea:  `*fláuxz` → OE flēah
- goose: `*gánsz`  → OE gōs
- louse: `*lūsz`   → OE lūs

Polysyllables with unstressed final syllable (3):

- friend: `*fríjōndz` → OE frēond
- milk:   `*mélukz`   → OE meolc
- month:  `*mḗnōθz`   → OE mōnaþ

All are athematic consonant stems (root nouns or, for friend/month, older
consonant-stem formations). What historical development removes this `*-z`, at
what stage, in what environment, and is it the same development as the general
unstressed final-`*z` loss (Dossier B) or the later stressed-monosyllable
`*-Vz` loss (Dossier C)?

This dossier does **not** begin from the conclusion that a notation such as
`*-sz` is spurious. The question is what the notation represents and what
historical development CAPR should model.

## 2. Four representational layers

Following the author's instruction, the analysis distinguishes:

1. **Morphological reconstruction** — the analysis of the nominative singular
   as root + case ending (e.g. `*bōk-` + nom. sg. `*-z`).
2. **Conventional etymological notation** — a dictionary's citation form (e.g.
   Orel's systematic `*bōkz`; Kroonen's systematic stem citation `*bōk-`).
3. **Predicted phonetic realization** — what a speaker plausibly said (e.g.
   whether `*-sz`, `*-kz` were stable clusters, or simplified early, or never
   articulated as written).
4. **Later historical reflex** — Gothic `-s`, ON `-r`/∅, WGmc ∅.

Sources that appear to "disagree" often operate at different layers. Orel's
`*bōkz` (layer 2 encoding layer 1) and Kroonen's `*bōk-` (layer 2, stem only)
are not automatically contradictory reconstructions of layer 3.

## 3. What each source actually reconstructs

### 3.1 Orel (2003), *A Handbook of Germanic Etymology*

Orel's citation forms for consonant stems carry an explicit nominative `*-z`
**systematically**, across the whole letter range — not just for the three focal
words:

- `*bōkz` sb.f., p. 52: "ON bók 'beech; book', OE bóc …". Cross-referenced
  from `*bōkō` sb.f. (Goth boka 'letter'), also p. 52.
- `*flauxz` sb.m./f., p. 105.
- `*frijōndz` sb.m., p. 114: "Participle of `*frijōjan`"; cites Bammesberger,
  *Nom.* 215.
- `*ǥansz` sb.f., p. 126: "Span ganso id. is from Goth `*gansus`"; cites
  Bammesberger, *Nom.* 196, and Griepentrog, *Wurzelnomina* 201–232.
- `*lusz` sb.f., p. 252: cites Bammesberger, *Nom.* 195, and Griepentrog,
  *Wurzelnomina* 257–271.
- `*melukz` sb.f., p. 267.
- `*mēnōþz` sb.m., p. 270.

Because the `-z` appears on every consonant-stem headword, it is a
**morphologically explicit citation convention**: root + the productive Gmc
nominative marker `*-z` (the Verner-voiced reflex of PIE `*-s`). Orel is not
claiming a securely reconstructed phonetic `[sz]` cluster for 'goose' or
'louse'; he is encoding the morphological analysis nom. sg. = stem + `*-z`.

### 3.2 Kroonen (2013), *Etymological Dictionary of Proto-Germanic*

Kroonen cites consonant stems by **bare stem**:

- `*bōk-` f. 'book', pp. 71–72: "Also cf. Go. boka f. 'letter' … < *bōka/ō-.
  Related to *bōk(j)ō- 'beech'."
- `*gans-` f. 'goose', p. 168; and under `*ganuta(n)-`, p. 169: "oblique stem
  of an originally ablauting paradigm `*gans`, gen. `*gunzaz` <
  `*ǵʰh₂én-s`, `*ǵʰh₂n̥-s-ós`".
- `*meluk-` f. 'milk', p. 364.
- `*mēnōþ-` m. 'month', p. 365.
- **No entry for 'louse'** (the sequence runs `*lustu-` → `*lūtan-` directly).
- `*flauhō-` f. 'flea', p. 145 — an **ō-stem**, not a root noun.

Two substantive points. First, for 'goose' Kroonen's explicit paradigm is
nominative `*gans` (endingless at the surface: PIE `*ǵʰh₂én-s` yields a form
whose final sibilant is the root-final `s`, not a separate audible ending) with
Verner-voiced `z` only in the oblique (`*gunzaz`). Second, for 'flea' Kroonen's
reconstruction (`*flauhō-`) disagrees at the level of **stem class** with
Orel's root-noun `*flauxz`; ON fló, OE flēah are compatible with both.
Kluge/Seebold s.v. *Floh* (p. 304, printed page confirmed against the PDF
page labels) add a **third** stem-class analysis, masculine a-stem
`*flauha-`, whose nom. sg. `*flauhaz` would have lost `-z` with the
unstressed a-stem endings (SC020) rather than with the root nouns; the
attested forms are compatible with all three analyses, and the selected
input remains Orel's `*flauxz` (pre-push adjudication, this programme).

### 3.3 Ringe (2017), *From Proto-Indo-European to Proto-Germanic*, 2nd edn.

- Consonant-stem endings table, p. 306: nom. sg. **"∅ ~ -z (~ -s?)"** — with
  the note: "Whether there was a nom. sg. alternant *-s depends on whether the
  leveling of Verner's Law alternants in favor of *-z in nominal endings
  affected all monosyllabic nouns, a detail which is unrecoverable."
- 'mouse', p. 149: "PIE *mū́s 'mouse', gen. sg. *mūsés … > PGmc **\*mūs**,
  gen. sg. *mūsiz (with Verner's Law alternants leveled)" — i.e. for an
  s-final root the nominative is cited endingless: PIE `*-s-s` had already
  been reduced (degemination), and PGmc *mūs shows no separate ending.
- Root-noun paradigm table, p. 313: nom. sg. **"fōts? (fōs?)"**, **"tanþs?
  (tans?)"**, **"nahts? (nahs?)"**, **"mūs"**; 'brother' endingless `*brōþēr`,
  gen. `*brōþurz`. The double question marks encode exactly the layer-3
  uncertainty: whether the cluster (`*-ts`) survived or was simplified
  (`*-s`) in PGmc is left open.
- Polysyllabic consonant stems (`*mēnōþ-`, `*wītwōd-`, `*magaþ-`), p. 311.

Ringe therefore reconstructs the **morphology** confidently (an animate
consonant-stem nominative in `*-z` ~ `*-s` or ∅) while flagging the
**phonetics** of obstruent + sibilant nominatives as unrecoverable.

### 3.4 Ringe & Taylor (2014), *The Development of Old English*

- p. 28 (root-noun u-lowering): the root-noun nom. sg. "(which either ended in
  *-s or *-z, or was endingless)" — same three-way agnosticism.
- p. 118 (root nouns in PWGmc): "Whatever the nom. sg. ending of root nouns
  might have been in PGmc … it appears that there was **no ending in PWGmc, as
  none of the daughters exhibits any**." This is the decisive terminus for
  CAPR: by PWGmc the consonant-stem nominative is endingless in West Germanic,
  whatever its PGmc shape was.
- pp. 44–5 treat `*fadurz` > `*fadur` (r-stem gen. sg.) under the general
  unstressed final-`*z` loss — see Dossier B; relevant here only for the
  friend/milk/month allocation question (§7).

### 3.5 Fulk (2018), *A Comparative Grammar of the Early Germanic Languages*

- §7.2, p. 143: by **Szemerényi's law**, the nom. sg. ending `*-s` was lost
  after sonorant-final consonant stems (with compensatory lengthening), and
  endinglessness was then **extended analogically** to other consonant stems.
- §§7.26–7.27, pp. 165–166: Griepentrog identifies 23 Germanic root-stems;
  paradigms of Go. reiks / OIcel. maðr, and Go. baúrgs / OIcel. bók / OE bōc /
  OS+OHG naht: Gothic shows nom. sg. **-s** (baúrgs, nahts, reiks…); OIcel.
  bók and OE bōc are endingless.
- §7.28, pp. 167–168: "PGmc. nom. sg. *burz-s > *burxs"; in ON "the reflex of
  PIE *-s was retained in a few fem. vocalic stems, where the vowel is subject
  to R-umlaut (§4.7): **kýr, sýr, ær**"; OE gen./dat. sg. bēc is original;
  Szemerényi's-law discussion with Lat. pēs < *ped-s; 'tooth' is the only
  root noun with provable Gmc root ablaut.

Fulk thus gives the fullest **phonological** account (cluster simplification by
Szemerényi's law + analogy) and the key ON conditioning datum: root nouns whose
root ends in a **vowel** (kýr, sýr, ær < `*kō-z`, `*sū-z`, `*awi-z`-type)
**retain** the nominative as ON -r (< `*-z`, with R-umlaut), while root nouns
whose root ends in a **consonant** (bók, gás, lús, mús, nótt) are endingless.

### 3.6 Bammesberger (1990), *Die Morphologie des urgermanischen Nomens*

The most explicit morphological treatment (cited by Orel as *Nom.*):

- §2.5.3.1, p. 23: PIE animate nominative marker `*-s`, with lengthened root
  grade in root-noun nominatives (`*pōds`, `*rēǵs`); Szemerényi's alternative
  (assimilation `*pod-s` > `*poss` > `*pōs` with degemination and
  compensatory lengthening) reported.
- §8.2.3.1, pp. 190–192 (nominative singular of Germanic root nouns):
  - Expected `*fōs` (< PIE `*pōs`) was isolated and was replaced; the
    root-final consonant was **restituted from the oblique cases** (and/or the
    nominative rebuilt on the accusative: `*fōtuz` after acc. `*fōtun`, hence
    Go. u-stem fotus, OE hnutu/studu-type residues).
  - "Jedenfalls bei den Stämmen auf stimmhaften Konsonanten wurde wohl der
    Nominativ Singular durch **`*-z`**, das an die in den obliquen Kasus
    gebräuchliche Alternante antrat, charakterisiert: urg. **`*burgz`** führt
    zu got. baurgs, und urg. **`*frijōndz`** genügt als Ausgangspunkt für got.
    frijonds." (p. 192) — i.e. for voiced-final stems the PGmc nominative
    **did** carry an overt `*-z` attached to the root.
  - "Wie der Nominativ Singular bei Wurzeln auf stimmlose Konsonanten lautete,
    läßt sich kaum feststellen" — ON fótr could have added -r only after
    `*-z` > -R, and "erweist sicherlich nicht eine **phonotaktisch kaum
    denkbare** Vorform urg. `*fōt-z`" (p. 192): a voiceless-obstruent +
    `*-z` nominative is phonotactically "hardly conceivable" as a stable PGmc
    surface form.
  - "Im Gotischen wurde auslautendes *-z generell stimmlos (baurgs, reiks),
    **im Westgermanischen fiel es ab**." (p. 192)
  - Footnote 306, pp. 192–193: in ON the **masculine** root noun shows -r <
    `*-z` (fótr), while the **feminines** are endingless: "nótt 'Nacht', geit,
    mjǫlk."
- §8.3 (root-noun list): `*lūs-`, p. 195 — Pokorny's PIE `*lū-s`, `*luw-ós`;
  in PGmc `*lūs-` the `*-s-` is part of the (extended) root, its model being
  'mouse'. `*gans-`, p. 196 — Szemerényi: PIE nom. `*ǵʰanss` > `*ǵʰans` >
  `*ǵʰann` > `*ǵʰān` (Gr. χήν); in Germanic the nominative was rebuilt, "der
  wohl im Urgermanischen **`*ganz`** gelautet hat", reanalyzable as `*gan-z`,
  whence the secondary stem `*gan-` underlying `*gan-ut-/-at-a-` (OE ganot,
  OHG ganazzo 'gander'). `*mel(u)k-`, p. 196 (Go. miluks a consonant stem).
- §7.6, pp. 186–187: `*mēnōþ-` 'month' beside `*mēnan-` 'moon': PIE nom.
  `*mēnōs` > PGmc `*mēnōz`; 'moon' arose "nach dem **nicht lautgesetzlichen**
  Schwund von *-z" (loss of the `*-z` in `*mēnōz` > `*mēnō` was *not* regular
  sound change), with circumflex intonation and transfer to the n-stems; the
  'month' paradigm restored `-þ-` from the oblique cases. Similarly `*nefōz` >
  `*nefa` (p. 187).
- §9.4.2–9.4.3, p. 215: nd-stems (frijōnd-, fijand-) with Gothic nom. sg. in
  -s (frijonds, nasjands paradigm).

### 3.7 Kluge/Seebold (2011), *Etymologisches Wörterbuch*, 25th edn.

- Buch, p. 158: "Aus g. *bōk-(ō) f. … (ursprünglich Wurzelnomen)"; writes
  "*bōks 'Buchstabe' ursprünglich ein Wurzelnomen" — nominative cited with
  **-s** (unvoiced notation), root-noun analysis affirmed.
- Gans, p. 331: "(ursprünglich offenbar Konsonantstamm, später i-Stamm)";
  cites Griepentrog (1995), 211–232.
- Laus, p. 563: "Aus g. *lūs- f."; the s is an "s-Erweiterung zu einem *luw-";
  cites Griepentrog (1995), 257–263 — i.e. the sibilant of 'louse' is **root
  material** (an extension), matching Bammesberger p. 195.

### 3.8 Crist (2001/2002), *Conspiracy in historical phonology* (z-loss chapters)

Crist's two z-deletions concern `*-Vz#` sequences (unstressed loss; later
Ingvaeonic monosyllable loss) — see Dossiers B and C. He does not treat the
consonant-stem nominative cluster as a separate WGmc sound change; it is
already gone from his input forms. This is itself evidence for early (pre-WGmc
or PWGmc-initial) removal.

## 4. The morphology of the consonant-stem nominative singular

Synthesis of §3: the PIE animate nom. sg. marker was `*-s` with lengthened root
grade in root nouns. Verner's law created `*-s ~ *-z` alternants in endings;
leveling in favor of `*-z` in nominal endings is secure for vowel-final stems
(a-stem `*-az`, i-stem `*-iz`, u-stem `*-uz`) but, per Ringe (2017: 306),
**unrecoverable** for monosyllabic consonant stems. The nominative of a
consonant stem was therefore, depending on root-final segment and analogy:

| Root-final segment | PGmc nominative (best evidence) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| vowel/diphthong | overt `*-z`, survives as ON -r with R-umlaut | `*kō-z` (kýr), `*sū-z` (sýr), `*awiz` (ær) |
| voiced consonant | overt `*-z` restituted on oblique root | `*burgz` (Go. baúrgs), `*frijōndz` (Go. frijonds) — Bammesberger p. 192 |
| voiceless obstruent | indeterminable: `*-ts`/`*-s`/∅ ("fōts? (fōs?)") | `*fōt-`, `*naht-`, `*bōk-` — Ringe p. 313; Bammesberger p. 192 |
| root-final `*-s` | endingless by degemination | `*mūs` (Ringe p. 149), `*lūs-` (Bammesberger p. 195), `*gans` (Kroonen p. 168) |

The `-z` in Orel's `*bōkz`, `*ǥansz`, `*lusz` is thus an **inflectional marker
in morphologically explicit notation** — root + the productive nominative
`*-z` — and for the voiced-final and vowel-final classes such a nominative is
positively reconstructible (Bammesberger's `*burgz`, `*frijōndz`; Fulk's ON
kýr/sýr/ær). For voiceless-obstruent and s-final roots the overt-cluster form
is a **morphological/notational construct** whose phonetic reality in PGmc is
either doubtful (`*fōt-z` "phonotactically hardly conceivable", Bammesberger
p. 192) or excluded (degemination already in PIE for `*mūs`-type). None of
this makes the notation erroneous: it encodes a real morphological analysis
plus a real, attested marker (Go. -s; ON -r), and reputable sources genuinely
differ in how they print it (Orel `*bōkz` : Kluge/Seebold `*bōks` : Kroonen
`*bōk-`/`*gans` : Ringe "fōts? (fōs?)").

## 5. Are book, goose, louse (and flea) exactly parallel?

Only superficially. All four are stressed monosyllabic consonant-final
nominatives, but they instantiate **three different phonotactic situations**:

1. **book `*bōkz`, flea `*fláuxz`** — voiceless obstruent + `*-z`. The overt
   cluster is the doubtful "fōts?/nahts?" type (Ringe p. 313); Gothic attests
   -s on this class (nahts, reiks, baúrgs), WGmc nothing. For flea there is
   additionally a **stem-class dispute**: Kroonen p. 145 reconstructs ō-stem
   `*flauhō-` (no `-z` at any layer), against Orel's root noun `*flauxz`
   (p. 105).
2. **goose `*gánsz`** — root-final `*-ns` + `*-z`. Kroonen p. 168–169 gives an
   endingless nominative `*gans` (PIE `*ǵʰh₂én-s`, root-final s) with Verner
   `*z` only obliquely (`*gunzaz`); Bammesberger p. 196 reconstructs PGmc nom.
   `*ganz` (single final sibilant, voiced) with reanalysis `*gan-z` feeding
   `*ganut-` 'gander'. Orel's `*ǥansz` stacks the root s and the marker z.
3. **louse `*lūsz`** — root-final `*-s` + `*-z`. The root s is itself an
   extension (`*luw-` + s, Kluge/Seebold p. 563; Bammesberger p. 195 on the
   'mouse' model). The `*mūs` parallel (Ringe p. 149) shows the expected PGmc
   nominative is endingless `*lūs` by degemination.

So the three focal words share the **morphological** analysis (root noun +
nominative `*-z`) and the **outcome** (endingless nominative in all WGmc), but
the predicted phonetic realizations differ, and for goose/louse the best
phonological reconstruction never contained an audible extra sibilant at all.
This heterogeneity is precisely why the historical development is best framed
morphologically (§8) rather than as a narrow-phonetic cluster law.

## 6. Daughter-language and runic evidence

- **Gothic**: nominative **-s retained** throughout the consonant stems:
  baúrgs, nahts, reiks, miluks, mēnōþs, frijonds, weitwōds, nasjands (Fulk
  pp. 165–166; Bammesberger pp. 192, 196, 215). Spanish ganso continues Goth.
  `*gansus`, a u-stem remodeling (Orel p. 126), so Gothic 'goose' does not
  witness the bare consonant-stem nominative.
- **Old Norse**: split. Feminine consonant-final root nouns **endingless**:
  bók, gás, lús, mús, nótt/nátt, geit, mjǫlk (Bammesberger p. 192 n. 306; Fulk
  p. 166). Vowel-final feminines **retain -r** < `*-z` with R-umlaut: kýr,
  sýr, ær (Fulk p. 167). Masculines carry -r: fótr (analogical after `*-z` >
  -R per Bammesberger p. 192), mánaðr, and nd-stem frændr. So the NGmc loss is
  conditioned by a **preceding consonant** (and gender-correlated analogy),
  not general.
- **West Germanic**: endingless across the board and across all genders and
  root types: OE bōc, gōs, lūs, mūs, niht, burg, meolc, mōnaþ, frēond; OS/OHG
  naht, OHG gans, miluh, mānōd, friunt (R&T p. 118: "no ending in PWGmc, as
  none of the daughters exhibits any"). Root-noun dat. pl. survives (OHG
  nahtum, buohhum, R&T p. 118), so the class as such persisted into WGmc; only
  the nominative marker is gone.
- **Runic**: no early-runic consonant-stem nominative in `*-z` is cited by any
  of the sources consulted; the early-runic evidence bearing on final `*-z`
  (e.g. the Frienstedt comb, `kaba`, c. 250–300 CE) concerns vowel + `*-z`
  (a-stem `*-az`) and is treated in Dossier B. Absence of a runic `*bōkz`-type
  form is consistent with early (pre-attestation) removal in the
  consonant-final class but does not independently date it.

## 7. Chronology

1. **Terminus ante quem for WGmc**: PWGmc — "no ending in PWGmc, as none of
   the daughters exhibits any" (R&T p. 118). The consonant-stem nominative
   `*-z`/`*-s` is already gone at the stage where Dossier B's unstressed-`*-z`
   loss and (much later) Dossier C's monosyllable loss operate. Crist's
   silence (§3.8) corroborates: his WGmc z-loss conspiracy never needs to
   handle `*-Cz` nominatives.
2. **Shared with NGmc under consonant-final conditioning**: ON bók/gás/lús/
   nótt endingless while kýr/sýr/ær keep -r. Either (a) a common NWGmc (or
   late-PGmc) loss/simplification in the environment consonant + `*-z(#)`, or
   (b. parallel but independent morphological generalization of the endingless
   nominative in each branch. The sources do not force a choice; Fulk's
   Szemerényi-law + analogy account (p. 143) makes the endingless type
   inherited variation already in PGmc, which both branches then generalized
   in consonant-final stems.
3. **Relative to rhotacism (SC003)**: in NGmc the surviving vowel-final
   nominatives went through `*-z` > -R > -r (kýr etc.), so the
   consonant-final loss must precede or bleed rhotacism there; in WGmc,
   word-final `*z` never rhotacizes at all (rhotacism is non-final in the
   CAPR cascade, matching R&T), so no ordering paradox arises: the A-loss
   simply happens early, before the PWGmc stage.
4. **Relative to Dossier B (unstressed `*-z` loss, PWGmc)**: A precedes B or
   is at latest simultaneous-and-independent. A's environment (stressed
   monosyllable, post-consonantal) is disjoint from B's (unstressed final
   syllable), and A is shared (in conditioned form) with NGmc while B is
   pan-WGmc only (R&T pp. 44–5: NWGmc `*-z` otherwise survives to become ON
   -r). A and B cannot be one change.
5. **Relative to Dossier C**: C is post-PWGmc, northern, and applies to
   stressed monosyllables in **vowel** + `*-z` (`*maiz`, `*hwaz`…), the exact
   class A does not touch. Centuries apart.

## 8. Analysis: morphological, phonological, or analogical?

The three candidate framings the programme requires be weighed:

- **Phonological cluster simplification** (Szemerényi-style): supported by
  Fulk p. 143 (PIE-level `*-s` loss after sonorant-final stems with
  compensatory lengthening) and by degemination for s-final roots (Ringe
  p. 149 `*mūs`). But this alone cannot cover `*bōk-`/`*naht-` (voiceless
  stop + s is a stable cluster — Gothic keeps it: nahts, baúrgs), and
  Bammesberger p. 192 explicitly denies that a phonological deletion of
  `*-z` after voiceless obstruents can be shown ("läßt sich kaum
  feststellen").
- **Morphological generalization of the endingless nominative**: supported by
  Ringe 2017 p. 306 (∅ as a genuine PGmc nominative alternant), R&T pp. 28,
  118 (endingless as one of three reconstructible options; endingless in
  PWGmc), Fulk p. 143 (analogical extension of endinglessness), Bammesberger
  pp. 190–192 (nominative repeatedly rebuilt: restitution, u-stem transfer,
  `*-z`-marking of voiced-final stems — i.e. the ending's presence was
  morphologically negotiated, not phonetically eroded).
- **Analogically conditioned restructuring**: the ON gender split (fótr,
  mánaðr with -r vs. bók, nótt, mjǫlk without; kýr/sýr/ær retaining inherited
  -r) and the Gothic -s (partly restituted per Bammesberger p. 192 n. 305,
  partly u-stem remodeling: fotus, `*gansus`) show each branch redistributing
  the marker analogically.

**Verdict: a combination, with the morphological component primary.** The
best-supported statement of the development is:

> In the consonant stems (root nouns and the older consonant-stem
> formations), the animate nominative singular marker `*-s/-z` — whose
> post-consonantal phonetic realization was already variable/moribund in PGmc
> (Szemerényi's law residue; degemination after s-final roots; "fōts? (fōs?)"
> indeterminacy) — was **eliminated as a morphological ending in the
> consonant-final environment**, the endingless nominative being generalized
> throughout the class. In North Germanic the elimination is conditioned
> (consonant-final stems only, with gender-correlated analogical
> redistribution); in West Germanic it is complete before PWGmc. Gothic
> instead generalized the overt marker (-s).

This is **not** a purely phonological `Cz# → C#` sound law: the sources locate
the action in the morphology of one inflectional slot of one stem class, and
the daughter branches resolve it differently (Gothic: keep marker; NGmc:
conditioned; WGmc: eliminate). It is also not "loss of a segment that was
always fictitious": for voiced-final stems (`*burgz`, `*frijōndz`) and
vowel-final stems (`*kōz` > kýr) the overt `*-z` is positively reconstructible.

## 9. Proposed historical rule (for Phase 2 adjudication; no Foma syntax)

**Rule A (working name: consonant-stem nominative `*-z` loss / endingless
nominative generalization).**

- **Historical content**: elimination of the nom. sg. marker `*-z` after a
  stem-final consonant in the athematic consonant stems.
- **Stage**: completed by (early) PWGmc for West Germanic (R&T p. 118);
  shared in conditioned form with NGmc, so plausibly initiated in late PGmc /
  NWGmc as morphological variation (Ringe p. 306 ∅-alternant).
- **Environment (CAPR-implementable)**: word-final `*z` deleted immediately
  after a consonant. In CAPR's corpus this environment is **exactly
  coextensive with the morphological class** (all seven `-Cz` PROTOFORMs are
  consonant-stem nominatives — Phase 0 census), so the phonological
  environment expresses the morphological generalization without lexical
  listing and without overreach. No representation of stress or syllable
  count is required for the corpus as it stands; whether the rule should be
  *stated* with a monosyllabicity condition depends on the friend/milk/month
  allocation (below).
- **Ordering**: before Dossier B's rule (and therefore well before rhotacism
  interactions and all later OE developments). Within CAPR, any position
  before SC020's current slot that precedes no rule sensitive to final `-Cz`
  is historically safe; exact slot is Phase 3 work.
- **Intended positive witnesses (corpus)**: book `*bōkz`, flea `*fláuxz`,
  goose `*gánsz`, louse `*lūsz` (+ friend/milk/month if allocated to A).
- **Comparative controls (non-corpus)**: positive — `*mūs`, `*naht-`,
  `*burg-` (endingless WGmc reflexes mūs, niht, burg); negative — ON kýr,
  sýr, ær (vowel-final: `*-z` survives to rhotacism), Go. baúrgs/nahts
  (marker generalized, no loss), ON fótr/mánaðr (analogical -r).
- **Negative controls (corpus)**: `*bárdaz` and the ~107 unstressed `-Vz`
  forms (must be left to B); medial `z` (deer `*déuzan`, hoard, learn, meed,
  berry) must still rhotacize; no corpus form ends in stressed vowel + `z`
  (C's domain is unwitnessed).

**Open allocation question for Phase 2 (author decision if unresolved):**
friend `*fríjōndz`, milk `*mélukz`, month `*mḗnōθz` are consonant-stem
nominatives (A's morphological class) **and** unstressed final syllables (B's
phonological environment). Evidence cuts both ways: R&T pp. 44–5 put the
structurally parallel `*fadurz` > `*fadur` under the general unstressed loss
(→ B); Bammesberger pp. 186–187 calls the loss in `*mēnōz` > `*mēnō` "nicht
lautgesetzlich" (→ morphological, i.e. A-like); ON mánaðr keeps -r (the
polysyllabic loss is **not** shared with NGmc, unlike A's monosyllabic core)
while ON mjǫlk is endingless (shared). Computationally the choice moves three
firings between A and B (A=7/B=107 vs. A=4/B=110) and decides whether A needs
a monosyllabicity condition; the OE outputs are identical either way.

## 11. Conclusion

The final `*-z` of `*bōkz`, `*fláuxz`, `*gánsz`, `*lūsz` (and of
`*fríjōndz`, `*mélukz`, `*mḗnōθz`, allocation pending) is the athematic
nominative singular marker in morphologically explicit notation. Its removal
is a real, early, historically distinct development — the generalization of
the endingless nominative in consonant-final athematic stems, complete in West
Germanic before PWGmc and shared in conditioned form with North Germanic — and
is **not** an instance of either the PWGmc unstressed final-`*-z` loss
(Dossier B) or the later northern stressed-monosyllable loss (Dossier C).
Best generalization: **a combination — phonologically seeded (Szemerényi's
law, degemination), morphologically enacted (endingless nominative
generalized), analogically redistributed in the daughters** — implementable in
CAPR as post-consonantal word-final `*z` deletion at an early cascade stage,
that environment being exactly coextensive with the morphological class in
the corpus.
