# Dossier B — PWGmc loss of word-final `*-z` in unstressed syllables (2026)

Branch `sc001-sc020-chronology-audit`. Phase 1 of the three-rule *-z programme.
Research/documentation only: no production FST, protoform, TSV, metadata,
rule-ID, baseline, or binary change. This dossier proposes a historical rule but
no Foma syntax (Phase 3, after adjudication).

Treated independently of Dossier A (consonant-stem nominative `*-z`) and
Dossier C (later stressed-monosyllable `*-Vz` loss). CAPR's current SC020
firing inventory is used only as CAPR evidence about the corpus, not as proof
of the historical rule.

## 1. Research question

What is the exact environment, stage, geographic scope, and chronology of the
general Germanic loss of word-final `*-z` — the development that currently
accounts for the bulk of SC020's 114 firings (e.g. beard `*bárdaz` → `*bárda`)
— and how does it relate to Verner's law, rhotacism, final-vowel loss, the
`*-ō` → `*-u` raising, the `*-ō` vs. `*-ōz` contrast, and the `*-az` → `*-a`
→ ∅ chain?

## 2. The core formulation in the sources

### 2.1 Ringe & Taylor (2014), pp. 44–45, §3.1.1

The central modern statement:

- "The r-stem gen. sg. ending `*-urz` (vol. i 4.3.4 (i); p. 276) **lost its
  *-z in all its surviving reflexes**; e.g. `*fadurz` 'father's' > ON fǫður,
  Angl. OE fadur (CædH), feadur (Ps(A)), fador (Li). We have no way of knowing
  whether *-z was simply lost or the cluster first underwent assimilation,
  i.e. *-rz > *-rr > -r (see 3.3.1)." (p. 44; footnote: `*-uraz` "is also
  conceivable (Bammesberger 1990: 207)".)
- "Though much of the evidence has been eliminated by syncretism and other
  morphological changes, it is clear that **the loss of *-z in unstressed
  syllables was a pan-WGmc sound change**." (p. 44)
- Dating: "It might be attested in Tacitus, i.e. late in the 1st century AD
  (Grønvik 1998: 96 with references), though judgments from Latinized endings
  can never be completely secure." Early loss "makes it easier to understand
  the remodelling of the weak past endings (see section 3.2.1), which in the
  southern dialects must have preceded the unrounding of bimoric *ō — another
  pan-WGmc sound change. On the other hand, it seems to have followed the
  northern WGmc remodelling of u-stem inflection (see 3.1.4). **On balance it
  seems most reasonable to ascribe the loss of word-final *z in unstressed
  syllables to PWGmc.**" (p. 44)
- Two changes, not one: "this decision entails separating the loss of *-z in
  unstressed syllables from the loss of *-z in stressed syllables — i.e.
  monosyllables — which was not uniform throughout WGmc and was clearly a late
  change, probably post-PWGmc (see section 3.3.1). The relative chronology
  suggests that those were **two historically separate sound changes (so Crist
  2001: 107–8)**." (pp. 44–45) They weigh the alternative of "a single change
  whose period of variable implementation lasted for many generations" and
  judge it "less likely because loss of *-z does not exhibit the kind of
  morphological interference that is typical of long-drawn-out sound changes"
  (p. 45) — while conceding that if it nevertheless happened, "the real
  chronology of changes can have been more complex" (p. 45).

### 2.2 Crist (2002), LSA handout, §5

"Throughout WGmc, PGmc *z deletes word-finally in unstressed syllables; this
is already understood (e.g. Campbell p. 166). I assume here that this pan-WGmc
rule **predates and is separate from** the Ingvaeonic rule of *z-deletion
proposed here. The earlier WGmc sound change applies across the board, and has
major consequences for WGmc morphology, since *-z was very common among PGmc
inflectional suffixes. After this Proto-West-Germanic sound change, *z exists
only word-medially in syllable onsets, and in the codas of stressed syllables"
(handout p. 2, §5). Crist's appendix likewise excludes unstressed-syllable
`*z` from his study "since these were uniformly deleted throughout WGmc"
(handout p. 4). R&T's page citation for the two-change position is to the
dissertation version, Crist 2001: 107–8 (p. 45).

### 2.3 Fulk (2018)

Runic anchor: "The loss of final *-z in kaba (for kamba) on the **Frienstedt
comb (ca. 250–300)** is a specifically WGmc. development (see Schmidt, Nedoma,
& Düwel 2011), but there is no need to assume that the change had affected all
of WGmc. by the date of its deposit. Compare the mother-goddess names Vatvims,
Aflims, and Gabims in Ubian inscriptions (dat. pl., 1st–3rd centuries; Much
1887, and cf. Ringe & Taylor 2014: 46)." (p. 25 n. 1, §1.14)

### 2.4 Older statements

Campbell (1959: 166), quoted by Crist (handout p. 3, §7): "z is lost in West
Gmc. finally in unaccented syllables, e.g. many n.s. and n.p. inflexions" —
the traditional account, which then lumps the pronominal monosyllables in as
"unaccented" (see Dossier C for why that is contested).

**Consensus**: every consulted source treats the unstressed-final loss as (i)
regular, (ii) pan-WGmc, (iii) early, and (iv) distinct from whatever happened
in stressed monosyllables.

## 3. Exact environment

Word-final `*z` in an **unstressed syllable**, regardless of the preceding
segment:

- After short vowels: `*-az` (a-stem nom. sg. `*þewaz` > PWGmc `*þeu`, Early
  Runic þewaz; `*kuningaz` > `*kuning`; R&T p. 45), `*-iz`, `*-uz`, `*-ez`.
- After long vowels: `*-ōz` (see §6 below).
- **After consonants**: r-stem gen. sg. `*fadurz` > `*fadur` (R&T p. 44) and
  dat. pl. `*-mz` (< `*-maz/-miz` after vowel reduction; cf. the retained -ms
  of the Ubian Aflims/Vatvims type that R&T pp. 45–46 and Fulk p. 25 n. 1
  discuss and set aside). The rule is **not** restricted to post-vocalic
  position.
- The rule targets `*z` only, never `*s`: PGmc gen. sg. `*dagas` keeps its
  sibilant into PWGmc `*dagas` > OE dæges while nom. sg. `*dagaz` > PWGmc
  `*dag` (R&T p. 212 derivation; the ordering argument at pp. 45–46 turns
  precisely on "*a was lost before word-final *-z but not before word-final
  *-s"). The change is sensitive to the Verner-law voicing contrast.

What "unstressed syllable" excludes: stressed monosyllables (`*maiz`, `*hiz`
type — Dossier C; R&T p. 44 explicitly separate them) and the stressed
consonant-final nominatives (Dossier A — which on R&T's view p. 118 were
already endingless in PWGmc, so never reached this rule with an ending).

## 4. Stage and geographic scope

- **Stage: PWGmc** ("most reasonable to ascribe … to PWGmc", R&T p. 44),
  possibly beginning as early as the late 1st century AD (Tacitus per Grønvik
  1998: 96, with R&T's caution), materially attested by c. 250–300 (Frienstedt
  comb kaba, Fulk p. 25 n. 1).
- **Scope: pan-West-Germanic** — all daughters (OE, OF, OS, OHG, and per the
  comb, continental WGmc generally). Not shared with North Germanic: NGmc
  retains reflexes of final `*-z` as -R/-r for centuries (Early Runic -z/-R;
  "reflexes of *-z were lost only centuries later (and many still survive in
  modern Icelandic)", R&T p. 45; ON dagr < `*dagaz`). Not shared with Gothic
  (dags, with devoicing).
- This branch distribution is what makes B a **West Germanic** innovation and
  a genuine isogloss separating WGmc from NGmc — unlike A (shared with NGmc in
  conditioned form) and unlike C (northern WGmc only).

## 5. Relationship to Verner alternations and final `*s ~ *z`

Final `*-z` in nominal/pronominal endings is the Verner-voiced reflex of PIE
`*-s`, generalized in PGmc nominal inflection (Ringe 2017: 306 on the leveling
"of Verner's Law alternants in favor of *-z in nominal endings"; Crist handout
p. 2 §4 on the resulting distribution of `*z`, after Campbell 1959: 163–4).
Because the loss targets only the voiced member, the PGmc `*-s`/`*-z`
contrast surfaces in WGmc as retention vs. loss: `*dagas` (gen.) vs. `*dagaz`
(nom.) > PWGmc `*dagas` vs. `*dag` (R&T p. 212). Where OE/OS show -s in the
a-stem nom. pl. (OE -as, OS -os), R&T treat the sibilant as **not** the
regular reflex of `*-ōz` (see §6); Campbell 1962: 223 and Brunner, by
contrast, took it as a voiceless Verner alternant `*-ōs` (R&T pp. 115–116
report and dispute this). CAPR's corpus, being nominative-singular based, is
not affected by this dispute, but the dossier records it as the principal
locus of scholarly disagreement about B's interaction with Verner doublets.

## 6. Relationship to `*-ō` → `*-u` raising and the `*-ō` vs. `*-ōz` contrast

PNWGmc (i.e. **before** B) raised *word-final* bimoric non-nasalized `*-ō` to
`*-ū`, then shortened final long high vowels: `*landō` > `*landu`, `*dagō`
(inst.) > `*dagu`, `*kʷemō` > `*kʷemu` (R&T pp. 15–16, §2.1.1; summarized as
the two-change validation of the NWGmc clade, p. 24, §2.3). Because the
raising was word-final only, `*-ōz` sequences were **sheltered**: the `*z`
still closed the word. When B later deleted that `*z` (PWGmc), the newly final
`*-ō` did *not* undergo the (completed) raising — hence the a-stem nom. pl.:
PGmc `*-ōz` > PWGmc `*-ō` on R&T's reconstruction (pp. 115–116: "I suggest
that the original ending survives in the OF variant -a < PWGmc *-ō < PGmc
*-ōz (van Helten 1890: 127–8); I accordingly reconstruct *-ō for PWGmc,
though with considerable uncertainty"), with OE -as / OS -os as later
innovations of debated origin (Verner `*-ōs` per Campbell/Brunner; `*-ōziz`
per Bammesberger 1990: 39, 43–4; an innovation per R&T pp. 115–116).

This counterfeeding order (raising before z-loss) is exactly the ordering
constraint already implemented in CAPR: SC019 `PNWGmcFinalLongORaising`
(manifest position 15) shelters `*-ōz` and precedes SC020 (position 16). The
corpus witness is rest `*rástōz` (firing-audit row 75): `*-ōz` survives SC019,
then loses `*z` by SC020.

## 7. Relationship to rhotacism

- WGmc rhotacism is **non-final**: "Though nonfinal *z became r in every
  NWGmc language … it can be proved that this 'rhotacism' occurred
  independently in Norse and in WGmc, and even in the latter group its
  application was not uniform" (R&T p. 37, §2.3.1).
- B precedes rhotacism and bleeds it: after B (and Dossier A's development),
  "*z exists only word-medially in syllable onsets, and in the codas of
  stressed syllables" (Crist handout p. 2, §5); only that residue rhotacizes
  (or, in the north, deletes — Dossier C).
- The deletion is sensitive to the `*r`/`*z` contrast (original `*r` never
  deletes: `*hēr` > OE hēr; Crist handout p. 2, §6), so B must precede the
  `*z` > r merger — deletion-after-merger would wrongly delete old `*-r`.
- In NGmc the mirror-image order holds (no B; final `*-z` > -R > -r), which
  is why ON dagr contrasts with OE dæg.

CAPR's cascade encodes this compatibly: `EAFRhotacism` (SC003) is restricted
to non-final position (right context `?`), so SC020's ordering after it in the
executable chain is harmless — final `*z` cannot rhotacize in the FST at all
(Phase 0 audit §2). Historically B < rhotacism; computationally the two rules
are disjoint in environment.

## 8. Relationship to final-vowel loss (`*-az` → `*-a` → ∅ chain)

R&T pp. 45–46 (§3.1.2) address the ordering of B against the pan-WGmc loss of
word-final unstressed `*a` and `*ą`:

- "The most economical ordering of the sound changes is 1) **loss of *-z after
  all unstressed vowels**, followed by 2) **loss of word-final *-a and *-ą**."
  (p. 46)
- The reverse order (as in ON, where the low vowels went first, before AD 700,
  Noreen; and short vowels dropped before all obstruents) would require
  explaining "why *a was lost before word-final *-z but not before word-final
  *-s" (dagas!) — "Ordering the loss of *-z first is less complex and
  therefore preferable." (p. 46)
- The Ubian dat. pl. forms Aflims, Vatvims, Saithamims (2nd–3rd c. AD, lower
  Rhine) are the "only potential counterexamples" and are set aside: the
  engravers' language is uncertain, and the vowels lost there "were in the
  third and fourth syllables" (p. 46).

So the chain for beard-type forms is `*bárdaz` → (B) `*bárda` → (apocope)
`*bárd` — two ordered PWGmc changes, exactly as CAPR implements them (SC020,
then the bare-a loss; Phase 0 audit §3 ordering constraints). Upon the vowel
loss, post-consonantal `*j`/`*w` vocalized (`*harjaz` > `*hari`, `*gelwaz` >
`*gelu`, R&T p. 46) — a further dependency inherited by later rules.

## 10. CAPR's firing inventory under B

From the committed firing audit (`sc020-final-z-firing-audit.tsv`, 114 rows)
and the Phase 0 census: removing Dossier A's stressed monosyllabic
consonant-final class (book, flea, goose, louse = 4 rows) leaves **110**
candidate B firings, of which 3 (friend `*fríjōndz`, milk `*mélukz`, month

non-final `*z`); no corpus form ends in stressed `*-Vz` (C unwitnessed).

## 11. Proposed historical rule (for Phase 2 adjudication; no Foma syntax)

**Rule B (working name: PWGmc unstressed final `*-z` loss).**

- **Historical content**: regular deletion of word-final `*z` when the final
  syllable is unstressed; the voiced sibilant only (`*-s` untouched);
  preceding segment irrelevant (vowels of any quality/length, and consonants
  of the `*fadurz`/`*-mz` type).
- **Stage**: PWGmc (R&T p. 44); onset possibly late 1st c. AD, attested by
  c. 250–300.
- **Scope**: pan-WGmc; not NGmc, not EGmc.
- **Ordering**: after PNWGmc final `*-ō` raising (counterfeeding; `*-ōz`
  sheltered — SC019 < SC020 as implemented); before the loss of word-final
  unstressed `*a`/`*ą` (R&T's economical ordering, p. 46); before rhotacism
  (bleeding; and deletion is `*r`/`*z`-sensitive, Crist §6); after the
  northern u-stem remodelling and before the southern weak-past remodelling +
  bimoric `*ō` unrounding (R&T p. 44) — the latter two are morphological
  anchors outside CAPR's rule set.
- **Identity in CAPR**: this is what SC020 (`EAFFinalZDeletion`) has actually
  been doing for ~94% of its firings. Per the programme's default
  architectural expectation, SC020 should be **retained** for exactly this
  development, with its environment narrowed to the unstressed condition
  (equivalently, in the corpus: not immediately after the A-class removal has
  applied, and not in stressed monosyllables) once A and C exist as separate
  rules.
- **Intended positive witnesses**: the 107 uncontested firings (± the 3
  contested).
- **Intended negative controls**: book/flea/goose/louse (A), stressed `*-Vz`
  monosyllables (C; none in corpus), medial `*z` (rhotacism), any `*-s`-final
  form.

## 13. Conclusion

Dossier B's development is the best-attested and least controversial of the
three: a regular, pan-West-Germanic, PWGmc-era deletion of word-final `*z` in
unstressed syllables, ordered after the PNWGmc final-`*-ō` raising, before the
word-final low-vowel apocope, and before (indeed bleeding) rhotacism. It is
distinct from Dossier A's consonant-stem nominative development (different
stage, different conditioning, different branch distribution) and from Dossier
C's late northern monosyllable loss (R&T pp. 44–45 explicitly; Crist §5
explicitly). In CAPR it corresponds to the retained identity of SC020, with
107 uncontested corpus witnesses and 3 further firings (friend, milk, month)
pending the Phase 2 allocation decision between A and B.
