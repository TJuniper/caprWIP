## Historical problem

`SC069-SC078` is currently a visibility scaffold rather than a historically
coherent nine-change chapter. It sits after the promoted `SC066-SC068`
late weak-tail syncope corridor and before the still-scaffolded
`SC079-SC087` closing cluster, so the book needs an explicit placeholder here.
But the grouped row mixes at least six different evidence profiles:

1. a broad/far opener (`SC069`);
2. an early local fronting/shortening seam (`SC070-SC071`);
3. a stronger shortening/merger core (`SC072-SC073`);
4. a narrow medial-`i` lowering bridge (`SC074-SC075`);
5. a negative but source-backed prefix note (`SC076`);
6. a right-edge weak-tail reduction hinge (`SC078`).

The central dossier question is therefore not whether the region exists, but how
it should later be split into smaller production-ready units without forcing one
uneven placeholder into a false chapter center.

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959
- source_key: Campbell1959
- locator: §§345--354; §§388--389
- witness_used: `docs/references/campbell_old_english_grammar.txt`
- locator_confidence: section_safe
- terminology: loss of unaccented vowels; vowels of prefixes; early OE shortening of unaccented long vowels; loss of medial vowels after short syllables
- short quotation:

  > "Medial unaccented vowels are very freely dropped after short syllables ..."

- paraphrase: Campbell gives strong support for the broad late unstressed-vowel region but not for the present nine-member grouping as such. His sections on early OE loss of unaccented vowels and early shortening of unaccented long vowels support the general terrain behind `SC069-SC075` and `SC078`, while the explicit "Vowels of prefixes" section gives direct historical footing for `SC076` as a real late prefix-vowel phenomenon [@Campbell1959, §§345--354, 388--389].
- conditioning: final-vowel loss, shortening of unaccented long vowels, prefix-vowel weakening, and later medial-vowel loss all belong to one late weak-tail neighborhood
- chronology: Campbell supports a staged late reduction zone after the earlier umlaut and back-mutation material, but he does not flatten that zone into one chapter-sized `SC069-SC078` unit
- examples: `weorod`; `heafodu`; `betra`; `micle`; prefix vowels in unstressed formations
- cautions: Campbell is best evidence for the broad neighborhood and for `SC076`'s historical legitimacy, not for treating SC069-SC078 as one ready-made report

### Hogg 1992
- source_key: HoggPhonology1992
- locator: pp. 121--122
- witness_used: `docs/references/hogg_vol1.txt`
- locator_confidence: page_safe
- terminology: apocope; syncope; reduction of unstressed vowels; simplification in consonant groups
- short quotation:

  > "Apocope affected the high vowels / i / and / u / ..."
  >
  > "The high vowels were also subject to syncope in medial positions after a heavy syllable ..."

- paraphrase: Hogg is especially useful for the internal weighting of the row. He treats apocope and syncope as neighboring but distinct late processes, shows how `weorod`, `heafodu`, and `heafdu` reflect their overlap, and then immediately notes that syncope-created consonant clusters can trigger later simplification [@HoggPhonology1992, p. 121]. That supports `SC072-SC075` and `SC078` as genuine late weak-tail material, while also warning against narrating every CAPR step as a separate textbook law.
- conditioning: heavy-syllable and trisyllabic apocope, medial syncope after heavy syllables, and downstream consonant-cluster simplification all belong to the same late zone
- chronology: later than i-mutation; apocope and syncope are partly overlapping rather than a flat one-rule ladder
- examples: `fet`; `word`; `scipu`; `weorod`; `heafodu`; `heafdu`
- cautions: Hogg does not isolate SC069, SC070, SC071, or SC076 as standalone handbook headings; his support is strongest for the broader shortening/syncope/reduction environment

## CAPR formulation versus literature

The literature and the cards do **not** support the current grouped row as one
future production chapter. They support a late region with several internal
seams of different strengths:

1. `SC070-SC071` is a real local early seam.
2. `SC072-SC073` is the strongest middle core.
3. `SC074-SC075` is a narrower local bridge.
4. `SC076` is source-backed but chronology-negative.
5. `SC078` is a historically real right-edge hinge with a forward tie into the
   closing region.

That profile is enough for a later structural split. It is not enough for honest
grouped promotion of `SC069-SC078` unchanged.

## Chronology implications

The chronology cards and the source tradition line up on six practical
conclusions.

1. `SC069` is a broad/far one-sided opener, not a local core.
2. `SC070-SC071` forms the clearest early local pair, but only SC070 has a
   positive boundary on both sides.
3. `SC072-SC073` is the strongest internal production-scale seam in the row.
4. `SC074-SC075` forms a real but narrow local bridge that is probably too small
   and witness-limited to lead the next promotion.
5. `SC076` should not be turned into a positive chronology anchor; its value is
   historical visibility, not tested local chapter structure.
6. `SC078` is the clearest singleton or right-edge bridge candidate, but its
   later `SC086` relation means the closing cluster on the right matters to its
   eventual framing.

## Proposed internal split map

The most plausible later split is not a simple `SC069-SC072` / `SC073-SC076` /
`SC078` division. A more faithful map is:

1. `SC069` as a standalone scaffold opener/context note.
2. `SC070-SC071` as a short early fronting/shortening bridge.
3. `SC072-SC073` as the strongest late-tail shortening/merger core.
4. `SC074-SC075` as a narrow medial-unstressed-`i` lowering bridge.
5. `SC076` as a singleton prefix-reduction note.
6. `SC078` as a singleton weak-tail reduction note or right-edge bridge.

If the project wants fewer units, the most defensible compression is to keep
`SC074-SC075` and `SC076` scaffolded while promoting only `SC072-SC073` or
later `SC078`. What the dossier does **not** support is promoting the present
nine-member row unchanged.
