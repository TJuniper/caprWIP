## Historical problem

`SC028-SC030` is the compact entry seam between the already promoted nasal
spirant corridor and the already promoted West Saxon diphthong chain. The book
needs this row because otherwise the assembled half would jump directly from the
nasal-loss corridor into the fuller diphthongal region without explaining the
intermediate glide/fronting setup.

The internal hierarchy is now much clearer than it was in the bare scaffold.
SC028 is historically legible as a type of `x`-loss, but its chronology card is
negative on both sides: the earlier search stops at bundled `PWGmcChanges`, and
the later search reaches the current SC087 search boundary without a real break.
SC029 and SC030 are stronger. Their reciprocal `hay` / `strew` evidence gives
the row one clear local core, and SC030 then hands directly forward into SC032
through a broader no-output failure set. The dossier question is therefore no
longer whether the row has enough historical material to discuss at all, but
whether the future prose should keep one cautious grouped entry with SC028 as a
brief preface, or split later into an SC028 note plus a tighter SC029-SC030
core.

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959

- **source_key:** `Campbell1959`
- **locator:** §120; §461
- **terminology:** loss of `x`; `auj` developments; `hég / hig`; `ieg`; `strégan`
- **short quotation:** "`Further examples of loss of x are fléam ... héla`"; "`auj > auu > au ... au- > éa- ... hég, hig ... ieg ... strégan`"
- **paraphrase:** Campbell gives the clearest compact handbook basis for the row's asymmetry. In §461 he explicitly lists forms such as `fléam` and `héla` as examples of loss of `x`, which makes SC028 historically legible even if the chronology card presently remains negative and the CAPR conditioning is more explicit than the handbook shorthand [@Campbell1959, §461]. In §120 he then treats the `awj` material directly: `auj` develops through a geminated West Germanic stage and then fronts in Old English, yielding forms such as `hég / hig`, `ieg`, and `strégan` [@Campbell1959, §120]. That gives SC029 and SC030 a real historical basis as part of one glide/fronting region rather than as merely model-internal rules.
- **conditioning:** loss of `x` in historically reduced environments; `awj` sequences and their later Old English fronted reflexes
- **chronology:** Campbell supports the entire row as a real historical neighborhood, but not as three equally strong steps. SC028 looks like backgrounded left-edge context, while SC029-SC030 looks like the real handbook center.
- **examples:** `fléam`; `héla`; `hég`; `hig`; `ieg`; `strégan`
- **cautions:** Campbell does not present one finished `SC028-SC030` chapter. The `x`-loss material is especially broad and should not be read as direct proof of the exact CAPR ordering or conditioning.

### Ringe and Taylor 2014

- **source_key:** `RingeTaylor2014`
- **locator:** §3.1.3; §6.1.2
- **terminology:** `*awj`; reversal of gemination into `auj`; OE fronting outcomes; `néah`; `hēlan`
- **short quotation:** "`when the preceding vowel was *a the usual OE outcome seems to reflect not *aw'w but *auj`"; "`*hawja ... > WS OE hieg`"; "`*strawjaną ... > Angl. OE strégan`"
- **paraphrase:** Ringe and Taylor provide the best comparative derivation for the SC029-SC030 core. In their early West Germanic discussion they argue that sequences from PGmc `*awj` were effectively restored as `auj`, which then developed normally in Old English; the examples include `*hawja > hieg` and `*strawjaną > strégan` [@RingeTaylor2014, §3.1.3; @RingeTaylor2014, §6.1.2]. Those are exactly the witnesses that define the reciprocal SC029/SC030 chronology cards. Elsewhere they also derive forms such as `néah` and `hēlan` through older `h/x`-bearing material, which is enough to keep SC028 historically intelligible even though it still lacks any positive first-break boundary in the current searchable corridor [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.2.1].
- **conditioning:** inherited `*awj` sequences; later Old English fronting of `au`; older `h/x` material surviving into Old English reflexes
- **chronology:** This source strongly supports the local SC029-SC030 core. It does not, however, turn SC028 into a comparably strong chronology anchor.
- **examples:** `hieg`; `strégan`; `néah`; `hēlan`
- **cautions:** Ringe and Taylor support the historical type and derivations, not the final book architecture. Their evidence fits a grouped row with internal hierarchy just as easily as a later split.

### Luick 1914

- **source_key:** `Luick1914`
- **locator:** §116
- **terminology:** normal Old English development of `ww` / `awj`; `deaw`; `hēawan`; `scēawian`
- **short quotation:** "`Die altenglischen Formen haben die normale Entwicklung durchlaufen ... deaw, ... hēawan, scēawian`"
- **paraphrase:** Luick is most useful as a reinforcing witness that the `awj` material belongs to the ordinary Old English diphthongal history rather than to a narrow lexical accident. His discussion of the normal Old English development of these sequences includes the same `aw`/`ēaw` region seen in Campbell and supports the idea that the `hay` / `strew` pair belongs to a genuine early entry zone before the fuller West Saxon diphthong chain [@Luick1914, §116].
- **conditioning:** inherited `ww` / `awj` sequences and their regular Old English outcomes
- **chronology:** Luick reinforces the SC029-SC030 core but adds little pressure to keep SC028 attached as a coequal chapter member.
- **examples:** `deaw`; `hēawan`; `scēawian`
- **cautions:** Luick is strongest on the broader diphthongal region, not on the narrow question of whether SC028 should remain inside the same eventual production unit.

## CAPR formulation versus literature

CAPR sharpens one real historical neighborhood into three adjacent rules.

SC028 `NWGmcPreconsonantalXLoss` is the weak left preface. Campbell's explicit
`x`-loss examples and Ringe and Taylor's retained `h/x` derivations make the
rule historically legible as a type [@Campbell1959, §461; @RingeTaylor2014,
§6.2.1]. But the chronology card is negative on both sides, so the rule is
currently better understood as backgrounded entry context than as a strong local
chapter center.

SC029 `OEAwwjResolution` and SC030 `OEAuBrightening` are different. Campbell
and Ringe and Taylor both treat the `awj` material directly, and the same
`hay` / `strew` derivations that appear in the handbook tradition also define
the reciprocal chronology boundary [@Campbell1959, §120; @RingeTaylor2014,
§3.1.3; @RingeTaylor2014, §6.1.2]. SC029 is still one-sided because its earlier
search remains runner-bounded, but its later SC030 boundary is real and local.
SC030 is stronger again because it both reciprocates SC029 locally and points
forward to SC032 through a larger no-output handoff into the next promoted
region.

The practical result is a grouped row with a clear internal hierarchy: SC028 as
the historically legible but chronology-negative preface, then SC029-SC030 as
the real glide/fronting core.

## Chapter-shape options

### Option 1: keep SC028-SC030 as one cautious adjacent entry bridge

This remains viable if the book wants one compact seam between the nasal
spirant corridor and the West Saxon diphthong chain.

- **Why it works:** the rules are adjacent, all three are ordinary FST changes, and together they narrate a real entry into the fronting and diphthongal zone.
- **How it should be narrated:** SC028 as a brief `x`-loss preface, SC029-SC030 as the explicit local core.
- **Main caution:** the prose would need to state openly that SC028's chronology card is negative on both sides.

### Option 2: split later into SC028 plus SC029-SC030

This is the strongest alternative and the one the current source pass modestly
favors.

- **Why it works:** both the source tradition and the chronology cards are sharper for SC029-SC030 than for the three-change row as a whole.
- **Possible shape:** a very short SC028 boundary/context note followed by a compact SC029-SC030 fronting report.
- **Why it is not mandatory yet:** the present task is dossier preparation only, and the grouped scaffold still serves the assembled half well pending human chapter-shape review.

### Option 3: require substantially more source work before any chapter-shape decision

This now looks like the weakest option.

- **Why it was plausible earlier:** SC028 seemed too thin and the grouped row looked under-described.
- **Why it is weaker now:** the main historical picture is already clear. The unresolved issue is chapter architecture, not absence of handbook support.
- **Main caution:** source expansion could still enrich SC028, but it is no longer the blocking issue.
