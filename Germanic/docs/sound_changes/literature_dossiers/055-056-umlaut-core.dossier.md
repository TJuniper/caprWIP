## Historical problem

`SC055` is the real historical center of this pair. Old English i-umlaut is a
major handbook sound change, and the chronology cards now place it in a strong
local network: it must follow `SC052` OE Velar Palatalization and must precede
`SC056` OE Ws Palatal Diphthongization.

`SC056` matters because it provides the reciprocal left boundary and because it
captures a real West Saxon palatal-diphthongization behavior on the right edge
of the umlaut zone. But it should not automatically be treated as coequal with
`SC055`. In the source tradition, i-umlaut is the chapter-defining process,
whereas West Saxon palatal diphthongization is narrower, more dialect-specific,
and often discussed under a different chronology from CAPR's local
`SC055 < SC056` result.

The dossier therefore asks two questions at once:

1. how strongly the handbooks support a paired SC055-SC056 chapter at all;
2. how carefully the eventual report must distinguish literary chronology from
   CAPR's model-local ordering constraints.

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959
- source_key: Campbell1959
- locator: §39; §§190--197; §§248--251
- terminology: i-umlaut; i-mutation; palatal influence; West Saxon `ie`
- short quotation: "The process known as i-umlaut or i-mutation operates on practically all the sounds which it could theoretically affect in OE."
- paraphrase: Campbell gives the most classical handbook statement of i-umlaut as a major Old English process, affecting nearly the whole vowel system under a following `i/j` trigger. He also treats West Saxon `ie` and the older debate over whether diphthongs produced by palatal influence stood before or after i-mutation.
- conditioning: following `i` or `j` fronts all back vowels and raises certain short front vowels; in West Saxon the diphthongs `ea`, `eo`, and `io` can mutate to `ie`; forms such as `giest`, `giefan`, and `hierde` belong to the West Saxon `ie` zone [@Campbell1959, §39; §§190--191].
- chronology: Campbell presents the traditional probability that palatal influence was earlier than i-umlaut, while also recording the later scholarly challenge to that chronology. That is useful here because it means the source tradition does not simply map onto CAPR's current local `SC055 < SC056` serialization [@Campbell1959, §§248--251].
- examples: `sǣ` 'sea'; `giest`; `giefan`; `hierde`; `ieldra` [@Campbell1959, §39; §§190--197; §§248--251].
- cautions: Campbell strongly supports i-umlaut as the historical core, but his treatment of palatal influence and West Saxon `ie` is broader and more debated than the neat SC055-SC056 pair suggested by the cards.

### Hogg 1992
- source_key: HoggPhonology1992
- locator: pp. 112--115
- terminology: i-mutation; i-umlaut; palatal diphthongisation
- short quotation: "we come now to a change which is almost as uncontroversial as it is important"
- paraphrase: Hogg presents i-mutation as one of the central Old English vowel changes, affecting back vowels, short front vowels, and diphthongs in a widespread and morphologically visible way. His earlier discussion of palatal diphthongization makes the West Saxon right edge real, but explicitly less stable and more controversial than i-mutation itself [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 106, 113--114].
- conditioning: i-mutation is triggered by following `i` or `j`, fronting back vowels and raising short front vowels and diphthongs; West Saxon diphthongization after palatal consonants affects front stressed vowels such as in `giefan` and `sceap` [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 106, 113--114].
- chronology: Hogg's organization is useful for this dossier because palatal diphthongization is treated before the main i-mutation discussion. That supports the claim that the handbook chronology and the CAPR order relation are not identical, even though the cards show a strong local dependency between SC055 and SC056.
- examples: `bryd`; `trymman`; `bedd`; `ciest`; `wiersa`; `hieran`; `ieldra`; `giefan`; `sceap` [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 106, 113--114].
- cautions: Hogg supports both sides of the pair, but the balance is uneven: i-mutation is chapter-central, while palatal diphthongization is narrower and partly controversial even within his own discussion.

### Ringe and Taylor 2014
- source_key: RingeTaylor2014
- locator: §§6.5.1; §§6.6.1--6.6.4
- terminology: diphthongization triggered by initial palatals in West Saxon; front mutation
- short quotation: "After initial velars and *sk had been palatalized, any following stressed non-high front vowel was diphthongized in WS"
- paraphrase: Ringe and Taylor provide the cleanest formal separation of the two phenomena. They first treat West Saxon diphthongization caused by initial palatals, then treat front mutation as a larger, system-wide process affecting many vowels and lexical classes. This makes them especially useful for showing why `SC055` is the historical center and `SC056` a narrower companion [@RingeTaylor2014, §§6.5.1, 6.6.1--6.6.4].
- conditioning: West Saxon palatal diphthongization applies after initial palatals to stressed non-high front vowels; front mutation applies under a following high front vocalic and has broad paradigmatic consequences [@RingeTaylor2014, §§6.5.1, 6.6.1--6.6.4].
- chronology: the handbook chronology here places the classical West Saxon diphthongization material to the left of front mutation, not after it. That does not cancel the CAPR card relation, but it does mean the card relation should be presented as a model-local dependency rather than a direct paraphrase of textbook chronology.
- examples: `giefan`; `gieldan`; `giest`; `scield`; `scieppan`; `bryd`; `wyrm`; `cyssan` [@RingeTaylor2014, §§6.5.1, 6.6.1--6.6.4].
- cautions: Ringe and Taylor strongly support both phenomena, but they do not naturally yield a coequal two-rule chapter in the same order that CAPR currently tests.

### Luick 1914
- source_key: Luick1914
- locator: §§181--183
- terminology: Palataldiphthongierung; i-Umlaut; palatale Beeinflussung
- short quotation: "Der wichtigste Fall von palataler Beeinflussung ... war die Veränderung der urenglischen Vokale durch i oder j der Folgesilbe"
- paraphrase: Luick is especially good for weighting the chapter correctly. He calls i-Umlaut one of the most thoroughgoing sound changes in English phonological history, while also treating it as part of a broader field of palatal influence that includes palatal diphthongization [@Luick1914, §§181--183].
- conditioning: i-Umlaut is triggered by `i/j` in the following syllable and most strongly affects guttural vowels, though front vowels and diphthongs also participate; the conditioning trigger is often later altered or lost in the transmitted forms [@Luick1914, §§181--183].
- chronology: Luick is valuable because he explicitly warns that the exact temporal relation of some palatal-diphthongization material is not always decidable, while still placing i-Umlaut clearly after earlier fronting developments. That caution aligns well with the dossier's need to avoid over-reading SC056 as a clean coequal follower [@Luick1914, §181; §182].
- examples: `endlifan`; `enihtre`; `anlic`; `hlæfdige` [@Luick1914, §§182--183].
- cautions: Luick gives excellent scale and chronology caution for SC055, but he is less useful than Ringe and Taylor for the exact `gift` / `sheath` witness set on the SC056 side.

### Fulk 2018
- source_key: Fulk2018
- locator: §4.7; §4.13
- terminology: front mutation; front umlaut; diphthongization by initial palatal consonants
- short quotation: "diphthongization by initial palatal consonants (which precedes front umlaut but not breaking)"
- paraphrase: Fulk is particularly valuable as a cautionary source. He gives a compact comparative account of front mutation as a major Germanic process, but he also states explicitly that diphthongization by initial palatal consonants precedes front umlaut in Old English. That makes him the clearest reminder that CAPR's `SC055 < SC056` result is a model-local sequencing claim rather than a simple restatement of comparative chronology [@Fulk2018, §4.7; §4.13].
- conditioning: front mutation is triggered by following high front vocalic material; West Saxon diphthongization follows initial palatal consonants and affects `a` and `e`-type vowels in West Saxon reflexes [@Fulk2018, §4.7; §4.13].
- chronology: Fulk explicitly places palatal-triggered diphthongization before front umlaut, while still treating both as early Old English vowel developments. This is one of the clearest source-based cautions against narrating SC056 as an uncomplicated historical right-edge follower.
- examples: `bryd`; `bēc`; `myne`; `giest`; `be-sciered`; `ceaster`; `geaf` [@Fulk2018, §4.7; §4.13].
- cautions: Fulk is best used for comparative framing and chronology caution, not for the exact CAPR witness pair `gift` / `sheath`.

## CAPR formulation versus literature

The source tradition and the CAPR pair line up well in one respect and less well
in another.

They line up strongly on `SC055`. Every major handbook source treats i-umlaut as
one of the central Old English sound changes. On that point, CAPR is simply
tracking a well-established historical chapter core.

They also line up on the existence of West Saxon palatal diphthongization, but
less cleanly on its role. The handbooks treat that material as real, yet
narrower, more dialect-specific, and sometimes earlier in the broader historical
chronology than front mutation itself. CAPR's `SC056` is therefore best read as
the model's right-edge articulation of that material, not as proof that the
standard historical tradition places a coequal palatal-diphthongization law
after i-umlaut.

That suggests a three-part distinction for the eventual production report:

1. **handbook-level history:** `SC055` as a major i-umlaut chapter;
2. **narrower companion material:** `SC056` as West Saxon palatal-diphthongization on the chapter's right edge;
3. **model-local chronology:** `SC055 < SC056` as a CAPR result anchored by current derivations such as `gift` and `sheath`.

## Chronology implications

The dossier supports the chronology cards, but only with a careful distinction
between handbook chronology and model chronology.

1. `SC055` must follow `SC052`. This is historically plausible and well aligned with the literature, since the sources treat palatalization and related fronting material as earlier than the full i-umlaut system.
2. `SC055` must precede `SC056`. This is strongly supported by the current cards through `gift` and `sheath`, but the source tradition does **not** simply state the same order in the same form. The eventual report will need to explain that the local CAPR dependency is narrower than the broader handbook chronology.
3. `SC056` must follow `SC055` in the current model. That reciprocal left boundary is real evidence for chapter structure inside CAPR.
4. `SC056`'s later side is boundary-limited. The no-break-before-`SC087` result must therefore remain exactly that: a search-boundary fact, not a historical claim that `SC056` must precede `SC087`.
5. `SC055` already interacts with later promoted material. `SC063` OE High Vowel Apocope must follow it, and the promoted SC066-SC068 report already uses `SC055` as the left boundary for the later syncope corridor.
