# Palatalization research note: identity still open

**Authority:** [`anglo-frisian-chronology.synthesis.md`](anglo-frisian-chronology.synthesis.md),
especially §§2–3. This note is a question/evidence guide, not another
chronology or an adjudication. No SC identity, scope, stage or rule changes.

CAPR requires a strictly tree-like topology **with an Anglo-Frisian node**.
Its existence is fixed here; the open question is whether a precisely
conditioned palatalization layer belongs on its stem or on daughter
branches, and whether that layer is phonetic, phonemic or assibilated at
the node. Failure to share one layer does not remove the node. See the
synthesis's required-node reconstruction table for the competing states.

## Separate the events before testing sharing

| Event | Why it must remain separate |
|---|---|
| Phonetic palatalization onset | Can precede a contrast between phonemes; continental onset is Laker's preferred inference, not proof (pp. 166, 171–173). |
| Productive palatalization cutoff | Kyn/kening preserve velars before mutation-created front vowels (Laker p. 168). |
| Phonemicization | Loss of predictable conditioning may establish a contrast without a new onset; Kortlandt and reported Fulk analyses differ (Kortlandt pp. 267–268). |
| Assibilation | Northumbrian final-k and Frisian distributions can differ after palatalization (Laker pp. 180–183). |
| Palatal diphthongization | Later and dialect-restricted; not interchangeable with consonant palatalization (Laker pp. 175, 184). |
| Voiceless h/χ palatalization | May have independent conditioning/history; not automatically synchronous with k/g (Laker pp. 176–177). |
| Analogy and loan substitution | Surviving forms may reflect leveling or adaptation rather than productive sound change (Laker pp. 171–172, 182–184). |

## What Laker actually argues

His positive thesis is a probable continental beginning, plus removal of
three objections to sharing; he does not demonstrate a common inherited
node (pp. 166, 172–173, 184).

1. **Reported Stiles palatal-diphthongization argument:** ċeorl rather
   than a proposed ċierl proves breaking before palatal diphthongization,
   not necessarily breaking before velar palatalization. Laker explicitly
   supplies compatible alternative orders (pp. 174–176).
2. **Reported h argument:** breaking before h need not date k/g
   palatalization if h developed independently (pp. 176–177).
3. **Reported different-conditioning argument:** tser(e)ke, dīk, rīk(e)
   versus English palatal forms may reflect later outcomes or leveling;
   ja-stem doublets are relevant controls (pp. 180–184).
4. **Au argument remains open:** **ċeapian/kapia**, exactly as printed
   without macrons, is real initial-velar evidence but admits competing
   vowel histories (pp. 177–180, 184). His criticisms of Stiles are not
   his own independent-palatalization thesis.

Stiles1995 and Fulk1998 remain missing direct works. Hogg1979,
Goblirsch1991 and Nielsen2001 have subsequently been acquired, but their
positions in this note are still known through reports pending direct review.
Hogg1992 and Luick are held, but cannot silently replace
those distinct works or unverified cited passages.

The synthesis now incorporates **direct** Luick §§118–120, printed
pp.130–132: common a-fronting, au first-component participation and a
tentative Frisian regressive-assimilation alternative
([@Luick1914, pp. 130–132]). This verifies selected vowel-history prose,
**not §637's palatalization claim**, which remains unlocated. The corrupt
Frisian intermediate glyph is not reconstructed; do not promote Laker's
Luick/Goblirsch palatalization/reversal scenario merely because those
different vowel-history passages have been checked.

## Competing histories to preserve

- **Campbell:** possible common onset, different completion after
  daughter vowel changes; Frisian palatalization distinguishes e from
  short-a fronting and open long ai reflexes (pp. 106–107).
- **Kortlandt:** AF4 ai>ā < AF6 restricted fronting < AF7; later
  English E14 is a distinct layer (pp. 267, 270–271, 273).
  P.267 explicitly differentiates already-contracted ai and unchanged au:
  there is no contradiction with the numbered chain.
- **Shared-fronted au then Frisian contraction/reversal:** Laker reports
  Luick/Goblirsch and Fulk alternatives; depalatalization or later backing
  must be evidenced, not assumed to rescue a tree (pp. 178–179).
- **Late English au restructuring:** Laker's reported scenario 4 permits
  an English-specific later palatalizing layer distinct from ordinary
  short fronting (pp. 179–180).
- **Versloot2025:** late-breaking reconstruction challenges the vowel
  framework, but expressly defers velar palatalization and au (p. 107).
  Its spelling fit does not resolve consonant-stage identity.

## Tree test and stage caution

Shared onset is at most H-compatible if it fits before the split; it does
not make all subsequent completed laws shared. An English-only late
fronting preceding an English late palatalization cannot identify that
late event with the earlier Frisian one (Kortlandt pp. 267, 271).
Conversely, eliminating a supposed breaking-before-palatalization edge
removes an obstacle, not proves inheritance (Laker pp. 175, 184).

Frisian-only earlier au-contraction followed by alleged shared fronting
fails that inherited **shared-event assignment**, even if it predicts the surface consonants;
the required AF node and tree remain
(Campbell pp. 91, 104; synthesis §2). No diffusion escape hatch applies
to CAPR's inference; Laker's continuum preference remains his own
faithfully recorded methodological view (p. 166).

## Evidence needed before a verdict

Prioritize paired au witnesses with k and g; ai versus inherited ē1
controls; ja-stem paradigms with leveling controls; independent dialect
evidence for final-k palatalization versus assibilation. See the witness
queue. Do not call a loan substitution a productive firing or a spelling
an observed phoneme (Laker pp. 168–172, 180–184).
Runic dates must separately state inscription, dating, phonetics,
phonemicization and inferred chronology (Waxenberger pp. 63–74).

**Open:** one inherited early innovation, independent onset, or common
early sensitivity with daughter extensions; au versus ordinary fronting
identity; reversals/leveling; and absolute stage boundaries. This note
licenses none of those alternatives as a verdict.
