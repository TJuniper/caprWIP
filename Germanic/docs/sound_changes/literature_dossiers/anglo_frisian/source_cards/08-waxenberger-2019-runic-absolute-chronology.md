# Waxenberger 2019, "Absolute Chronology of Early Sound Changes Reflected in Pre-Old English Runic Inscriptions"

> **Reading note for this card.** This source is unlike the handbook
> sources in the SC043 dossier: it supplies **absolute** (calendar)
> chronology, not just relative order. An archaeologically dated object
> gives a secure date for *an inscription*. It gives a date for a *sound
> change* only through a chain of further inferences. Following the
> research-area README ("Levels of claim"), every chronological claim in
> this card is decomposed into a five-step chain — see the table in §6.
> The single most load-bearing distinction throughout is that **the
> existence of a fronted allophone [æ] is not the same event as the
> phonemicization of /æ/**; wherever Waxenberger crosses that line the card
> flags it.

## 2. Author's own thesis

Waxenberger argues that the early major Pre-OE sound changes can be given an
**absolute** chronology by reading them off the dated Pre-OE runic
inscriptions, rather than only reconstructing their relative order "on
linguistic principles in handbooks" (p. 62). She frames a **Pre-OE runic
period ca. AD 425–610/650** written in an intermediate rune-row she calls
the **Pre-fuþorc** — the Common Germanic fuþark of 24 runes, "extended and
modified" during this period (pp. 60, 62). She takes the **Caistor-by-
Norwich Brooch (ca. AD 610–650)** to mark the **beginning of OE**, because
on it the four new/reassigned runes — ᚩ *o*, ᚪ *a*, ᛟ *œ*, ᚨ *æ* — "with
their new phonemic sound-values appear together for the first time," and
because "all the relevant sound-changes were obviously completed by then"
(pp. 60, 62). Her thesis statement: she seeks "to establish an absolute
chronology for the emergence of Pre-OE /ɔ̃:/, Pre-OE /a:/ (< Gmc */aɪ/) and
the phonemic split leading to Pre-OE long /a:/ and /æ:/ as well as the one
that brought about Pre-OE short /a/ and /æ/" (p. 61). The data reveal **four
phases**; "phases one and four are straightforward, while phases two and
three are more complex, with two scenarios being possible" (p. 60). Her
concluding meta-claim (p. 74) is a "chain reaction": once the first change
(the WGmc /a:/ split producing /ɔ̃:/) set the system in motion, the
remaining changes followed "within a relatively short time" to relieve the
system's temporary imbalance; she states that phonemicization of Pre-OE /a/
happened "if not parallel to, then at least immediately after" the
restructuring of the WGmc /a:/ reflexes.

Note the explicit hedge she attaches to the whole enterprise: "I am aware of
the fact that a new find and/or new dating methods may have a great impact
on my theory" (p. 61).

## 3. Positions reported, not held

- **Bammesberger 1996 (p. 21):** that Gmc */aɪ/ "was already
  monophthongized by ca. AD 500." Waxenberger uses this as the *foundation
  for her Scenario 1* (pp. 68, 71) but does not commit to it; she says
  Scenario 2 (late monophthongization) "seems more probable to me" (p. 74).
  This is reported, not held.
- **Vennemann forthcoming:** the alternative *skanomodu* etymology deriving
  ā from Gmc *skain-* 'visible, shiny' (a-grade class I deverbal adjective
  base), under which *ai > ā* is "shared by Frisian and Old English" and the
  inscription "can no longer be classified as exclusively Frisian … but only
  as Anglo-Frisian" (p. 63). Reported as an option, quoted.
- **Roschdi forthcoming:** that the Amay Bone Comb and the Schweindorf and
  Harlingen solidi "can only be placed within a wider North-Sea Germanic
  context" by their fuþorc-diagnostic runes (pp. 64, 64 n.13). Reported.
- **The "Anglo-Frisian unity" debate** (Nielsen 1994; Hines 2017): she
  reports the question as "rekindled" and open, and treats Pre-OE vs
  Pre-OFris. separation as "still a matter of debate" and "one of the
  research questions of the RuneS project" (pp. 60 n.1, 64). She does *not*
  assert Anglo-Frisian unity.
- **Bammesberger 2006 (p. 178):** two readings of the Caistor Astragalus
  diphthong, [råɪxån] or [råexån] (p. 68 n.15) — reported, and she adds her
  own third possibility [råı̊xån] (nasalized second element).
- **The three sound changes producing /ɔ̃:/** are attributed to Campbell
  1959 (§§ 119, 121, 127) and the allophonic *[ã:] stage to Hogg 1992
  (§§ 313, 3.22) (p. 66) — reported handbook mechanisms she adopts.
- **Bammesberger (2003) vs Hines (1991b):** two translations of the Caistor
  Brooch text (p. 62 n.4) — reported.
- Various object datings are reported from **Hines, Hills, Scull, Blackburn,
  Looijenga, Quak** (see §6, §13) — she adopts these as inputs, they are not
  her own datings.

## 4. Languages and evidence actually used

- **Pre-Old English runic inscriptions** (the **Pre-OERC**, ca. AD 425–650)
  do the primary work: 13 inscriptions, of which 7 + the unprovenanced
  *skanomodu* solidus are "useful with regard to the early sound changes"
  (p. 62).
- **Pre-Old Frisian runic inscriptions** (the **Pre-OFrisRC**, 5th c.–650):
  6 inscriptions + *skanomodu*, of which only 4 are relevant — Amay Bone
  Comb, Harlingen & Schweindorf solidi, *skanomodu* solidus (pp. 63–64). She
  stresses their "Frisianness … is by no means certain" (p. 64).
- **Runic graphemics / the rune-row itself** is treated as evidence: the
  addition of ᚩ and ᚪ, the reassignment of ansuz ᚨ to /æ(:)/ with a shift
  from row-position 4 to 26 (rune *æsc*), and ᛟ *ōþil* → /œ(:)/ in slot 24
  (pp. 65, 67). The London Thames scramasax rune-row order is also invoked
  (p. 67).
- **Handbook phonology** (Campbell 1959, Hogg 1992) supplies the reconstructed
  mechanisms she dates.
- Not used as argument: no place-names, no manuscript OE, no North Germanic
  comparanda beyond the Undley provenance discussion. Dialect-internal OE
  distinctions are largely absent (she works at the Pre-OE level); one WS
  form, *sāwon*, is cited in passing (p. 73).

## 5. Proposed relative chronology

Waxenberger's relative order is largely expressed *through* the absolute
phases, but the following ordering edges are stated or clearly entailed.
These are kept SEPARATE from the calendar dates in §6.

### Pre-OE (shared/undifferentiated stem — she does not split OE from OFris.)

```
[1] WGmc /a:/ split (→ Pre-OE /ɔ̃:/ + Pre-OE /a:/)
        <  [2] monophthongization Gmc */aɪ/ > Pre-OE /a:/
        <  [3] partial merger of the two long ā's  +  phonemicization of [æ:] > /æ:/
        ‖  [4] phonemic split of short /a/ into /a/ and /æ/   (parallel-to / immediately-after [3])
```

- edge `[1] < [2]`: (pp. 66–67) [1] is "the starting point in both
  scenarios" (p. 71); the new rune ᚩ /ɔ̃:/ is already present on the Undley
  Bracteate (Phase 1), before the āc ᚪ rune that marks the */aɪ/
  monophthong appears.
- edge `[2] < [3]`: (pp. 69, 72) the partial merger of [a:]₁ (< WGmc /a:/)
  and /a:/₂ (< Gmc */aɪ/) presupposes that the */aɪ/ monophthong exists;
  merger and phonemicization of [æ:] are dated *after* monophthongization.
- relation `[3] ‖ [4]`: (p. 74) "the phonemicization of Pre-OE /a/ …
  happened, if not parallel to, then at least immediately after the
  restructuring processes of the Pre-OE reflexes of WGmc /a:/." This is her
  "chain reaction." She does **not** give a strict edge between [3] and [4];
  the card must not upgrade "parallel to / immediately after" into an order.
- A separate, indirectly-anchored edge: **allophonic i-umlaut of /o(:)/ <
  phonemicization of i-umlaut of /o(:)/** (p. 73). The allophonic stage is
  attested by ᛟ used in umlaut environments (Watchfield, Chessell Down); the
  phonemicized /œ:/ is first overt on the Caistor Brooch. She uses this as an
  *indirect* proxy for the timing of edge [4] (the /a/ split was likewise
  triggered by the "overload" of [æ] produced mainly by i-umlaut of [a] and
  [å]).

### English vs. Frisian, separately

**Not given.** She deliberately does *not* separate an OE chain from an
OFris. chain; she "used the sound-changes of Pre-OE as a framework and
examined how the Frisian runes … fit into that frame" (p. 64). The Frisian
material is folded into the same Pre-OE phases, with provenance flagged as
uncertain (pp. 64, 70). This is important for CAPR: the source supplies **no
Frisian-specific ordering edge** that could discriminate a shared node from
a parallel innovation (see §10, §12).

**Summary of what is dated, and the inferential distance:**

- Every calendar date attaches directly only to an **object** (column b),
  most on archaeological/art-historical grounds, two (skanomodu, Harlingen)
  on **numismatic** grounds, and several with **disputed provenance** (Undley)
  or **no provenance** (skanomodu).
- The date reaches a **sound change** only across three inferences
  (c → d → e). The card must not present column (e) as if it had the
  security of column (b).
- The distinction (d) matters most: rows 1, 5, 8 are claims about
  **phonemicization / a completed split**; rows 4 and 7 are claims that a
  state was **still allophonic**. Conflating "allophone [æ] existed" with
  "/æ/ was phonemic" would collapse rows 4 and 5 into one — which is exactly
  the error the README warns against.

**The *skanomodu* conditionality (must not be flattened).** *skanomodu* is
useful as a terminus **only conditional on two things** (pp. 63, 68):

1. **Its etymology.**
   - *skaun-* reading (dominant): first element < adjective *skaun-*
     'beautiful'; monophthongization **Gmc *au > ā**, which is "exclusively
     Frisian" (p. 63). Under this reading the coin dates an **au-monophthong**
     (a Frisian change), *not* the */aɪ/ change she is chronologizing.
   - *skain-* reading (Vennemann forthc.): ā < Gmc *skain-* 'visible, shiny';
     monophthongization **Gmc *ai > ā**, "shared by Frisian and Old English"
     (p. 63). Under this reading the coin *does* date the `*aɪ > ā` change of
     her main chronology.
2. **Its linguistic provenance (English or Frisian?).** The coin is
   unprovenanced (p. 63); its "Frisianness … is by no means certain" (p. 64).
   Under the *skaun-* reading it is classified as **exclusively Frisian**;
   under the *skain-* reading it "can no longer be classified as exclusively
   Frisian … but only as Anglo-Frisian, i.e. Anglo-Saxon or Frisian" (p. 63).

Consequently: her use of *skanomodu* as the **TAQ for `*aɪ > ā`** (p. 68,
row 3) holds cleanly only under the *skain-* etymology *and* only if the
datum is admitted to the Pre-OE frame at all. Under the *skaun-* etymology
the coin's evidential force is about a *Frisian* `*au > ā` change and its
bearing on the Pre-OE `*aɪ` monophthongization is indirect. She papers over
this by writing "with the monophthong obviously in place, according to all
hitherto proposed etymologies" (p. 68) — true that *some* ā-monophthong is
present under either etymology, but *which change* and *whose language* is
undetermined. The card records the conditional as conditional. (Also: the
ending *-u* is "not a reliable feature for Frisian provenance," p. 63 n.11.)

**Two-scenario ambiguity (Table 5, pp. 72–73).** Because the date of the
`*aɪ` monophthongization is "the least straightforward point" (p. 67), she
offers two absolute scenarios that differ in Phases 2–3:

- **Scenario 1** (monophthongization ca. AD 500, per Bammesberger 1996):
  two long ā-phonemes /a:/₁ (< WGmc /a:/) and /a:/₂ (< Gmc */aɪ/) coexist
  ca. AD 500–575/610, then partially merge; [æ:] is phonemicized ca. AD
  575/610.
- **Scenario 2** (monophthongization shortly before AD 575–610; her
  preferred, "seems more probable," p. 74): the new /a:/ from */aɪ/ emerges
  and "incorporates" allophonic [a:] of WGmc /a:/; [æ:] reaches phonemic
  status at the end of Phase 3.

Both scenarios share Phase 1 (ᚩ /ɔ̃:/, ca. AD 450–500) and Phase 4 (short
/a/ split, ca. AD 575–625).

**Changes she can date vs. cannot:**
- *Datable:* emergence of /ɔ̃:/ (Phase 1, ca. AD 450–500); the WGmc /a:/
  split; the short-/a/ split into /a/ + /æ/ (ca. AD 550–575/625, Phase 4);
  phonemicization of i-umlaut of /o(:)/ (ca. AD 550–610/650).
- *Cannot pin down:* the **exact date of the `*aɪ` monophthongization**
  (hence the two scenarios, pp. 67, 74).
- *No direct runic evidence:* the **phonemic split of /a/ caused by i-umlaut**
  has "no immediate runic evidence" (p. 73); she supports it only
  **indirectly** via the datable phonemicization of i-umlaut of /o(:)/.

## 7. Proposed shared innovations

Waxenberger works within a single Pre-OE frame and folds the (uncertainly
Frisian) coin material into it, so she does not present a list of
individually-argued *shared* OE+OFris. innovations. What she does state:

- The **āc ᚪ** and **ōs ᚩ** runes are "diagnostic Anglo-Frisian runes"
  common to the Pre-OE and Pre-OFris. corpora (p. 63) — a **graphemic**
  (surface) commonality, offered as such, not as a demonstrated single
  phonological innovation.
- She treats the short-/a/ split as **"Pre-OE(/Pre-OFris.)"** jointly (pp.
  72–73), the Harlingen solidus serving as "an example of phonemicized /a/"
  on the (uncertainly) Frisian side (p. 72, Table 5 note).
- Under the *skain-* reading only, the `*ai > ā` monophthongization is
  reported (from Vennemann) as "shared by Frisian and Old English" (p. 63) —
  reported, conditional, not asserted.

She explicitly leaves the **Anglo-Frisian unity** question open (pp. 60 n.1,
64), so none of these is advanced as a proven shared *node*.

## 8. Proposed independent developments

- The **Gmc *au > ā** monophthongization (relevant under the dominant
  *skaun-* reading of *skanomodu*) is stated to be **"exclusively Frisian"**
  (p. 63) — i.e. *not* shared with OE. This is the clearest
  language-specific development in the paper, and it is precisely the reading
  under which *skanomodu* is a Frisian, not Pre-OE, datum.
- Beyond that she does not argue that any OE/OFris. correspondence is a
  parallel (independent) innovation; she reports the whole shared-vs-
  independent question as unresolved (§3).

## 10. Tree-test implications  *(CAPR analysis — not Waxenberger's)*

Applying the README criterion. This section is CAPR's, not the author's.

**Can any alleged Anglo-Frisian innovation still occupy a shared ancestral
node, if her chronology is correct?**

- Her chronology is almost entirely a **Pre-OE-internal ordering with
  calendar anchors**; it does **not** furnish an OE-vs-OFris. differential.
  She *assumes* a joint Pre-OE(/Pre-OFris.) frame rather than testing it (p.
  64). Therefore her results are **compatible with** a shared node for the
  low-vowel changes (WGmc /a:/ split; short /a/ split) but do **not
  positively establish** one: there is no Frisian-specific edge here that
  either forces or forbids a shared node. Under the README four-way scheme
  these remain **S / U**, not **H**.
- **Absolute dates constrain a tree only if they can be attached to specific
  ordered edges.** Where she *can* do this: the TPQ (Astragalus, row 2) and
  TAQ (skanomodu, row 3) bracket the `*aɪ` monophthongization to a real
  interval (ca. 425/475 – 575/610), and the ansuz-vs-āc transition (rows 4–5)
  brackets the short-/a/ split (ca. 550 – 575/625). These are genuine edge
  constraints **within the Pre-OE chain** and are tree-usable *for the OE
  branch*. Where she **cannot**: none of these dates is attached to an edge
  that separates OE from Frisian, so none of them constrains the *shape of
  the OE/OFris. split* — they constrain positions along a single assumed
  stem only.

**Which correspondences would have to be independent under a tree model?**

- The one place she supplies a language-specific edge cuts **against**
  sharing: the *skaun-* reading makes `*au > ā` **exclusively Frisian**
  (p. 63). If that reading is right, that monophthongization is a Frisian
  branch change (parallel/independent, README class **P** on the *au* side),
  and *skanomodu* is not a Pre-OE datum at all. This directly touches the
  SC030/SC043 *au*-side question flagged in `missing-direct-sources.md`
  (Goblirsch item).

**Where does she invoke wave/continuum/linkage instead of an ordering
argument?** She does not argue for a wave model, but she twice **withholds**
a tree-usable edge by deferring the split question: Pre-OE separation from
"a North-West Germanic continuum or a possible intermediary stage … Anglo-
Frisian … North Sea Germanic" is "still a matter of debate" (p. 60 n.1), and
the Frisian specimens "can only be placed within a wider North-Sea Germanic
context" (Roschdi, p. 64). Recorded neutrally: these are places where the
source supplies **no edge**, not places where it is wrong.

**Net for CAPR:** this source is strong for **absolute anchoring of the
Pre-OE low-vowel chain** and for the **allophone-vs-phoneme sequencing**
(rows 4/5, 7/8), but it does **not** adjudicate any shared-node question,
because it assumes rather than tests the OE/OFris. relationship.

## 11. Uncertainty grading

- **Firmly demonstrated (data compel):**
  - The Pre-fuþorc rune-row was *graphically* reorganised (ᚩ, ᚪ added; ansuz
    reassigned to /æ/ and moved 4→26; ᛟ→/œ/) by the Caistor Brooch horizon
    (pp. 65, 67). This is a graphemic fact; its *phonological* interpretation
    is a (well-supported) inference.
  - Relative brackets: `*aɪ` monophthong absent on the Astragalus (ca.
    425/475) and present on skanomodu (ca. 575/610) — subject to the reading
    caveats (rows 2–3).
- **Author's preferred reconstruction (consistent, alternatives known):**
  - **Scenario 2** (late `*aɪ` monophthongization) — "seems more probable to
    me" (p. 74); Scenario 1 explicitly remains "possible."
  - Short /a/ split dated ca. AD 550–575/625 as a **phonemicization** driven
    by allophone "overload" (pp. 69, 71).
  - Nasalized open /ɔ̃:/ at the Undley date, with later /ɔ̃:/>/ɔ:/>/o:/
    (p. 61 n.2).
- **Plausible but non-unique inference:**
  - The **absolute** date of the WGmc /a:/ split (tied to disputed-provenance
    Undley).
  - *skanomodu* as TAQ for `*aɪ > ā` (conditional on etymology + provenance;
    §6).
  - Phonemicization of i-umlaut of /o(:)/ ca. 550–610/650 used as an
    **indirect** proxy for the /a/ split (p. 73).
- **Explicitly unresolved (author says so):**
  - The exact date of the `*aɪ` monophthongization — "the least
    straightforward point," two scenarios (pp. 67, 74).
  - The "Anglo-Frisian unity" and Pre-OE/Pre-OFris. separation questions
    (pp. 60 n.1, 64).
  - The provenance/"Frisianness" of skanomodu, Amay, Harlingen, Schweindorf
    (pp. 62 n.6, 63–64).
  - Whole-theory hedge: new finds/datings "may have a great impact" (p. 61).

## 12. Implementation-ready extracts (Frisian)

**Insufficient for a Frisian cascade.** She deliberately does not build a
separate Frisian chain (§5); the Frisian coins are read *through* the Pre-OE
frame, with provenance uncertain. The only Frisian-specific extractable
datum is:

- **Gmc *au > ā (monophthongization), "exclusively Frisian" (p. 63).**
  - input: Gmc *au ; output: OFris. ā ; conditioning: (unrestricted
    monophthongization, per the *skaun-* etymology of *skanomodu*) ;
    scope: Frisian only ; approximate stage: present by ca. AD 575–610 if
    skanomodu is Frisian ; ordering: n/a (single datum) ; evidence:
    *skanomodu* (unprovenanced, conditional) ; uncertainty: **high** —
    depends on the disputed etymology and on Frisian provenance.

Everything else she dates is presented as **Pre-OE(/Pre-OFris.) jointly**
and cannot be assigned to a Frisian branch without begging the split
question. No other implementation-ready Frisian extract is available.

## 13. Dependencies on sources CAPR does not hold

Load-bearing works cited by Waxenberger that CAPR does **not** hold locally
(cross-ref `../missing-direct-sources.md`):

- **Waxenberger forthcoming, *A Phonology of Old English Runic Inscriptions*
  (chapter 3)** — the single most load-bearing dependency. Nearly every
  quantitative/chronological claim defers detail to it (pp. 61, 62, 63, 66,
  68, 69, 70, 73, 74). Without it, the *arguments behind* the datings in §6
  are known only in summary.
- **Bammesberger 1996** (Frisian and Anglo-Saxon runes) — the **foundation
  of Scenario 1** (monophthongization ca. AD 500, p. 68). Not held (CAPR
  holds Bammesberger 1990 and 1997, which are *not* substitutes).
- **Vennemann forthcoming** (skanomodu: Frisian or Anglo-Frisian?) — the
  alternative *skain-* etymology on which the *non*-exclusively-Frisian
  reading of skanomodu depends (p. 63). Not held.
- **Object datings**, all load-bearing for §6 and not independently held:
  Hines 1990/1991 (Astragalus, Undley, Loveden Hill, Welbeck, Chessell
  Down); Hills 1991a/b (Spong Hill, Undley archaeology); Scull 1986
  (Watchfield); **Blackburn 1991** (skanomodu, Harlingen, Schweindorf coin

- **Roschdi forthcoming** (OFris. runic corpus edition) — for the
  "North-Sea Germanic context" placement of the Frisian specimens (p. 64).
- **Campbell 1959, §§ 119, 121, 127** — the three mechanisms producing
  /ɔ̃:/ (p. 66). *Held* (`campbell_old_english_grammar.txt`), so this one is
  checkable locally, but note the specific sections cited.
- **Hogg 1992, §§ 313, 3.22** — the allophonic *[ã:] stage (p. 66). *Held*
  (`hogg_vol1.txt` / `hogg_fulk_2011_grammar_old_english_vol1_phonology.txt`),
  checkable.
- **Nielsen 1991/1994** (Undley vocalism; Anglo-Frisian review) and
  **Hines 2017** (the Anglo-Frisian question) — frame the unity debate she
  defers to (pp. 62 n.6, 64). Not held; cf. the Nielsen and Stiles items in
  `../missing-direct-sources.md`.
- **Waxenberger 2013 / 2016 / 2017 / 2018** — her own prior work carrying the
  detailed readings (esp. 2017 for the periodization and the "How English is

None of these absences blocks the *architecture* of this card, but the
**Bammesberger 1996** and **Vennemann forthcoming** gaps directly weaken
two named inferential steps (Scenario 1's date; the skanomodu conditionality),
and the **Waxenberger forthcoming ch. 3** gap means the underlying arguments
for every date in §6 are held only at summary strength.
