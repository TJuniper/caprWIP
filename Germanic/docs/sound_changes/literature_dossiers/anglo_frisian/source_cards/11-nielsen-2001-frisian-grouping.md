## 2. Author's own thesis

Nielsen interprets the monophthongization of *ai*/*au*, fronting of long and short *a*, palatalization/affrication, i-mutation and breaking in Frisian as independent innovations (p. 521). His decisive difference from the chronology he attributes to Stiles is that long-*ā* fronting is an independent Proto-OFr. innovation “postdating (or co-occurring with)” monophthongization, rather than an earlier Ingvaeonic innovation (p. 521). He prefers a structural push-chain account in English too: new open monophthongs restrict the inherited long vowel's space; short-vowel front/back differentiation follows the long system (pp. 514–516). He nevertheless accepts an earlier North Sea Germanic package—nasal/oral differentiation, nasal loss before fricatives and uniform verbal plurals, with certain pronominal changes—before the Anglo-Saxon exodus (pp. 520–521). This is rejection of the *listed changes as shared Anglo-Frisian innovations*, not a demonstration that English and Frisian lack any common ancestor (pp. 512–513, 521).

## 3. Positions reported, not held

- Sweet's common Anglo-Frisian ancestor and Bremer's language unity contrast with Siebs's more limited label for shared phonological features (pp. 512–513). Morsbach, Rooth, Dal and Århammar supply proximity/Sprachkreis/continuum explanations including Saxon, with varying accounts of its later German features (p. 513).
- **Nielsen's report of Stiles 1995:** common development did not extend beyond long-*ā* fronting; Frisian/English are Ingvaeonic relict areas (p. 513); fronting preceded monophthongization (pp. 514, 521). This is not direct access to Stiles, nor a separately verified refutation of his argument.
- **Nielsen's report of Fulk 1998:** short-*a* fronting and palatalization were common (p. 513); specifically, palatalization, **not phonemicization of palatals**, preceded divergence, with a concession about the lack of compelling reason for that early date (p. 520). The quotation is evidence for Nielsen's presentation of Fulk only.
- Traditional English order, attributed to Campbell/Hogg/Stiles: long-*ā* fronting < *ai* monophthongization < short-*a* fronting, with *au* > *ēa* contemporaneous with the last event (pp. 514–515). Nielsen proposes a different chain mechanism, not this sequence as his own (p. 515).
- Jørgensen/Campbell put Frisian monophthongization before mutation; Hofmann reverses them and assigns monophthongization to the eighth century, later proposing contact with Low German to account for the *ai* split (pp. 515–516). Nielsen objects to the early *au* rune evidence, nonmatching lexical distributions and the undated Low German split (pp. 515–516).
- Krupatkin's nasal-system scenario is adopted as a promising possibility, not an unqualified established history; Nielsen revises his own earlier Undley interpretation (p. 517). Kuhn's largely post-emigration North Sea Germanic account is contrasted with Nielsen's acceptance of earlier correspondences (pp. 520–521).

## 4. Languages and evidence actually used

Manuscript OFr. and OE accented systems, reconstructed Proto-OFr. long vowels, and North/West Frisian dialect agreement do substantive work; Insular North Frisian and Weser Frisian preterites enter through Hofmann's reported argument (pp. 514–516). Rune evidence is the London gold solidus, Undley bracteate, rune names/values, Oostum comb and Folkstone tremissis—not a directly reviewed edition of each object (pp. 515, 517, 519). OS common manuscript forms contrast with Straubing Heliand vocalism; OS unaccented evidence distinguishes Eastphalia/Engern from Westphalia and the M/S/C manuscripts (pp. 516, 518). R1, c. 1300, matters for unaccented Frisian vowels, with early Northumbrian versus ninth-century OE comparanda (p. 518). OHG, OLF and Old West Flemish provide negative or regional controls; Gothic and Old Danish/Swedish provide selected comparanda (pp. 514, 517–521).

## 5. Proposed relative chronology

`<` means preceding; `≤` below explicitly allows co-occurrence, not a strict edge. These are source-local reconstructions, not CAPR chronology entries.

### English

```text
ai > ā ≤ long-vowel front/back split-plus-merger < short-a front/back split
short-a fronting < breaking < i-umlaut
long/short fronting < affricated outcomes < i-umlaut
```

- **New ā ≤ long-vowel restructuring:** preferred structural push-chain premise, *ā* from *ai* restricts the inherited *ē¹* reflex; *ǣ* versus *ā*, with the nasalized reflex eventually merging with *ō* (p. 515). The explicit allowance for co-occurrence is stated for Frisian in the conclusion; the English passage likewise describes triggering, not an independently dated interval (pp. 515, 521). English *au* stays diphthongal (*ēa*); do not import Frisian *au* > *ā* into this edge (pp. 514–516).
- **Long contrast < short contrast:** “modelled on, and therefore secondary” is the author's systemic premise, with *stæf* versus *faran*, not a lexical feeding derivation (p. 515).
- **Short fronting < breaking < mutation:** derivational argument from *seah* and *nieht*, with the exact source chains in §9 (p. 517).
- **Fronting < affricated outcomes:** fronted-*a* environments in *ceaf*, *cēace* are Nielsen's conditioning argument (p. 520). **Palatal/affricated treatment < mutation:** retained velar *cynn* versus palatal outcomes, subject to the event-layer qualification below (pp. 519–520).
- No English restoration edge is supplied: *faran* illustrates the back member of a split, not an explicitly ordered restoration operation (p. 515).

### Frisian

```text
1 ai/au monophthongization
  ≤ 2 long-ā fronting / long-system split-plus-merger
  < 3 short-a front/back differentiation
  < 4 palatalization/affricated treatment
  < 5 i-mutation

ai > ǣ < “gravity” split (ǣ > ā in grave environments) < i-mutation
ai/au monophthongization < i-mutation [preferred, contested]
```

This is the **recoverable five-event spine**, not a numbered five-stage list printed by Nielsen, not five absolute periods, and not a rigid ordering of every subchange (pp. 514–515, 519–521). In particular the first two may co-occur, and the gravity branch has no separately demonstrated placement relative to short fronting or palatalization (pp. 514, 521).

- **1 ≤ 2:** new open vowels compress the space of WGmc. *ā* < Gmc. *ē¹*; Nielsen's structural push-chain preference (p. 514), maintained regardless of which order of monophthongization/mutation is chosen (p. 516), qualified by “postdating (or co-occurring with)” (p. 521). This is a theoretical causal premise, not compelled by *dēd(e)* alone.
- **2 < 3:** short-*a* split models the established long-system contrast; *stef* versus *fara* (p. 515). No separate restoration process, full short-fronting exception list, or date is given (pp. 514–515).
- **3 < 4** (also **2 < 4**): affricated *tsetel*, *tzīse* before fronted short/long vowels; Nielsen infers independent emergence from his earlier independent-fronting reconstruction (p. 520).
- **4 < 5:** unchanged velar in *kenn* < *kunja-* is the counterfeeding diagnostic: mutation did not create a vowel that the earlier palatal process could then affect (pp. 519–520).
- **Gravity split < mutation:** *rāp*, *māra* versus *stēn*, *brēd*, and consistently front *hēra* from mutated *au*, which Nielsen says would not result if gravity assimilation followed mutation (p. 514). “Grave” is the source's conditioning label; it is not expanded into an invented segment list.
- **Monophthongization < mutation:** Nielsen favours Jørgensen/Campbell, supported by runic *au* > *ā* and the pan-Frisian lexical distribution of the *ai* split; Hofmann's contrary history remains explicitly discussed (pp. 515–516).
- **Breaking:** independent *i, e* > *iu* before *ch(t), chs, rch*, and *i* > *iu* before *(n)kw, ngw*; no Frisian breaking-versus-mutation edge is established (p. 517). No English breaking edge may be transferred here.

**Event-layer caution:** p. 519 says the OE phonemic split arose “in the wake of i-umlaut” and illustrates a new contrast; p. 520 calls the “new phonemes” earlier than umlaut. For Frisian p. 519 similarly speaks of increased phoneme number but argues from pre-mutation palatal treatment. Record the pre-mutation conditioning and later contrast separately; the chapter's terminology does **not** license a precise common date for allophonic palatalization, affrication and phonemicization (pp. 519–520).

### Shared / undifferentiated stem

```text
nasal/oral differentiation ≤ nasal loss before voiceless fricatives
loss of n in 3pl *-anþi < generalized uniform present plural endings
WGmc unaccented *-z loss < generalized consonantless pronouns
generalized *i/*e ‘he’ < initial h- remedy
```

The first is a reconstruction of the nasal package: nasalized long *ā* before nasals merges with the products of *a* + nasal + voiceless fricative; Nielsen accepts the contrast and loss as pre-exodus but supplies no separately measured interval between them (pp. 517, 520). The plural and pronoun edges are stated causal/levelling premises, not direct sound-law derivations of each paradigm (pp. 520–521). They are **North Sea Germanic**, not exclusively Anglo-Frisian; wider ancestry or additional explanation would be needed for Saxon/OLF occurrences (pp. 520–521).

Other explicit orders: accented long/short fronting < unaccented *-a* fronting/merger with *-ai* reflex; that merger < *-o* > *-a* in the OS *-e, -a* group (systemic/drag-chain premises, pp. 518–519). Their apparent common unaccented system is not evidence of a pre-split event, since accented fronting is reconstructed independently (p. 518). Medial *z* > *r* < medial *s* voicing, and WGmc. *ð* > *d* < medial *þ* voicing, are explicit source orders (pp. 519–520).

## 6. Absolute chronology

- **Before the fifth-century Anglo-Saxon exodus:** Nielsen assigns the nasal package, uniform present plurals, consonantless monosyllabic pronouns and nom.sg.m. *h-* here; this is a reconstruction tied to an historical migration, not an inscription date for each change (pp. 520–521).
- **Late fifth century:** Undley object/new rune, interpreted now by Nielsen as already denoting *o*; its hypothetical earlier nasal value is an additional inferential step and remains a possibility (p. 517).
- **Sixth century:** London gold solidus with *skanomodu*, interpreted *skānomōdu* < *skaunamōd-*; object dating plus phonological identification supports *au* > *ā* by then (p. 515). Earliest English attestation of the *āc* rune is seventh-century, explicitly possibly fortuitous; that is not its invention date (p. 515).
- **From the seventh century:** colonization of Sylt/Föhr/Amrum; uniform lexical distribution of the *ai* split suggests an earlier Common Frisian change, conditional on that migration/dialect-history interpretation (p. 515).
- **Eighth century:** Hofmann's monophthongization date is a reported alternative, not Nielsen's accepted date (p. 515). Frisian mutation **on the phonemic level** “may not” have arisen until this century; the hedge and layer are essential (p. 517). Jørgensen's reconstructed pre-mutation long system is said to have no currency beyond the eighth century (p. 514).
- **Sixth-century OE i-umlaut:** explicitly Luick's dating as reported by Nielsen (p. 517), not an independently established Nielsen terminus.
- **c. 600 / c. 800:** Folkstone *æniwulufu* / Oostum *habuku* date objects with distinct written *f* and *b*; graphemic retention is not itself a uniquely diagnostic phonetic distinction (p. 519).
- **c. 1300 / ninth century / earliest OE manuscripts:** R1 / comparison OE unaccented systems / earlier four-vowel OE system are text-system observations, not matching sound-change dates (p. 518).

## 7. Proposed shared innovations

Nielsen accepts pre-exodus nasal/oral differentiation and nasal loss before fricatives, uniform verbal plurals, final-consonant loss/generalization in monosyllabic pronouns, and initial *h-* in ‘he’ (pp. 520–521). He offers nasalized long/short *a* as the only plausible shared early **accented-vowel** shift, with a tentative rune interpretation, not fronting or eventual rounding as one indivisible inherited event (pp. 516–517). Similar fricative organization is described across OFr./OE/OS, but the chapter does not prove a single exclusive Anglo-Frisian event; medial velar-fricative loss distinguishes OFr./OE from OS without a demonstrated shared chronological placement (pp. 519–520).

## 8. Proposed independent developments

Explicitly independent: Frisian *ai/au* monophthongization; long and short fronting; palatalization/affrication; mutation; breaking (p. 521). English/Frisian/Saxon long-vowel developments and fronting/rounding are described as independent, despite their resemblance; English *au* was not even monophthongized (p. 516). A hypothetical OS/Frisian parallel split is structurally conditioned and need not imply common origin or contact diffusion (p. 516). The extension of pronominal *h-* beyond nom.sg.m. in Frisian/English/Middle Dutch “probably” occurred independently (p. 521). The OS palatal stop before an i-mutated vowel belongs to another chronology, not the OFr./OE development (p. 520).

## 10. Tree-test implications

Under CAPR's fixed strict tree **and retained Anglo-Frisian node**, Nielsen's preferred daughter-local monophthongization premise blocks **his package of consequent long fronting**, and therefore short fronting and fronting-dependent affricated treatment, from being assigned as single shared-node events: a shared X cannot follow a required daughter-only Y (pp. 514–516, 520–521). This is conditional on event identity and the causal premise, not a proof from the final correspondences alone. The allowance for monophthongization/fronting co-occurrence does not create positive evidence for an earlier shared fronting; a common-node version would require reconsidering which part of the triggering event is actually daughter-only (p. 521).

Earlier nasal/oral differentiation and the nasal-loss package remain **compatible** with shared ancestry, and in Nielsen's account precede the independent long-vowel restructuring; their wider North Sea scope may require a node earlier than Anglo-Frisian, not an exclusive Anglo-Frisian diagnostic (pp. 517, 520–521). Mutation and breaking are explicitly daughter-local, but no single direct daughter-only predecessor proof is given for every mutation/breaking subevent; report the author's conclusion without manufacturing missing edges (pp. 517, 521). Palatal allophony must not be excluded merely by substituting later phonemicization for it: Nielsen explicitly reports Fulk's distinction, even while rejecting the broad shared account (p. 520).

The historical survey's continuum, relict-area and diffusion accounts are attributed positions, not CAPR explanations (pp. 512–513). Nielsen's own structural parallelism explicitly requires neither common origin nor diffusion for the hypothetical OS/Frisian split (p. 516). Excluding these event assignments **does not remove the required node**, and compatible placement of the earlier package does not prove its exclusive ancestry (pp. 516–517, 521).

## 11. Uncertainty grading

- **Firmly demonstrated at the source-observation level:** different OFr./OE ai/au reflexes; examples of retained velars before mutation-created front vowels; contrast in broken versus unbroken fronted-a treatment (pp. 514, 517, 519–520). Their common/independent origin is a further inference, not an observation.
- **Author's preferred reconstruction:** monophthongization-driven long fronting, short contrast modelled on long, independent fronting and subsequent affricated outcomes; early Frisian monophthongization and pre-exodus nasal/pronominal package (pp. 514–517, 520–521).
- **Plausible but non-unique inference:** pan-Frisian lexical distribution implies pre-colonization ai split; rune acrophony implies English invention/export; hypothesized nasal origin of the new rune (pp. 515, 517). Independence of affrication is conditional on the preceding fronting reconstruction (p. 520).
- **Explicitly unresolved/qualified:** orthographic e may conceal /e:/ versus /æ:/; push versus drag possibilities; monophthongization/mutation alternatives entertained even while a preferred order is defended; incomplete OS dialect/conditioning knowledge; Undley long/short value and revised interpretation; eighth-century mutation at the phonemic layer only, hedged (pp. 514–517).

**Multiple histories within this discussion:** yes. Nielsen records two mutation/monophthongization orders and says his structural fronting consequence holds whichever is selected (p. 516); he allows fronting to follow **or co-occur** with monophthongization (p. 521); possible nasal-rune origins need not be its actual Undley value (p. 517). This is not a free choice of every permutation, nor endorsement of the traditional fronting-before-monophthongization account (pp. 514–516, 521).
