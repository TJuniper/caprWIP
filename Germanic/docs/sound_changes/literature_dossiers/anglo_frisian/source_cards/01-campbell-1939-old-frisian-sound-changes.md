## 2. Author's own thesis

Campbell tests "whether … a very early Anglo-Frisian linguistic unity can be
assumed" and, in Part II, whether OE parallels illuminate "the nature and
the chronology of the principal early Frisian sound-changes" (p. 78). He
confines himself to "the vowels of accented syllables" (p. 78).

His answer to the unity question is **negative for a close unity, at most a
loose one**. Part I "has revealed no evidence that the two languages ever
formed a closely knit unity … in the earliest times they had no closer
contact with each other than with OSax" (p. 92, §10). He grants only that
"at a somewhat later date" they develop WGerm ā in all circumstances, and
Germ *a* before nasals, alike, so "there may have been a period in which
they formed a loose unity. A compact unity is not to be assumed" (p. 92).
Crucially he argues that the "most celebrated point of similarity" — the
fronting of Germ *a* to *æ* — actually **arose independently**: "since the
fronting of Germ *a* took place later than the monophthongization [of *ai*]
in OE, it must have taken place independently in the two languages" (p. 91).
Surviving agreements he attributes to "a similarity of articulation
developed during the period of close contact" rather than to genetic unity
(p. 92), citing Borchling approvingly for the idea that the conquering
Saxons "had previously been for a time in close contact with the Frisians,
but had not any ancient relationship with them" (p. 92 n.).

Part II's positive contribution is an explicit **ordered chronology of the
principal OFris vowel changes** (II §12, p. 104), built by importing the
well-understood OE relative order onto the Frisian material. Campbell
concedes the solution "rests on the assumption that ā was fronted earlier
than *a*" and that he is "much influenced by the views of Professor
J. R. R. Tolkien, as expressed in his lectures" (p. 90 n.).

## 3. Positions reported, not held

- **Siebs' definition of the OFris *ai* > ā retraction environments** (open
  syllables before a back "dunklem" vowel or *w*; after *w* without following
  i/j) is reported and broadly endorsed, "despite Heinertz," as having "its
  general method of approach … correct" (pp. 96, §2).
- **van Helten's rival theory** of the same environments (labial/velar
  consonants, back vowels, consonants modified by following *u/w*) is
  reported and treated as less satisfactory (p. 96, §2; p. 101 n., §8).
- **Karstien's theory** that OFris ā (not the *e*-forms) was the regular
  reflex of Germ *ai*, the *e*-forms being Low German loans, is reported at
  length and **rejected**: "the whole assumption is little short of absurd"
  (p. 97 n., §3).
- **Heinertz** (IF xxx.312) is cited as denying the "verdumpfend" character
  of the OFris *ai* > ā environments; Campbell rejects this (p. 96, §2).
- **Luick's** claim that OKt `æ ~ e` spelling variation indicates an
  intermediate sound is reported and rejected as "not justified" (p. 94 n.,
  §1).
- **Kluge** (*Urgermanisch*³ 121) is cited as "incorrectly" placing OE
  *pere* under the *i > e* change (p. 81 n., §4 of Part I).
- **van Haeringen** (PBB xliv) is cited as having proved OFris fronting
  operates in open as well as closed syllables and for "a final demolition
  of the view that fronting is later than i/j-mutation" (p. 93 n.). Campbell
  adopts this result; it is van Haeringen's, not his own.
- The claimed **OFris ~ OKt** parallel in the IE *eu* reflexes is reported
  and rejected: "they are, in fact, radically dissimilar" (p. 85, §5).

## 4. Languages and evidence actually used

- **Old English** by dialect does heavy work; the dialect split is
  load-bearing. West Saxon (only OE dialect with ǣ < WGerm ā before
  i-mutation, p. 96); **Anglian/Mercian** (Vespasian Psalter; early *æ > e*
  "Zweite Aufhellung," p. 93); **Kentish** (late *æ > e*, datable in
  charters, p. 93); **Northumbrian** (preserves WGerm *eo/eu* distinction,
  pp. 85, 91; restoration of *a* before *r*+cons., p. 94 n.).
- **Old Frisian** by branch: OEFris/ONFris (rounding of *a* before nasals
  written *o*, pp. 87–88); OWFris (later restoration of *a*, p. 88). Heavy
  use of **modern Frisian dialects** as witnesses: Saterland, Wangeroog
  (East); Föhr, Sylt, Karrharde, the Halligen (North); Japicx (Modern West
  Frisian).
- **Old Saxon** is the constant control: the Part I demonstration turns on
  OE and OFris never agreeing on a form "which is not found in OSax also"
  (p. 80). OHG is a further comparand.
- Evidence is **lexical/paradigmatic and orthographic** (manuscript
  spellings, modern-dialect reflexes). No runic, onomastic or archaeological
  material is used.

## 5. Proposed relative chronology

Campbell supplies (a) OE ordering edges in Part I, used to date *a*-fronting
against the diphthong monophthongizations, and (b) an explicit numbered
OFris chronology in II §12. Both are reproduced.

### English (edges established in Part I §9, pp. 90–91)

```
fronting(WGerm ā long)  <  monophthongization(ai → ā)  <  fronting(Germ a short → æ)
```

- edge `fronting(ā) < (ai→ā)`: (p. 90) "The final stage of this process
  [ai → ā] must have been reached after the fronting of WGerm ā, or the new
  ā (< ai) would have been fronted also."
- edge `(ai→ā) < fronting(a)`: (p. 90) "the monophthongization must have
  been completed before the fronting of Germ *a*, or the first element of
  the diphthong would have been fronted, and a diphthong with *æ* as its
  first element could hardly have been monophthongized to ā."
- Germ *au* stays a diphthong in OE; its first element becomes *æ* "no doubt
  when Germ *a* > æ," second element unrounds to *a* (written *ea*, early
  *æo*) (p. 91).
- Unrounding of *au*'s second element `<` unrounding of *eo*'s second
  element (p. 91): the *eo* second element is *not* generally unrounded in
  OE (only a limited Northumbrian area).

### Frisian (the numbered chronology, II §12, p. 104)

Transcribed from the corrected text (open ǣ = Campbell's special open glyph;
see header):

```
(1) WGerm ā → ē (normal) / ō (before nasals);  Germ ą & 'Ingvaeonic' ą → ō;
    Germ a → o before nasals  (this o ≠ Germ ō)
(2) Germ au → ā
(3) Germ a → æ  (except where already o before nasals);
    consequently Germ ai → æi, monophthongized to ǣ before (4)
(4) æ (< Germ a) and ǣ (< Germ ai) → ā  under certain conditions
(5) æ (< Germ a) → e  ('Zweite Aufhellung');  ǣ (< Germ ai) NOT affected
(6) i/j-mutation
(7) shortening & lengthening before consonant groups
(8) æ (shortening of ǣ < ai, and of the mutation of ā < au) → a
(9) [later changes, order undetermined]
```

**[corrected]** Step (1)'s normal reflex of WGerm long ā is **ē**, not the
`ǣ` the withdrawn extract implied. Campbell's own footnote 1 to §12 (now
legible) states: "If the fronting was first of all to æ, this sound was
raised to ē before change (4) took place" (p. 104 n. 1). So the OFris
history of WGerm long ā is `ā → (æ) → ē`, with the raising `æ → ē` ordered
**before change (4)** — corroborated at p. 96 n. 1: it "must have reached
the later stage [ē] before *æ* > *a* before a back vowel, for there would
otherwise be … a corresponding change ǣ > ā, as in WSax, but there is no
trace of such a change in OFris."

Edges and their diagnostics:

- `(1) < (2)` / `(2) < (3)`: *au* must contract to ā (2) **before**
  *a*-fronting (3) can reach its first element: "Probably the second element
  [of *au*] was developed to *a*, as in OE, but at an earlier date, before
  the fronting of Germ *a* could affect the first element. Then the
  resulting *aa* was contracted to ā" (p. 91).
- `(3): a→æ feeds ai→æi→ǣ`: (p. 90) in OFris "the monophthongization of
  Germ *ai* to ē may well have been over an intermediate stage *æi*, which
  was occasioned by the fronting of Germ *a* to *æ*." So *a*-fronting `<`
  the OFris *ai* monophthongization — the **reverse** of the OE order (see
  §10). The OFris *ai* reflex is Campbell's *open* ǣ, "considerably more
  open than ē < WGerm ā" (p. 95), still kept distinct from ē < WGerm ā "in
  many living dialects" (p. 90).
- `(3) < (4)`: restoration/retraction to ā presupposes the fronted *æ*/ǣ
  inputs; the redeveloped OFris *a* "is more likely to have been from *æ*
  than from *e*" (p. 94, II §1); the whole series is written "**a > æ > a**"
  and assumed "in both languages" (p. 95).
- `(4) < (5)`: the Zweite Aufhellung *æ > e* "must have overtaken *æ* before
  the operation of i/j-mutation" (p. 97, §3); the restored *a* of (4) is
  what (5) may or may not re-front (p. 104).
- `(5) < (6)`: (p. 97) the Zweite Aufhellung precedes i-mutation "for … the
  *æ* produced by that change [mutation] had quite a different history."
- `(6) < (7) < (8)`: shortening/lengthening act on the i-mutation outputs;
  the shortening product of ǣ (< ai) and of mutated ā (< au) only later
  merges to *a* (§§6, 8, 11).
- **eo → ia** (WGerm *eo* < IE *eu*): "cannot be placed in chronological
  relationship to the changes dealt with in the last section, but … the
  unrounding of its second element must have been later than (3)" (p. 105,
  §13) — later than *a*-fronting, else *ea* would have shared *a > æ*.
- **Frisian breaking** (Germ *e, i, ī* → *iu* before *ht, hs*
  **[corrected]** — the withdrawn extract's third input "ā" was a misread;
  the clean text has *e, i, ī*, p. 105) and **iu < i before *w/u***: "Two
  important early changes, which cannot be chronologically related with the
  others" (p. 105, §13). Breaking is nonetheless ordered *later than
  i-mutation*: `siucht` 'sees' shows "it must have taken place later than
  i-mutation, for the *i* of the 3rd pers. sg. … was not syncopated till
  after it had caused mutation," and loss of intervocalic *h* is later still
  (p. 105).

### Shared / undifferentiated stem (changes placed before any split)

Campbell places no change on a genuinely *shared node*; he places several in
a period of at most "loose unity" / "close contact":

- WGerm ā → ō before nasals (p. 86, §7): "the first universal and
  far-reaching agreement."
- Germ *a* → slight rounding (*o*) before nasals (pp. 88–89, §8), asserted
  to have "took place simultaneously and to a similar degree in OE and
  OFris."
- WGerm ā fronting "may be said to be the same in the two languages, except
  in the WSax dialect of OE" (p. 89, §9) — but see §10: he denies this
  licenses a unity.
- The **onset** of velar sensitivity of *k, g* is *not excluded* from "a
  period when English and Frisian were in sufficient contact to develop some
  common characteristics" (p. 106, §14) — a deliberately weak, contact-framed
  claim.

## 6. Absolute chronology

Campbell offers almost no calendar dates, and none directly for a Frisian
change. The only absolute anchors are OE Kentish and are several inferential
steps from any sound change:

- **8th-century Kentish charters** and the "early ninth-century will of
  Æthelnoth of Eastry" keep `æ (< Germ a)` and `e (< Germ e)` "perfectly
  distinguished" (p. 93, §1). *Dates a manuscript spelling distinction*,
  from which Campbell infers the Zweite Aufhellung had not yet merged the
  sounds in Kt — two inferential steps (spelling → phoneme; Kt → prehistory).
- **811**: a charter "which refers to a transaction which took place in 811"
  shows "the form *et* beside *æt*," the earliest evidence for Kt `æ/e`
  confusion (p. 93 n.). *Dates the transaction, and by assumption the
  manuscript, not the sound change*; used only to argue the Kt merger
  "cannot reasonably be dated back to the prehistoric period."
- **"Second decade of the ninth century"**: the point from which Kt `æ/e`
  spelling confusion appears (p. 93). A spelling terminus, not a Frisian one.

No absolute date is assigned to any OFris change; the §12 chronology is
purely relative. (Borchling's monograph, invoked for the contact
hypothesis, is dated **Assen 1938**, p. 92 n. **[corrected]** from "1935".)

## 7. Proposed shared innovations

Developments Campbell regards as genuinely common (though he insists the
commonality is contact/parallel-articulation, not a compact genetic unit):

| Development | Page | Reason he gives |
|---|---|---|
| WGerm ā → ō before nasals (also nasalized ą, 'Ingvaeonic' ą) | p. 86 (§7) | "universal and far-reaching agreement"; both have `mona, komon, brocht`. |
| Germ *a* → rounded *o* before nasals | pp. 88–89 (§8) | Divergences (E/WFris vs E/NFris) are "later than the time when mutation took place"; "no objection to assuming" simultaneous early rounding. |
| WGerm ā fronting (ǣ in WSax, raised to ē elsewhere and in OFris) | p. 89 (§9) | "the same in the two languages, except in the WSax dialect of OE." |
| Velar palatalization sensitivity of *k, g* (onset) | p. 106 (§14) | Nothing shows it does *not* date to the contact period; both front *k, g* before the *a*-derived front vowel. |

## 8. Proposed independent developments

Superficially similar developments Campbell explicitly makes parallel /
post-split:

| Development | Page | Reason he gives |
|---|---|---|
| Fronting of short Germ *a* → æ | pp. 90–91 (§9) | In OE it is *later* than *ai*-monophthongization, which "proceeded on such different lines" it "must have taken place after the dissolution of the Anglo-Frisian unity"; therefore *a*-fronting "must have taken place independently in the two languages." |
| Monophthongization of Germ *ai* (OE ā vs OFris open ǣ) | pp. 90–91 (§9) | Different outcomes/mechanisms; OFris ǣ < *ai* and ē < WGerm ā "still distinguished clearly in many living dialects." |

## 10. Tree-test implications

*(This section is CAPR analysis under `../README.md`, not Campbell's own
framing. Campbell works with "unity/loose unity/contact," not the
ordered-tree criterion.)*

**Innovations that could still occupy a shared ancestral node if Campbell's
chronology holds (H- or U-candidates):**

- **WGerm ā → ō before nasals** (§7) and **Germ *a* → rounded *o* before
  nasals** (§8). Placed earliest (§12 step 1), before any demonstrably
  daughter-specific edge, with asserted simultaneity. No secure intervening
  daughter-specific change is offered, so the CAPR test does not *force* them
  off a shared stem. They remain **U**, not **H**, because Campbell's own
  support is "a similarity of articulation … during the period of close
  contact" (p. 92) — a contact argument, not a shared ordering edge.
- **WGerm ā (long) fronting to ē** (§9, §12 step 1). Compatible with a shared
  node in the pure ordering, so **U**. Note the WSax ǣ vs non-WSax/OFris ē
  raising split is internal, so the *raising* stage is post-node.

**Correspondences that must be independent parallel changes under a tree
model (P), on Campbell's own chronology:**

- **Fronting of short Germ *a* → æ.** The decisive CAPR case, and Campbell
  reaches the same verdict by his own route. The relative order of
  *a*-fronting and *ai*-monophthongization is **reversed** between branches:
  OE has `ai→ā  <  a→æ` (p. 90), OFris has `a→æ  <  ai→æi→ǣ` (p. 90). A
  branch-specific change on this reconstruction (the differently-executed *ai*
  monophthongization, `Y`) sits on opposite sides of the shared-looking
  `X = a-fronting`. This is exactly the README's forcing configuration ⇒
  **P**, conditional on accepting the proposed intermediate and event
  identities. Campbell's Frisian *æi* intermediate is introduced with
  "may well"; the tree consequence must not harden that premise into
  demonstrated fact. (Campbell: "it must have taken place independently in the two
  languages," p. 91.) *This finding is unchanged by the re-OCR and if
  anything is now stated more sharply* (see closing summary).
- **Monophthongization of *ai* and treatment of *au*** ⇒ **P** (different
  outputs, explicitly post-dissolution, pp. 90–91).
- **IE *eu* reflexes** ⇒ **P** ("entirely different," §§5–6).
- **Zweite Aufhellung *æ > e*** ⇒ **P**: OFris-specific, "more drastic," and
  even within OE it differs Merc vs Kt (pp. 93, 97).
- **Completion of velar palatalization** ⇒ **P** for the completed law: its
  cutoff is set by OFris-specific Zweite Aufhellung outputs (front *k/g*
  before *e < æ* but back before open ǣ < *ai*, p. 107), so the finished
  distribution cannot be a single shared event even though its *onset* may
  be shared (**U** for onset).

**Where the author invokes wave/continuum/contact in place of an ordering
argument (neutral record — these mark absence of a tree-usable edge, not
error):**

- "there may have been a period in which they formed a **loose unity** …
  A compact unity is not to be assumed" (p. 92, §10).
- Agreements "may have been due to a **similarity of articulation** developed
  during the period of **close contact**" (p. 92).
- Borchling's contact-not-inheritance model endorsed: the Saxons "had
  previously been for a time in **close contact** with the Frisians, but had
  not any ancient relationship with them" (p. 92 n.).
- The Northumbrian ~ OFris *eo/eu* parallel is "**interesting rather than
  significant**" (p. 85) — a similarity from which "no close connexion …
  can be inferred."

## 11. Uncertainty grading

**Observations and the inferences Campbell presents as compelling:**
- OE, OFris (+ OSax) never agree on a reflex absent from OSax in the
  *i/e, o/u, eu* material (Part I §§1–6, pp. 79–86) → no compact early unity.
- The *ai*-monophthongization ordering edges in OE (p. 90) follow
  necessarily from the non-fronting of ā < *ai* and of the *ai* first element.
- The **independence** of short-*a* fronting is Campbell's stated conclusion:
  "it is evident … it must have taken place independently" (pp. 90–91).
  It depends on his preceding reconstructions, including the hedged Frisian
  *æi* intermediate; it is not an independently observed historical event.
- Velar retention before restored *a* (`gard`) dates restoration before
  mutation (p. 94 n.).

**Author's preferred reconstruction (consistent; alternatives acknowledged):**
- The whole `a > æ > a` retraction series for OFris ("it seems highly
  probable that the same series of changes … took place in both languages,"
  p. 95).
- The open value of OFris ǣ (< *ai*), argued from four convergent points
  (§7, pp. 100–101), and the *æi* (not *ei*) intermediate stage — hedged
  "may well have been over an intermediate stage æi" (p. 90).
- The numbered §12 chronology as a whole.

**Plausible but non-unique inference:**
- OFris `ku` as evidence for ū < ō — "may be the result of a variation
  already existing in IE" (p. 83).
- Simultaneity of *a*-before-nasal rounding in OE and OFris — asserted with
  "no objection to assuming," i.e. compatibility, not proof (p. 89).
- Late retention of a distinct WGerm *eu* in OFris — only "if we are able to
  lay any weight on the form *treuwa*" (p. 85).

**Explicitly unresolved (author says so):**
- Whether the Zweite Aufhellung affected restored *a* in OFris: "must be
  left undecided" (p. 98, §4).
- Whether the change *i > e* before *z* was Frisian at all: "cannot,
  therefore, be shown to have been Frisian" (p. 82, §2).
- The precise environments of OFris *ai* > ā: "have never been satisfactorily
  defined" (p. 96).
- The circumstances of Frisian breaking: "exceptionally obscure" (p. 105).
- Whether *æ < Germ a* was still distinct from *e < Germ e* at mutation:
  "It is not possible to decide" (p. 99).

## 12. Implementation-ready extracts (Frisian)

Preparation only — **no** cascade change is licensed here. Ordering keys are
Campbell's §12 step numbers (p. 104). Symbols per the header convention
(ē = close < WGerm ā; ǣ = Campbell's open long front vowel < Germ *ai* /
mut. of ā < *au*; æ = short *a*-fronting product; ā = back long *a*).

**F1 — WGerm ā → ē / ā-nasal rounding**
- input: WGerm ā (long); also nasalized ą and 'Ingvaeonic' ą
- output: ō (before nasals); **ē** elsewhere, via an intermediate *æ*
  (footnote 1, p. 104: "raised to ē before change (4)")
- conditioning: before nasal consonants → ō
- scope: general OFris
- stage: §12(1), earliest
- ordering: precedes all of F2–F8; the *æ → ē* raising precedes F5 (p. 96 n. 1);
  ō merges with ō < Germ ō only post-mutation (§12 step 9)
- evidence: `mona, komon, brocht, toth` (p. 86)
- uncertainty: firm as data; "shared" status is contact-based, not tree-based
- **[corrected]** output is **ē**, not ǣ (withdrawn-extract error)

**F2 — Germ *a* → *o* before nasals**
- input: Germ *a* (short)
- output: *o* (rounded), **not** identical to Germ *o*
- conditioning: before nasal consonants
- scope: OEFris/ONFris write *o*; OWFris later restores *a*
- stage: §12(1)
- ordering: before F3 ("except when it had already become *o* before nasals")
- evidence: Saterland `mon, lound`; OEFris `o`-spellings (pp. 87–88)
- uncertainty: preferred reconstruction; branch divergence is post-mutation

**F3 — Germ *au* → ā**
- input: Germ *au*
- output: ā (via *aa* contraction; second element → *a* first)
- conditioning: unconditioned
- scope: general OFris
- stage: §12(2)
- ordering: **F3 < F4** (au must contract before *a*-fronting reaches its
  first element, p. 91)
- evidence: OFris ā-reflexes of *au*; mutation product ǣ (p. 99)
- uncertainty: "Probably the second element was developed to *a* … but at an
  earlier date" (hedged, p. 91)

**F4 — Fronting of Germ *a* → *æ* (+ *ai* → *æi* → open ǣ)**
- input: Germ *a* (short), outside nasal context (see F2)
- output: *æ*; and feeds Germ *ai* → *æi* → **open ǣ**
- conditioning: general; operates in open and closed syllables (van Haeringen, p. 93 n.)
- scope: general OFris
- stage: §12(3)
- ordering: **F3 < F4**; **F4 < (OFris ai monophthongization)**; **F4 < F5 <
  F6**; also **F4 < eo→ia unrounding** (p. 105)
- evidence: retraction series `makia, falla`; open ǣ < *ai* (§7)
- uncertainty: CAPR-critical — **independent of OE** on Campbell's chronology
  (P); the *ai* → *æi* intermediate is hedged ("may well have been," p. 90),
  the independence conclusion is firm ("must," p. 91)

**F5 — Restoration/retraction *æ*, open ǣ → ā**
- input: *æ* (< Germ *a*) and open ǣ (< Germ *ai*)
- output: ā
- conditioning: (1) before *l*+cons.; (2) sporadically before *r*+cons.;
  (3) before *h*+cons.; (4) before back vowel of following syllable (p. 94);
  the ǣ < *ai* retraction adds Siebs' "verdumpfend" set (open syllable +
  back vowel/*w*; after *w* without i/j) (p. 96)
- scope: general OFris; environments "difficult to determine … all the
  instances may not be equally old" (p. 94 n.)
- stage: §12(4)
- ordering: **F4 < F5 < F6**; the restored *a* keeps velar *k/g* (`gard`),
  so **F5 < i-mutation and < palatalization cutoff** (p. 94 n.)
- evidence: `fara, draga, makia, waria, halia; falla, hals, half; flax,
  wax, achta, nacht; gard, kald` (pp. 94–95)
- uncertainty: preferred reconstruction; environments explicitly under-defined

**F6 — 'Zweite Aufhellung' *æ* → *e***
- input: *æ* (< Germ *a*, i.e. F4 output not retracted by F5)
- output: *e*, merging (perhaps immediately) with *e* < Germ *e*
- conditioning: general; **does not** affect open ǣ (< Germ *ai*)
- scope: general OFris ("more drastic" than Merc/Kt; resembles Merc, i.e.
  pre-mutation)
- stage: §12(5)
- ordering: **F5 < F6 < F7 (i-mutation)**; may (undecided) re-front restored
  *a* before cons.+back-vowel but not before cons.-groups (p. 98)
- evidence: OFris *e* of the texts = *e* < Germ *e*; contrast preserved open
  ǣ < *ai* (pp. 97–98)
- uncertainty: whether it touched restored *a* is "left undecided" (p. 98)

**F7 — i/j-mutation**
- input: ā → open ǣ; ō → ē; *a* → *æ*; *o* (< *a*/nasal) → *æ*; etc.
- output: front vowels; mutation of ā (< *au*) = open ǣ, identical with older
  ǣ (< *ai*); mutation of *a* = open *æ* (written *a/e*); mutations of ō, ū
  fall together with close ē
- conditioning: following i/j
- scope: general OFris; northern-island dialects keep some mutation products
  distinct
- stage: §12(6)
- ordering: **F6 < F7 < F8**; velar palatalization sensitivity ends *after*
  F6 but is fixed by F7 (front *k/g* before *e < æ* but back before open
  ǣ < *ai*, p. 107)
- evidence: `falt/felt, stant/stent, kelde` (§§8–10)
- uncertainty: several sub-outcomes "not possible to decide" (p. 99)

**F8 — shortening/lengthening before consonant groups; ǣ-shortening → *a***
- input: long vowels before consonant groups; specifically open ǣ (< *ai*)
  and mutated ā (< *au*)
- output: shortened open ǣ → *æ* → *a*; close ē → *e*; lengthened *æ* →
  merges with open ǣ
- conditioning: before consonant groups / in open syllables
- scope: general OFris, with dialect splits (§12 step 9)
- stage: §12(7)–(8)
- ordering: **F7 < F8**; final *a*/*e* split of unlengthened mutated *a* is
  later than F8 and "had not yet occurred in the period of the oldest
  manuscripts" (p. 105)
- evidence: `leda/latte, bleda/bled, reda/*red` (p. 99)
- uncertainty: preferred reconstruction

**F9 (adjunct) — Frisian breaking *e, i, ī* → *iu* before *ht, hs***
- ordering: later than i-mutation and later than loss of intervocalic *h*
  (`siucht`), but "cannot be chronologically related with the others" (p. 105)
- uncertainty: environments "exceptionally obscure"; not to be conflated with
  OE breaking. **[corrected]** inputs are *e, i, ī* (not *ā*).
