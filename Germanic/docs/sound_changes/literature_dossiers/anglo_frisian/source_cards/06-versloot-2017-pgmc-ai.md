## 2. Author's own thesis

Versloot argues that the monophthongisation of stressed PGmc. *ai* was
**one gradual, non-monolithic areal process** that spread over 'Germania'
in **two waves** between **ca. 400 and 900 AD**, crossed by a third,
gradual trend (p. 281 [abstract], p. 288, p. 319).

- **Wave 1** — monophthongisation in **"velar" contexts**, producing **ā**;
  it **spread from north to south** and, in the clearest languages, was
  "primarily limited to the contexts before -h and -r" (p. 281), with **-w**
  and further velar codas included in the fuller statement (p. 288,
  point 1). Within wave 1 the context **before -h is oldest and before -r
  later** (pp. 295, 299, 312).
- **Wave 2** — monophthongisation in the **remaining ("non-velar")
  contexts**, producing **ē** everywhere; it **did not reach Old High
  German or Old West Nordic** (nor Old Low Franconian), affecting only
  "central" languages — **Old Frisian, Old Saxon, Old East Nordic**
  (pp. 292–293).
- **A crossing trend `ai > æi`** (assimilation of the two diphthong
  elements) **spread south to north** at the same time (pp. 292–293). Where
  `æi` had already arrived before monophthongisation reached a region, the
  monophthong is **ē (< æi)**; where the vowel was still `[ai]`, the
  monophthong is **ā** (pp. 292–293, 314).

He states the "velar"-oldest logic as: partial monophthongisers (High
German, West Nordic) monophthongise **only** in the velar context, which
"can be taken as an indication that it reflects the oldest stage of the
process" (p. 288, point 3). **Old English is the broadest monophthongiser**:
"totally absent … 'velarity' condition … in Old English with unrestricted
monophthongisation to ā" (p. 289); OE has ā in *all* contexts (Table 3,
p. 312). Versloot's explicit meta-claim is that the "independent development
vs. common innovation" controversy "becomes superfluous" under an areal
reading (p. 319), and that structurally different outcomes in closely
related dialects "should not prohibit the appreciation of the communalities"
(p. 317).

**Verification of the CAPR bibliography note.** The note ("wave 1 before *h
and *r, north-to-south; wave 2 not reaching OHG or OWN; OE among the
broadest") is **confirmed** in substance (pp. 281, 288–289, 292–293), with
two refinements the note omits: (i) wave 1 is defined as the whole **"velar"
set** (h, r, and often w; in Frisian also g and rounded back vowels /
`-Cu-/-CwV-`), not only h and r (p. 288); and (ii) wave 1 is internally
ordered **h before r** (pp. 295, 312). Old Low Franconian is a separate case:
it monophthongises in *all* contexts except before palatal `i, j`
(pp. 286–287, 289 point 4).

## 3. Positions reported, not held

- **Stiles (1995: 198–200, 205–207):** the diverging development of *ai* is
  "among the oldest differences" between OF and OE (p. 282); Stiles concludes
  monophthongisation was an **independent** process in OE and OF, works in
  relative-chronology terms with no absolute dates or locations, and himself
  proposes a **wave/dialect-continuum** model on the Continent prior to the
  Anglo-Saxon migration; Stiles treats Frisian as more closely related to Old
  Saxon than to Old English (pp. 316–317). *(Versloot builds on Stiles's wave
  intuition but supplies the dating/geography Stiles omits.)*
- **Bremmer (2008: 287):** same "among the oldest differences" position
  (p. 282).
- **Kortlandt (2008: 270–271, 273):** monophthongisation `ai > ā` was a
  **common Anglo-Frisian development** on the Continent **before the 5th c.**
  (before the Anglo-Saxon migration), with Frisian later fronting ā > ǣ under
  conditions; implies a much later OE/OF split (pp. 282, 317). **Versloot
  rejects Kortlandt** (pp. 318: "Kortlandt's (2008) chronology must be
  rejected"; contradicted by *raïhan* and *aib*).
- **Nielsen (2001: 516):** monophthongisation "took place **independently** in
  each of the three languages [OS, OE, OF]" (pp. 282, 316–317). *(Versloot
  reports and argues against the "independent-by-definition" reasoning,
  pp. 317–318.)*
- **Voyles (1992: 4, 104…205):** "areal changes" spanning dialects without a
  common pre-stage; early Nordic monophthongisation ca. 450, shared OE/OF ā
  assumed, OS→OHG spread in the 7th c. Versloot says his own reconstruction
  "is in line with these observations" but goes further (p. 282; cf. p. 317).
- **De Vaan (2011):** phonological rules for OF ā (before r, h, g, velar
  (semi)vowels), and the analysis that OF ā/ē reflects a **partial fronting of
  ā** (De Vaan's account, not Versloot's) (pp. 286, 302 n.18). Versloot uses
  De Vaan's contexts but **reformulates** them statistically (§5.1).
- **Kümmel (2014a: 40–41):** alternative — early overall Frisian
  monophthongisation `ai > ǣ` with later retraction to ā in velar contexts,
  parallel to `au` (p. 309). Versloot **rejects** it (pp. 318–319).
- **Hofmann (1964: 182–185):** structuralist relative order for Insular North
  Frisian: **i-mutation < delabialisation < monophthongisation of ai & au**;
  Insular North Frisian had i-mutation but not yet monophthongisation
  (pp. 306–307). Versloot **partly reconciles** Hofmann by de-coupling *ai*
  from *au* (pp. 307–308).
- **Findell (2012a: 94; 2012b: 50):** Continental Runic `<ae>` "cannot be
  united or fitted into any model of the monophthongisation" (p. 297).
  Versloot reinterprets `<ae>` as the assimilation `ai > æi`, not a monophthong
  (p. 298).
- **Braune & Reiffenstein (2004: §44):** OHG monophthongisation dated to the
  7th c.; `<ai> > <ei>` completed in Franconian before ca. 750, in the south
  by ca. 800 (pp. 286, 299).

## 4. Languages and evidence actually used

Doing real argumentative work:

- **Old English** — outcome (always ā); Anglo-Saxon runes (§4.2): *āc*-rune,
  *raïhan* (Caistor-by-Norwich), Skanomodu (pp. 295–296).
- **Old Frisian / Runic Frisian** — the pivotal case (§4.3, §5.1); a
  **105-lemma** statistical dataset and Runic Frisian inscriptions
  (Westeremden, Rasquert, Oostum) (pp. 296–297, 302–306).
- **Old Nordic (esp. Old East Nordic) / Early Runic** — Scandinavian *faihan*
  attestations (Table 1), Rö *s[a]ir-* ~ *fahido* (§4.1) (pp. 293–295).
- **Old Saxon** — Gallée's deviating `<a>/<æ>/<ei>` spellings; a **27-token /
  20-lemma** count with a significance test (§5.2) (pp. 287–288, 310–311).
- **Old High German / Continental Runic** — *wrait/wraet*, *feha*, *aergu[n]þ*,
  Geroaldo coin; OHG grammar datings (§4.4) (pp. 297–300).
- **Old Low Franconian / Dutch coastal relics + Low Countries onomastics** —
  `*-haim` place-names (Gysseling); relic ā/ō words (*aak*, *Haamstede*)
  (§2.5, §4.5) (pp. 286–287, 300–302).

Dialect detail that matters: **within Frisian**, a **west→east cline** (ā more
common in the west, ē in the east incl. Insular North Frisian), and the special
status of **Insular North Frisian** (pp. 304–305, 306–308). Within OE he does
not sub-divide dialects for *ai*. Runic material is used with archaeological
dates as *termini*, not as direct sound-change dates (see §6 caveats).

## 5. Proposed relative chronology

Versloot's core relative chronologies are stated per macro-region and per
language. He is explicit that these are **relative** orders realised at
**different absolute dates** in different places (§6).

### The two regional chains (p. 292)
```
North:  ai > ā (velar)  <  ai > æi  <  æi > ē (other contexts)      [OF, OEN]
South:  ai > æi         <  æi > ē (velar)  <  æi > ē (other)         [OHG; OS, OLF]
```
- Both chains share the crossing edge **`ai > æi` (assimilation)** which is a
  **prerequisite for any ē outcome**: "the development to æi = ae … forms a
  prerequisite for the later transition from æi > ǣ > ē" (p. 298). Edge:
  `ai > æi  <  æi > ē` (pp. 292, 298).
- North vs south differ only in whether `ai > æi` arrives **before** or
  **after** wave-1 monophthongisation reaches the region (pp. 292–293).

### English
```
ai > ā (all contexts; waves 1&2 inseparable)  <  i-mutation (ā > ǣ)
```
- edge `ai>ā < i-mutation`: (p. 296) "the monophthongisation of ai > ā in
  Proto-Old English must have taken place … before the i-mutation, which
  affected ā < PGmc. ai" (diagnostic: *stān* : i-mutated *lǣdan* < *laiđjan*,
  p. 284, §2.2).
- Versloot: in OE "the second wave came so quickly after the first one that
  the south-north movement of ai > æi did not reach England before that time",
  so the two waves **cannot be separated** and OE monophthongises to ā
  everywhere (p. 293).

### Frisian
```
syncope  <  ai>ā monophthongisation (wave 1, velar)  <  i-mutation  <  delabialisation  <  ai>ē (wave 2)
```
- edge `syncope < monophthongisation (& < i-mutation)`: (p. 302) forms like
  *fætt* < *faitida-*, *aththa* < *aiþiþan* show a cluster that "only arose
  after syncope … indicating that syncope antedated monophthongisation … and
  certainly i-mutation."
- edge `monophthongisation (wave 1) < i-mutation`: (pp. 302, 305–306)
  "i-mutation is most likely younger than the ā < PGmc. ai"; supported by
  i-mutation applying to *lēna* < *laih(w)njan* (p. 306).
- edges `i-mutation < delabialisation < wave 2`: Versloot's reconciled order
  (pp. 307–309): stages 1–2 (au, and ai-velar → ā) are **common-Frisian**;
  then i-mutation; then (migration); then delabialisation of i-mutated long
  vowels; then wave 2 (remaining `/ai/ > ē`), which is **dialect-specific**
  (splits Insular North Frisian from the rest).
- **merger of r < PGmc. z and r < PGmc. r precedes monophthongisation in
  Frisian**: OF shows no ā/ē difference by r-source, so "the merger of the two
  types of r had already taken place" before monophthongisation (p. 286).
  *(Contrast Nordic, §6.)*

### Shared / undifferentiated stem
Versloot does **not** posit a discrete shared Anglo-Frisian node. The only
element he places on a genuinely common (cross-language) footing is
**participation in wave 1** as an areal event; he treats OE and OF as adjacent
points on one diffusion cline, **not** as sharing a single pre-split change
with identical conditioning (pp. 316, 319; see §7, §10).

## 6. Absolute chronology

Kept strictly separate from §5. Dates below are Versloot's; the "steps"
column flags how far the dated object sits from the sound change.

- **Wave 1 before /h/, Nordic:** monophthongised in Early Runic **by ca.
  440 AD** (Table 1, *faih-/fāh* forms); Nydam II *ahti* (< *aih-ti-z*) dated
  **ca. 250–320** hints earlier; Versloot henceforth **operates with 400 AD**
  (pp. 294–295). *Dated thing:* runic inscriptions on datable objects →
  reading → phonemic interpretation → identification with wave 1 (several
  inferential steps).
- **Wave 1 before /r/ later than before /h/:** Rö stone *s[a]ir-* beside
  *fahido* (p. 295); OHG shows `<ai>` before r still ca. 700 (pp. 299–300).
- **Old English:** still diphthongal **in all contexts in the mid-5th c.**
  (*raïhan*, Caistor-by-Norwich, dated **ca. 425–475**); *āc*-rune first half
  7th c. gives **datum ante quem ca. 600**; therefore OE `ai > ā` in the
  **late 5th / 6th c.**, before i-mutation (dated late 6th c.) (pp. 295–296).
  *(3+ steps from object to change.)*
- **Old High German / Continental Runic:** `ai > æi` in the **6th c.** (mid-6th
  c.); monophthongisation before /h/ in the **late 6th c.**, **before the
  Consonant Shift `k > h` (7th/8th c.)**; before /r/ back to the **7th c.**,
  north→south; OHG ǣ > ē **not later than the beginning of the 8th c.**;
  `<ai> (=æi) > ei` from the north (Franconian) in the 8th c. (pp. 297–300).
- **Frisian (explicit staged chronology, p. 309):**
  - 1a) before ca. 600: PGmc. *au* > /aː/ [ɑː]
  - 1b) before ca. 600: PGmc. *ai* in velar contexts > /æː/ [aː]  (**= wave 1**)
  - 2) late 6th / early 7th c.: i-mutation; /aː/ and /æː/ + i-mutation > /εː/
  - 3) 7th/8th c.: colonisation of the North Frisian Islands
  - 4a) 8th c.: delabialisation of i-mutated long vowels
  - 4b) 8th/9th c.: monophthongisation of the remaining /ai/  (**= wave 2**)
  - Skanomodu (au > ā) late 6th c.; emigration = **datum ante quem for wave 1**
    (pp. 297, 307–308).
- **Low Countries onomastics:** monophthongisation completed **before 800**;
  Versloot finds only the monophthongised toponym form locally from **690**
  onward; Low German toponyms give **800 as datum ante quem** (pp. 300–302).
- **Nordic wave 2 (OEN ei > ē):** 10th c. in Denmark, spreading north into
  Sweden/Norway to the 16th c. (Haugen, Bandle) (pp. 293–294).
- **Whole process:** "covered 500 years", **ca. 400–900** (pp. 281, 319).
- **Table 4 (pp. 314–316):** a 7-stage diachronic reconstruction (*taihwō*,
  *stainaz* across ON/OE/OF/OS/OHG). **Defect:** the stage-header year ranges
  and several century numerals in the comments are **blanked out** in the
  extract (rendered "–:" / "th century"); the reconstruction's structure is
  legible but its per-stage absolute dates are not recoverable from this file
  alone (see closing summary).

## 7. Proposed shared innovations

- **Wave 1 (`ai > ā` in velar contexts)** is presented as a **single areal
  innovation** shared, as a diffusing event, by **Old Nordic, Old English,
  Old Frisian, and (in relics) Old Saxon / Dutch coastal** (pp. 288–289,
  292–293). For OE and OF specifically, wave 1 is **the same north→south wave**,
  dated to the 6th c. in both, "no reason to assume that wave 1 developed
  substantially earlier in Frisian than in English" (p. 316).
- **Wave 2 (`ai > ē` in remaining contexts)** is a shared innovation of the
  **central** languages **Old Frisian, Old Saxon, Old East Nordic** — but
  explicitly **not** OE (where it is inseparable from wave 1), and **not** OHG /
  OWN / OLF (pp. 292–293).
- Crucially, Versloot regards "shared" here in an **areal / Sprachbund sense**,
  not as a single inherited node: he argues the common-origin-vs-independent
  dichotomy is the wrong question (pp. 317, 319).

## 8. Proposed independent developments

- Versloot **rejects the framing** that OE and OF monophthongisations are two
  fully independent developments (Nielsen's position; Campbell 1977: 53 on
  different outcomes) — pp. 317–318. So he offers **few** "independent"
  claims; his move is to dissolve the dichotomy.
- Where he does separate things:
  - **`ai`-monophthongisation is decoupled from the fronting of short `a`**
    ("Anglo-Frisian fronting"): he argues the OE *stān*/*read* vs OF
    *stēn*/*rād* contrast does **not** require linking diphthong-monophthong-
    isation to isolated-vowel fronting; "I see ample reasons to free the
    discussion … from this link" (pp. 289–290). *(Directly relevant to CAPR's
    SC004 vs SC043 boundary — see §10.)*
  - **`ai` and `au` monophthongisations are decoupled** (against the
    "monolithic/symmetrical" assumption of Hofmann and Kümmel): they need not
    be one process at one date (pp. 307–309, 318).
  - **Wave 2 in Insular North Frisian vs the other Frisian dialects** is
    **dialect-specific** (post-migration), i.e. an independent daughter-branch
    event, even though wave 1 is common-Frisian (pp. 307–308).

**Statistical / quantitative method (explicit; treat as a distinct, weak
evidence type for tree purposes — see §10).**

- **Old Frisian (§5.1, pp. 302–305):** dataset of **105 lemmas** with fairly
  certain PGmc. *ai* etymology. Partition: 13 shortened (out of scope), 29 with
  i-mutation (all → ē), **62 without i-mutation** → of these 31 only ē, 20 only
  ā, 11 both. Reformulated rules and hit-rates (words with both counted as
  half): **rule 1** (before `-Cu-, -CwV-, -Cō-`) ā in **68%** (n=20); **rule 2**
  (before g, h) ā in **79%** (n=7); overall ā among no-i-mutation lemmas **41%**.
  The two rules together explain **19/31 (61%)** of the ā items. Figure 3
  cross-tabulates coda × following (semi)vowel: h/g **79%**, k/r **50%**, rest
  **29%**; a/Ø **29%**, ō **54%**, u/w **100%**. **What is counted:** the
  proportion of lemmas showing ā (vs ē) per phonological context. **What it can
  establish:** that "velar" conditioning is statistically favoured for ā and
  that the conditioning is gradient (lexical-diffusion-like; Kiparsky 2003:314,
  cited p. 305). **What it cannot establish:** any absolute date, any ordering
  edge, or that OE and OF share a *single* change — it is a synchronic
  distributional argument over Frisian lexica, with 39% of ā items unexplained
  by the rules.
- **Old Saxon (§5.2, pp. 310–311):** **27 tokens / 20 lemmas** (Gallée), reduced
  to **18 clean items** for **Table 2**; the velar/non-velar × `<a,æ>`/`<ei>`
  contrast is significant (**Fisher's Exact Test, 2-sided, p = 0.029**).
  Versloot himself: "The amount of evidence is too limited (only 27 tokens) for
  firm conclusions to be based on it alone" (p. 311). **Defect:** the Table 2
  cell counts are blanked in the extract; only the p-value survived.

## 10. Tree-test implications

Applying the criterion in `../README.md`. This section is a statement about
**CAPR's formalism**, not a criticism of Versloot.

**Which alleged Anglo-Frisian innovations could still occupy a shared node?**

- **Wave 1 velar-context `ai > ā`** is the only candidate. It is dated by
  Versloot to the 6th c. in *both* OE and OF, before i-mutation in both, and no
  demonstrably OE- or OF-specific change is shown to precede it. As a **narrow,
  velar-context** change it is therefore *compatible* with a shared pre-split
  node (a genuine `H` for the velar subset only). Versloot himself would frame
  this areally rather than as a node (pp. 316, 319).

**Which correspondences would have to be independent/parallel under a tree
model?**

- The **OE unconditioned generalisation** of `ai > ā` to *all* contexts and the
  **OF wave-2** `ai > ē` are, on Versloot's own account, **not** the same change:
  OE has ā everywhere in one inseparable step (p. 293), whereas OF keeps the
  ā/ē split with wave 2 being **dialect-specific and possibly post-migration**
  (pp. 307–308). Under the CAPR tree test this is exactly the decisive
  configuration: the OF ē outcome sits **after** Frisian-specific delabialisation
  (Frisian-only Y precedes it), while OE has no such stage — so the *all-context*
  identity of the OE and OF outcomes cannot be one inherited innovation. The
  surface identity "OE and OF both lose the diphthong" is `S`; only the velar
  subset is a candidate `H`; the remainder is `P`/`U`.
- Because the **conditioning differs** (OE unconditioned vs OF velar-restricted
  in wave 1), even wave 1 is a shared node **only** at the velar-context grain;
  OE's later generalisation is a daughter-specific extension.

**Tree-usable ordering edges this source supplies (consumable by CAPR):**

- OE: `ai>ā < i-mutation` (p. 296).
- OF: `syncope < ai>ā < i-mutation < delabialisation < ai>ē(wave2)`
  (pp. 302, 305–309).
- General: `ai>æi < æi>ē` (p. 298); within wave 1 `before-h < before-r`
  (pp. 295, 312, 299).
- OHG (not OE/OF): `monophthongisation-before-h < Consonant Shift k>h` (p. 300).
- Nordic (not OE/OF): `monophthongisation-before-r < rhotacism z>r` (p. 285);
  in Frisian the reverse timing (`z/r-merger < monophthongisation`, p. 286) —
  a language-specific edge, not transferable to OE.
- Frisian `ai>ā < i-mutation` is one edge CAPR's SC004↔i-mutation ordering can
  cite as parallel external support for the OE edge already noted in
  `../../004-pwgmc-ai-monophthongization.dossier.md`.

**Where the author invokes wave/continuum/linkage in place of an ordering
argument (recorded neutrally — these supply *no* tree edge):**

- The entire cross-language "sharedness" of wave 1 and wave 2 is argued as
  **areal diffusion over a dialect continuum / Sprachbund** (pp. 283, 288–293,
  317, 319), explicitly invoking Haspelmath (2001) and Voyles's "areal changes".
- **North→south (wave 1) and south→north (`ai>æi`) directional spread** (pp.
  292–293) are geographical statements, not before/after-split edges.
- **"Wave 2 did not reach OHG/OWN"** and the **west→east Frisian cline** (pp.
  293, 304–306) are areal-extent statements; they do not order changes on any
  stem.
- Versloot's meta-argument that "similar developments following language-
  specific ones are [not] by definition not shared" (pp. 317–318) is precisely a
  rejection of the premise behind the CAPR tree test. CAPR records this
  faithfully but, per `../README.md`, cannot consume it as an escape hatch: for
  CAPR's own adjudication, only the within-language edges above are usable.

**Relation to CAPR's SC004 (`../../004-pwgmc-ai-monophthongization.dossier.md`).**
Versloot **agrees** with SC004's core framing: stressed/root `*ái > *ā` is a
North Sea Germanic **areal** development, not a discrete inherited Proto-Anglo-
Frisian node, with OE the broadest monophthongiser (SC004 already cites him to
this effect). Both agree OE ā < ai fronts to ǣ **only via i-mutation** (p. 284),
not by general brightening — consistent with keeping SC004 distinct from SC043.
He also **reinforces** SC004's caution against tying `*ai`-monophthongisation to
the isolated-`a` fronting of SC043 (pp. 289–290). No direct conflict with SC004
was found; the one tension is philosophical — SC004 treats the areal placement
as an "operational modelling corridor" within a tree cascade, whereas Versloot
would deny the tree question is well-posed at all (p. 319).

## 11. Uncertainty grading

**Firmly demonstrated (data compel):**
- Early Runic monophthongisation **before /h/** by ca. 440 (Table 1) (p. 294).
- OE still diphthongal in all contexts mid-5th c. (*raïhan*) and monophthongal
  by ca. 600 (*āc*-rune) (pp. 295–296).
- OF `ai > ā` predates OF i-mutation (pp. 302, 305–306).
- The OF ā/ē lexical split is (almost) uniform across Frisian dialects and is an
  old, pan-Frisian marker (pp. 285–286).

**Author's preferred reconstruction (coherent; alternatives exist and he knows
it):**
- The **two-wave** analysis itself and the crossing `ai > æi` trend (pp. 292–293).
- The Frisian staged chronology of p. 309 (reconciling Hofmann).
- Wave 1 = "velar" = a single phonetic scale shared with breaking/retraction/
  i-mutation-blocking contexts (pp. 312–313).

**Plausible but non-unique inference (evidence compatible, not decisive):**
- `<ae>` in Continental Runic = assimilation `ai > æi` rather than monophthong
  (Versloot's reading against Findell) (pp. 297–298); he calls the diphthong-
  shift "the most problematic and speculative part of this theory" (p. 290)
  and "impressionistic" for Frisian wave-2 onset (p. 315).
- OS two-wave interpretation from 27 tokens (Versloot: too limited alone,
  p. 311).
- The three readings of the west→east Frisian cline — he says "the limited data
  hamper a definite answer" (p. 305 n.21).

**Explicitly unresolved (author says open):**
- Whether OE also spread gradually through contexts (450–600) — "there is no
  evidence for this" (p. 316).
- Exact dating of `ai > æi > ei` in Old Nordic (limited value of Younger Futhark)
  (p. 315).
- The detailed treatment of shortened `æ < PGmc. ai` in Frisian "exceeds the
  scope of this article" (pp. 284–285, 319).

*Hedges preserved, not upgraded:* "most likely", "may", "seems", "impressionistic",
"probably", "the limited data hamper a definite answer" all appear verbatim in
the source at the cited places.

## 12. Implementation-ready extracts (Frisian)

**Preparation only. Do NOT implement.** No foma / corpus / TSV work is
authorised by this card. These are candidate Frisian cascade entries distilled
from Versloot's account (chiefly §5.1 and p. 309), to be adjudicated later
against the synthesis.

**F-1 — Wave 1: velar-context monophthongisation `*ai > ā`**
- input: PGmc. `*ai` (stressed, long; not shortened before cluster)
- output: `ā` (Runic Frisian /æː/ [aː], later merging with /aː/ < *au*)
- conditioning (velar), from Versloot's reformulation of De Vaan (p. 304):
  - **(nearly categorical)** before Proto-Frisian **-g-, -h-**, and before
    **-Cu-, -CwV-** (rounded/back following (semi)vowel);
  - **(common but variable)** before **-k-, -r-** (r < PGmc. *r* or *z*, already
    merged) and before **-Cō-**.
- scope: **common-Frisian** (all Frisian dialects); pan-Frisian lexical
  distribution (pp. 285–286, 305).
- approximate stage: **before ca. 600**; before Insular North Frisian migration
  (7th/8th c.) = datum ante quem (p. 309).
- ordering constraints: **after** syncope; **after** r(z)/r merger; **before**
  i-mutation; **before** delabialisation; **before** wave 2 (pp. 286, 302,
  305–309).
- evidence: *tāne* < *taihwō-*, *frāse* < *fraisōn*, *äh* (Westeremden B),
  *āskiath*; De Vaan rules + 62-lemma statistics (pp. 302–306).
- uncertainty: conditioning is **gradient/leaky** (rules explain 61% of ā items);
  treat as a *statistically favoured*, not exceptionless, environment.

**F-2 — i-mutation of the wave-1 output (`ā/*ai + i,j → ē`)**
- input: `ā` (< wave 1) and remaining `/ai/`, when `i, j` follows
- output: `ē` (/εː/); i-mutation "always turned a potential ā into ē" (p. 286)
- conditioning: `i/j` in the next syllable
- stage: **late 6th / early 7th c.** (p. 309)
- ordering: **after** F-1, **before** delabialisation and wave 2
- evidence: *lēra* < *laizjan*, *lēna* < *laih(w)njan* (pp. 286, 306)
- uncertainty: firmly demonstrated as later than F-1.

**F-3 — Wave 2: non-velar-context monophthongisation `*ai > ē`**
- input: remaining `/ai/` (non-velar contexts), i.e. `æi` stage
- output: `ē` (/εː/)
- conditioning: the **complement** of the velar set (all other contexts)
- scope: **dialect-specific** — affects mainland Frisian; **splits Insular North
  Frisian** off (which retains/relates `/ai/` differently); **shared with Old
  Saxon and Old East Nordic**, **not** with OE/OHG/OWN (pp. 292–293, 307–308).
- approximate stage: **8th / 9th c.**; possibly still in progress in 8th-c. Runic
  Frisian (*aib* beside *u[n]mædit*) (pp. 297, 309).
- ordering constraints: **after** delabialisation (4a, 8th c.); after migration
  (pp. 307–309).
- evidence: *u[n]mædit* / *aib*; the OF ā/ē doublets (*āth ~ ēth*, *lāre ~ lēre*)
  (pp. 286, 296–297, 305).
- uncertainty: onset dating "impressionistic" (p. 315); the split-from-INF claim
  is Versloot's preferred reconstruction, not compelled.

**F-4 — pre-condition: shortening `*ai > æ` before a consonant cluster**
- Versloot flags this as **out of scope** but real: `ai` shortened to `æ` before
  clusters (e.g. *flæsk*, *fætt*), some clusters arising only after **syncope**
  (pp. 284–285, 302). Record as a separate early Frisian rule; **do not** feed it
  into F-1/F-3. Detailed treatment "exceeds the scope of this article" (p. 319).

**F-5 — merger `ā (< *ai velar) + ā (< *au)`**
- the wave-1 [aː] < *ai* and [ɑː] < *au* were initially **distinct** (runic
  evidence) but **merged** into a single /aː/ before the earliest OF attestations
  (12th c.) (pp. 297, 306, 308–309). Order: after F-1; a late, low-confidence
  merger ("in fact an assumption", p. 306 n.22).

## 13. Dependencies on sources CAPR does not hold

Cross-reference `../missing-direct-sources.md` for current holdings.
Bremmer 2009, Nielsen 2001 and Goblirsch 1991 have subsequently been
acquired, but direct review remains pending; Stiles 1995 is still missing.
Per the standing rule there, Versloot's *report* of any of these is
evidence about **Versloot's reading**, not the source itself.

**Load-bearing dependencies and review status:**

- **Stiles 1995** ("Remarks on the 'Anglo-Frisian' Thesis") — the relative-
  chronology and continent-wave arguments Versloot builds on and partly opposes
  (pp. 282, 316–317). *Critical* absence per `../missing-direct-sources.md`.
- **Nielsen 1983 / 2000 / 2001** — the "independent in each language" thesis and
  the Early Runic dating Versloot leans on (e.g. Nielsen 2000:165 for the 400 AD
  date; Nielsen 2001:515–516 for rejecting Hofmann's OS split) (pp. 282,
  287–288, 295, 306–307). The 2001 chapter is now held, direct review pending;
  the 1983 and 2000 works remain missing.
- **Goblirsch on Germanic *ai/*au** — not cited by Versloot by name here, but the
  `*au` side of his de-coupling argument (pp. 307–309, 318) is exactly the
  material `../missing-direct-sources.md` flags Goblirsch for; needed before
  CAPR can test the `*ai`/`*au` symmetry claim. Goblirsch 1991 is now held;
  its direct review remains pending.
- **De Vaan 2011** ("West-Germanic *ai in Frisian") — the entire OF ā/ē
  conditioning that Versloot reformulates statistically (§5.1) rests on De Vaan's
  data and rules (pp. 286, 302–304). **De Vaan 2017** (*The Dawn of Dutch*) and
  **De Vaan 2012** underlie the Dutch-coastal argument (pp. 284, 287, 301, 305).
- **Kümmel 2014a/b** — rival reconstructions Versloot rejects (pp. 282,
  308–309, 318–319). Kortlandt 2008, also discussed there, is held directly;
  see card 03 rather than count it as missing.
- **Hofmann 1964** — the Insular North Frisian relative order (i-mutation <
  delabialisation < monophthongisation) that Versloot reconciles; his Frisian
  chronology (p. 309) is meaningless without it (pp. 306–308).
- **Findell 2012a/b** — Continental Runic corpus and the `<ae>` interpretation
  Versloot overturns (§4.4, pp. 297–300).
- **Waxenberger 2013** — the Anglo-Saxon runic datings (*āc*-rune first-half-7th-
  c.; *raïhan* ca. 425–475) that anchor the **OE** absolute chronology (§4.2,
  pp. 295–296). Any OE date here is only as firm as Waxenberger.
- **Boutkan 1995** — the unstressed word-final `-ai > -ē` Early Runic dating
  (out of scope but used to sever the fronting/monophthongisation link, p. 290).
- **Voyles 1992** — the "areal changes" framework Versloot adopts (pp. 282, 317).
- **Braune & Reiffenstein 2004** — all OHG datings (pp. 286, 297–300).
- Runic/onomastic corpora: **Looijenga 2003, Page 2006, Imer 2011, Krause 1966,
  Gysseling 1960/1962** — supply dated attestations not held directly.
  Bremmer 2008 is held (card 02), but a date reported there must still be
  distinguished from direct examination of its underlying inscription.

**Net dependency assessment:** Versloot's *within-language ordering edges* (§10)
are argued from forms quoted in-text and are reasonably self-contained. His
*absolute dates* (§6) and his *cross-language areal claims* (§7) depend almost
entirely on works CAPR does not hold (Waxenberger, Findell, Nielsen, De Vaan,
Gysseling, the runic corpora). They should be carried as **reported datings**,
not CAPR facts, until those sources are ingested.
