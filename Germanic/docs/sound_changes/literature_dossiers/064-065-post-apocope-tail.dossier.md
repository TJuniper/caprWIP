## Historical problem

`SC064-SC065` occupies the immediate post-apocope zone between the promoted
`SC063` **High-vowel apocope** report and the promoted `SC066-SC068`
**Syncope and degemination corridor**. That makes the pair structurally useful
even before it is historically settled.

The difficulty is asymmetry. `SC064` is the only member with positive
chronology-card evidence, and even that evidence is narrow: both sides come from
the same `fright` derivation. `SC065`, by contrast, is historically plausible as
a medial-syncope rule but currently contributes no positive first-break boundary
at all. The dossier therefore has to answer a chapter-shape question as much as
a source question: do the handbooks support a real two-step post-apocope tail,
or do they mainly support a broader weak-tail setting in which `SC064` is a
narrow bridge note and `SC065` is contextual lead-in material for the already
promoted `SC066-SC068` corridor?

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959
- source_key: Campbell1959
- locator: §§345--349; §§388--389
- witness_used: `docs/references/campbell_old_english_grammar.txt`
- locator_confidence: section_safe
- terminology: loss in final unaccented syllables; apocope; medial unaccented-vowel loss; syncope
- short quotation:

  > "w and ȳ ... were lost in Prim. OE, in final unaccented syllables after a long accented syllable, or a short accented syllable and another syllable."
  >
  > "Medial unaccented vowels are very freely dropped after short syllables, when the loss causes a group consisting of consonant + l or r to arise."

- paraphrase: Campbell gives strong support for the broader late weak-tail setting of this pair. He clearly distinguishes final high-vowel apocope from later medial-vowel loss and shows why the two processes belong in one neighborhood without being the same change. What he does **not** provide is a chapter-like account of a separate in-stem `n`-loss rule corresponding neatly to `SC064`.
- conditioning: final high vowels are lost under the familiar heavy/trisyllabic apocope conditions; medial vowels are lost in narrower cluster-producing environments
- chronology: Campbell's sequencing supports a post-apocope weak-tail aftermath followed by additional medial reduction, but not a fixed paired `SC064` + `SC065` chapter
- examples: `word`, `weorod`, `heafodu`, `betra`, `winstre`, `heolstor`
- cautions: Campbell is excellent for the late weak-tail framework and for keeping apocope and syncope distinct. He is much weaker as direct support for treating `SC064` as a broad standalone law rather than a narrow local note.

### Hogg 1992
- source_key: HoggPhonology1992
- locator: pp. 121--122
- witness_used: `docs/references/hogg_vol1.txt`
- locator_confidence: page_safe
- terminology: apocope; syncope; simplification in consonant groups
- short quotation:

  > "Apocope affected the high vowels /i/ and /u/ ..."
  >
  > "The high vowels were also subject to syncope in medial positions after a heavy syllable ..."
  >
  > "It seems likely that apocope and syncope were two quite different types of change operating at the same time ..."

- paraphrase: Hogg is especially useful for the structural interpretation of `SC064-SC065`. He treats apocope and syncope as neighboring but distinct late processes and explicitly notes that their interaction can produce contradictory-looking surface evidence. That strongly supports reading the pair as a bridge zone after `SC063`, but it does not by itself justify treating the pair as one settled two-rule handbook chapter.
- conditioning: apocope is weight-sensitive and final; medial syncope follows its own rhythmic/positional conditions; downstream consonant-cluster simplification can follow syncope
- chronology: the relevant losses are later than i-mutation and belong to the late reduction of unstressed vowels
- examples: `fet`, `word`, `scipu`, `weorod`, `heafodu`, `heafod`, `heafdu`, `cwidest > cwist`
- cautions: Hogg supports the broader post-apocope / post-syncope zone very well, but he does not isolate anything like `SC064` as a chapter center. His treatment is better evidence for neighborhood structure than for pair coherence.

### Ringe and Taylor 2014
- source_key: RingeTaylor2014
- locator: §§6.7.3--6.7.4; §§6.8.1--6.8.4
- witness_used: `docs/references/ringe_taylor_linguistic_history_vol2.txt`
- locator_confidence: section_safe
- terminology: general syncope of short vowels; other cases of syncope; apocope of short high vowels; consequences of syncope and apocope
- short quotation:

  > "After general syncope had run its course, short *i and *u were lost word-finally ..."

- paraphrase: Ringe and Taylor give the cleanest explicit staging for the whole local region: syncope first, then apocope, then further post-apocope consequences such as unstressed long-vowel shortening. They also discuss narrower syncope types such as the `nettle` material and downstream cluster results such as `spinel`, which is highly relevant for the right-hand context of `SC065`.
- conditioning: general syncope affects short vowels under late weak-tail conditions; apocope then removes final short high vowels; additional cluster and tail consequences follow
- chronology: the handbook chronology strongly supports the broad corridor `SC063 -> post-apocope tail -> SC066-SC068 -> SC072`, but it does not isolate a positive card-like boundary for `SC065`
- examples: `netele ~ netle`, `spinel`, `hilpst`, `strengþ`
- cautions: this source is a major reason to keep the pair visible between `SC063` and `SC066-SC068`, but its descriptive center is still the broader syncope/apocope sequence rather than a discrete two-change `SC064-SC065` unit

### Luick 1914
- source_key: Luick1914
- locator: §§304--306
- witness_used: `docs/references/luick_historische_grammatik.txt`
- locator_confidence: section_safe
- terminology: `Urenglischer Schwund von i und u`; `Apokope`; `Synkope` in offener Mittelsilbe
- short quotation:

  > "Im Auslaut schwanden i und u unmittelbar nach langer Tonsilbe, und auch nach kurzer, wenn darauf noch eine andere Silbe folgte ..."
  >
  > "Unter denselben Bedingungen ... schwanden auch i und u in offener Mittelsilbe ..."

- paraphrase: Luick is especially helpful for the historical logic of this dossier because he articulates the structural parallel between final apocope and open-medial syncope while keeping them distinct. That gives the pair a real historiographical backdrop as a post-apocope tail, but again mostly at the level of neighborhood and chronology rather than a ready-made handbook pair.
- conditioning: final `i/u` are lost after heavy syllables and in the relevant trisyllabic patterns; open medial `i/u` are also lost under analogous stress/weight conditions
- chronology: Luick's treatment strongly suggests that once the major apocope event has happened, additional weak-tail medial losses remain in play; this supports the structural location of `SC065` even though its chronology card is still negative
- examples: `flōd`, `word`, `werod`, `dēm`, `heafdes`, `dōgres`
- cautions: Luick supports the general tail sequence, but not a special `fright`-driven `SC064` chapter. The source is strongest for framing, not for making the pair symmetrical.

### Fulk 2018
- source_key: Fulk2018

- paraphrase: Fulk is useful mainly as comparative and chronological support. He keeps late medial and final vowel changes in one frame and confirms that the Anglo-Frisian syncope material is later than i-umlaut. That makes him good support for the general placement of `SC065` and for the weak-tail setting of the pair.

- chronology: late preliterary weak-tail reduction follows earlier umlaut-sensitive developments
- examples: `wine`, `bryd`, `egesa`, `hierde`, `deofol`
- cautions: Fulk does not provide strong direct evidence for pairing `SC064` with `SC065`, and he does not foreground a separate in-stem `n`-loss law of the `fright` type

## CAPR formulation versus literature

CAPR's present pairing maps onto the literature unevenly.

`SC064` is a very narrow formal rule: `NWGmcInStemNLoss` deletes stem-final `*n`
after `*ī` in the environment represented by the `fright` derivation, and the
current chronology card gives it real boundaries on both sides. The literature,
however, does not
seem to treat this as a broad named post-apocope law. The handbook tradition
mostly supplies the broader weak-tail frame, while Kroonen supplies the lexical
reality of the `*furht-*` witness family. That points toward a **narrow bridge
note** more readily than toward a full chapter center.

`SC065` maps more naturally onto handbook material, but only at a higher level
of abstraction. The grammars clearly support late medial syncope after the main
apocope zone. CAPR's actual `OEMedialSyncope` rule, however, is a much narrower
pre-dental slice of that wider historical phenomenon. The literature therefore
supports the *setting* of `SC065` better than it supports the specific rule as a
coequal chapter member beside `SC064`.

The pairing is therefore structurally plausible but not yet textually secure.
The sources support:

1. a real late weak-tail neighborhood after `SC063`;
2. a narrow lexical basis for the `fright` witness behind `SC064`;
3. broad historical support for medial syncope in the zone represented by `SC065`;
4. only weak direct support for treating `SC064` and `SC065` as one traditional pair.

## Chronology implications

The source tradition and the cards together suggest a cautious interpretation.

1. `SC064` has real positive card evidence across `SC041` and `SC072`, but both boundaries are carried by the same `fright` derivation.
2. The right-hand relation `SC064 < SC072` is meaningful because it reciprocates the already visible weak-tail chronology on the `SC072` card, but it is still narrow.
3. `SC065` has a historically plausible place in the late weak-tail region, yet its current card is entirely negative / boundary-limited.
4. No claim should therefore be made that `SC065` must follow a specific earlier rule or must precede `SC087`; the card does not show that.
5. The unresolved book question remains open: should this become a cautious paired post-apocope note, a narrower `SC064` bridge note, or a case in which `SC065` remains contextual/residual material between `SC063` and `SC066-SC068`?
