## Historical problem

`SC035-SC037` sits in an awkward but book-important stretch of the cascade. On
its left is the promoted `SC031-SC034` West Saxon diphthong corridor. On its
right is the promoted `SC039-SC040` medial-vowel core, followed by the
separate scaffold `SC041` final-loss note. The assembled book therefore needs
an eventual way to talk about three
ordinary FST changes here without pretending that the traditional grammars
offer one tidy chapter called "prefix and compound adjustments."

The internal asymmetry is sharp. SC035 looks like a narrow left-flank prefix
reduction rule with only one positive side, and that side points forward across
the non-local SC043 brightening pivot. SC036 is the strongest member: it has a
real two-sided chronology, source-legible unstressed-vowel material, and
historically interpretable `soul` / `world` derivations. SC037 is important in
the model, especially for compound outcomes like `rainbow`, but its live card
boundary is later than the technical marker SC038 rather than an ordinary
historical sound change. The resulting dossier question is not whether the row
matters, but whether the future prose should remain one cautious derivational
bridge or split around a stronger SC036 center.

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959

- **source_key:** `Campbell1959`
- **locator:** §354; §§338--339; §§356--357, 367, 386--387
- **terminology:** pretonic prefix-vowel loss; low-stress vowel retraction and umlaut in unaccented syllables; compound-second-element reduction; parasite connecting vowels
- **short quotation:** "`unaccented e is lost in pretonic syllables`"; "`weorold ... weoruld`"; "`second elements of compounds of reduced force`"
- **paraphrase:** Campbell is the clearest single handbook anchor for the whole row, but he supports it as a mixed unstressed-vowel and compounding region rather than as one classical chapter. In §354 he records early loss of unstressed vowels in pretonic prefixes, especially in reduced bi-/ni- forms, which gives SC035 a real historical analogue even though CAPR's exact `ge-`-side implementation is narrower [@Campbell1959, §354]. In §§338--339 he treats low-stress vocalism in forms such as `weorold` / `weoruld`, showing that unstable medial vowels between stressed syllables are historically real and not just model artifacts [@Campbell1959, §§338--339]. In §§356--357, 367, and 386--387 he then turns to obscured compound members, linking-vowel behavior, and reduced-force second elements such as `fultemian` beside `fultumian`, which provides the best handbook analogue for SC037's compound-juncture cleanup [@Campbell1959, §§356--357, 367, 386--387].
- **conditioning:** reduced pretonic prefixes; low-stress medial syllables; obscured or semantically weakened compound members; optional or unstable connecting vowels
- **chronology:** Campbell makes SC035 and SC037 historically legible only in broad structural terms. SC036 is the clearest beneficiary of his discussion because it sits directly inside his low-stress medial-vowel zone.
- **examples:** `binnan`; `nis`; `weorold`; `weoruld`; `fultemian`; `fultumian`; `cynegerd`
- **cautions:** Campbell does not supply a ready-made SC035-SC037 chapter. SC035 remains narrower than his prefix discussion, and SC037 is supported mainly as compound-juncture behavior rather than as a named standalone law.

### Ringe and Taylor 2014

- **source_key:** `RingeTaylor2014`
- **locator:** p. 245; p. 267; §6.3.3
- **terminology:** prefixed `*galaubijaną` / `*galaubidē`; unstressed-medial developments in `world`
- **short quotation:** "`PGmc *galaubijana ... > geliefan`"; "`*weraldu > *weruld ... > weorold ~ worold`"
- **paraphrase:** Ringe and Taylor provide the strongest comparative anchor for SC035 and a useful one for SC036. Their derivation of `PGmc *galaubijana` to OE `geliefan`, together with the past form `*galaubidē > geliefde`, shows that prefixed `ge-` verbs belong to a real unstressed-vocalic corridor rather than to a purely local CAPR convenience [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 245, 267]. Elsewhere they derive `*weraldu > *weruld > weorold ~ worold`, which supports the same historical space of unstable medial vowels and backs the `world` side of SC036's chronology [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.3.3].
- **conditioning:** unstressed vocalism inside prefixed verbs and in later medial syllables outside the main stressed foot
- **chronology:** This source does not make SC035 locally adjacent to SC043 or SC036 locally adjacent to SC040, but it does confirm that both rules live in real historical territory. SC036 in particular looks source-backed rather than merely computational.
- **examples:** `geliefan`; `geliefde`; `weorold ~ worold`
- **cautions:** Ringe and Taylor help most with SC035 and SC036. They do not turn SC037 into a normal handbook chapter, and they do not imply that SC035-SC037 is a traditional grouped unit.

### Hogg 1992

- **source_key:** `HoggPhonology1992`
- **locator:** §3.3.1.3; p. 99
- **terminology:** reduction in the variety of unstressed vowels; stress in prefixed forms and compounds; unstressed `ge-`
- **short quotation:** "`reduction in variety of unstressed vowels`"; "`ge- ... was never stressed`"
- **paraphrase:** Hogg is helpful less for direct chronology than for the stress architecture behind the row. His overview of Old English unstressed vowels stresses the gradual reduction of unstressed-vowel contrasts [@HoggPhonology1992, §3.3.1.3], while his discussion of word stress distinguishes prefixed verbs from prefixed nouns and notes that `ge-` was never stressed [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 99--100]. That makes SC035 and SC036 plausible as stress-conditioned vowel adjustments and helps explain why SC037 belongs in the same general derivational neighborhood: second elements of compounds carry secondary stress and can undergo their own reductions [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 99--100].
- **conditioning:** unstressed prefixes, weakly stressed medial syllables, and secondary-stress-bearing compound members
- **chronology:** Hogg supports the row as a real stress-sensitive region, but not as one neat chapter with tight local boundaries across all three members.
- **examples:** `gesceaft`; prefixed noun/verb contrast such as `angin` versus `anginnan`
- **cautions:** This is structural support, not a direct witness discussion. Hogg helps explain why the CAPR segmentation is plausible, but he does not by himself settle whether SC036 should later split out.

### Sievers-Brunner 1965

- **source_key:** `SieversBrunner1965`
- **locator:** §§167--168
- **terminology:** vowels in the composition joint; preservation or loss of linking vowels in compounds
- **short quotation:** "`Die Vokale in der Kompositionsfuge`"
- **paraphrase:** Sievers-Brunner gives a useful older-grammar corroboration for SC037. The grammar treats vowels in the composition joint according to the same principles as word-final vowels, with some vowels dropping and others surviving depending on stem shape and stress [@SieversBrunner1965, §§167--168]. That is not the same thing as CAPR's narrow compound-linking syncope rule, but it strongly supports the broader reading that SC037 belongs to composition-juncture management rather than to a large ordinary historical law.
- **conditioning:** vowel survival or loss at the junction between compound members, especially according to stem weight and the type of the first member
- **chronology:** This source supports SC037 as historically intelligible compound behavior, but not as a major standalone chapter. It is best used as secondary corroboration for a cautious or technical note.
- **examples:** `landfolc`; `cynedom`; `duruweard`; `beadurinc`
- **cautions:** Because the framework is older and more morphological than CAPR's rule inventory, this source is best used to justify the general type of SC037, not its exact chronology.

## CAPR formulation versus literature

CAPR sharpens a historically real but uneven region into three adjacent rules.

SC035 `OEPrefixAReduction` is the thinnest and most one-sided member. The
chronology card gives it only a rightward positive boundary at SC043, where
delaying the rule leaves `believe` as `ġealīefan` instead of `ġelīefan`. The
handbook tradition does make prefixal vowel reduction plausible, especially in
weakly stressed prefixes, but it does not present SC035's exact rule as a
chapter center [@Campbell1959, §354; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 245].

SC036 `OEInterStressRaising` is the strongest member. Campbell's low-stress
`weorold` / `weoruld` material, Ringe and Taylor's `*weraldu > *weruld >
weorold ~ worold`, and Hogg's general unstressed-vowel framework all show that
mid-word unstressed vocalism is a real historical issue rather than an ad hoc
CAPR trick [@Campbell1959, §§338--339; @RingeTaylor2014, §6.3.3;
@HoggPhonology1992, §3.3.1.3]. That makes SC036 a plausible prose center even though its
earlier boundary to SC019 is broad rather than locally adjacent.

SC037 `OECompoundLinkingSyncope` is historically intelligible but only in a
different register. Campbell and Sievers-Brunner both supply robust support for
compound-juncture vowel reduction, reduced-force second elements, and unstable
connecting vowels [@Campbell1959, §§356--357, 367, 386--387;
@SieversBrunner1965, §§167--168]. But the chronology card still finds no
ordinary historical positive boundary in either direction: the live later break
crosses technical SC038, and the `rainbow` relation becomes historically useful
mainly through the already promoted SC049 report to its right.

The practical result is a real derivational bridge with uneven source weight.
SC035 is a narrow prefixal flank note, SC036 is the best source-backed center,
and SC037 is best read as a technically important compound/juncture note rather
than as a normal chapter headline.

## Chapter-shape options

### Option 1: keep SC035-SC037 as one cautious derivational bridge

This remains viable and is the least disruptive reading if the row stays
unchanged.

- **Why it works:** the rules are adjacent, all three are ordinary FST changes, and together they form a real seam between the diphthong corridor and the later medial-lowering zone.
- **How it should be narrated:** SC035 as a thin prefixal lead-in, SC036 as the real historical center, SC037 as a technically important compound/juncture flank.
- **Main caution:** the report must admit that SC035 and SC037 are weaker and more model-shaped than SC036.

### Option 2: split later around SC036

This is the main alternative.

- **Why it works:** it matches the source distribution best. SC036 is the strongest source-backed member and has the cleanest two-sided chronology.
- **Possible shape:** a short SC035 prefix note, a central SC036 report, and an even shorter SC037 technical or implementation note.
- **Why it is not recommended yet:** the present task is dossier preparation only, not row splitting. The grouped scaffold is still useful, and nothing in the present source pass proves that three separate units would read better immediately.

### Option 3: treat SC037 as primarily a technical/compound note

This is likely the best reading of SC037 even if the grouped row stays intact.

- **Why it works:** the only live later card break crosses technical SC038, and the best ordinary-historical use of `rainbow` currently comes from SC049's left boundary.
- **How it should affect prose:** SC037 should probably be short, explicit, and honest about its implementation-heavy role.
- **Main caution:** this should not erase SC037 from the book. Every ordinary FST change still needs explicit prose somewhere.
