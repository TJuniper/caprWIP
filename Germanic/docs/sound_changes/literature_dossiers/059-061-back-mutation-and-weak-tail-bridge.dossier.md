## Historical problem

`SC059-SC061` sits at a real seam in the assembled half. On its left are the
promoted `SC055-SC056` umlaut core and the earlier `SC046-SC048`
restoration/nasal-tail corridor whose right edge already points forward to
SC059. On its right lie the promoted `SC063` high-vowel-apocope report, the
promoted `SC064-SC065` post-apocope tail, and the still-broad `SC069-SC078`
late unstressed-tail scaffold. The book therefore needs some explicit prose in
this region eventually, but not necessarily in the current grouped form.

The internal hierarchy is now much clearer than it was in the bare scaffold.
SC059 is the strongest member: it is a real back-mutation center with positive
boundaries on both sides. SC060 and SC061 are different. Each has one real
earlier boundary (`SC055 < SC060`, `SC023 < SC061`) and a later side that is
runner-bounded rather than historically fixed. The real dossier question is
therefore not "does this region matter?" but "should the eventual prose keep one
cautious late-vowel / weak-tail bridge with SC059 carrying most of the weight,
or should SC059 split away so that SC060 and SC061 remain separate one-sided
notes?"

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959
- source_key: Campbell1959
- locator: §207; §§248--251; §§345--349
- terminology: back mutation; West Saxon `ie`; loss of unaccented vowels
- short quotation: "back mutation of the latter was hence seldom possible"; "w and ȳ ... were lost in Prim. OE, in final unaccented syllables after a long accented syllable, or a short accented syllable and another syllable."
- paraphrase: Campbell gives the clearest direct handbook footing for SC059. His back-mutation section treats the process as a later Old English diphthongizing development before following back vowels and uses forms such as `heofon` and `leofad` to show why the rule is historically legible as more than a model artifact [@Campbell1959, §207]. His discussion of West Saxon `ie` and palatal influence is useful for SC060, but only as narrower companion material beside the main i-umlaut chapter rather than as a separate coequal law [@Campbell1959, §§248--251]. For SC061 Campbell contributes mainly the broader weak-tail frame: late loss in unaccented vowels belongs to the same general neighborhood, but he does not isolate a special chapter-like weak-tail nasal-loss rule corresponding neatly to CAPR's SC061 [@Campbell1959, §§345--349].
- conditioning: back mutation before following back vowels under West Saxon restrictions; palatal `ie` outcomes in narrower West Saxon contexts; later final-vowel loss under weight-sensitive weak-tail conditions
- chronology: Campbell makes SC059 historically clear as a later vowel change, keeps SC060 conceptually tied to the umlaut/palatal-vowel region, and treats the weak-tail losses behind SC061 as part of a broader late reduction sequence rather than a discrete named law
- examples: `heofon`; `leofad`; `giest`; `giefan`; `hierde`; `word`; `weorod`
- cautions: Campbell does not hand us a ready-made `SC059-SC061` chapter. His strongest support is for SC059; SC060 and SC061 remain narrower and more structurally dependent on neighboring chapters.

### Hogg 1992
- source_key: HoggPhonology1992
- locator: [back-mutation discussion]; pp. 111--114; pp. 120--121
- terminology: back mutation; i-mutation; apocope; syncope
- short quotation: "back mutation. This change has many parallels with the much earlier one of breaking"; "It seems likely that apocope and syncope were two quite different types of change operating at the same time ..."
- paraphrase: Hogg is most useful for chapter weighting. He treats back mutation as a real later diphthongizing development parallel in type to breaking, which supports SC059 as a legitimate historical center rather than as a purely technical seam [@HoggPhonology1992]. He also treats i-mutation as a major chapter and the palatal-diphthongal material as narrower and more controversial, which fits the dossier's need to keep SC060 conceptually linked to the already promoted SC055-SC056 report by cross-reference rather than by merger [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 115--116]. Finally, his discussion of apocope and syncope as neighboring but distinct late processes helps explain why SC061 belongs to the late weak-tail neighborhood without automatically becoming the core of a grouped bridge report [@HoggPhonology1992, p. 121].
- conditioning: later back-vowel-triggered diphthongization; front mutation under following `i/j`; later weak-tail apocope and syncope in separate but overlapping environments
- chronology: Hogg supports the broad sequence "earlier umlaut material -> later back mutation -> later weak-tail reduction", but not a symmetrical three-rule SC059-SC061 handbook chapter
- examples: `heofon`; `leofad`; `giest`; `giefan`; `weorod`; `heafodu`
- cautions: Hogg strengthens the dossier's structural reading, but he does not turn SC060 or SC061 into chapter-defining companions of SC059.

### Ringe and Taylor 2014
- source_key: RingeTaylor2014
- locator: §6.5.1; §§6.6.1--6.6.4; §6.9.4
- terminology: back umlaut; front mutation; palatal diphthongization; `dōn`
- short quotation: "Back umlaut of *e was much less restricted"; "`*mahtiz ... > WS *miehti > mieht ... > miht`"; "`*nahti ... > WS *niehti > nieht > niht`"
- paraphrase: Ringe and Taylor provide the sharpest technical split among the three members. Their back-umlaut discussion directly supports SC059 through forms such as non-West-Saxon `geofad` beside West Saxon `giefan` and Mercian `weofan` beside West Saxon `wefan`, showing that the back-mutation region is historically real even where CAPR's exact witness set is narrower [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.9.4]. The same source gives the cleanest direct support for SC060's `might` / `night` material by tracing `*mahtiz > mieht > miht` and `*nahti > nieht > niht`, which is exactly the sort of over-development the chronology card detects when SC060 is moved too early [@RingeTaylor2014, §§6.5.1, 6.6.1--6.6.4]. For SC061, Ringe and Taylor are most useful in a narrower lexical way: they discuss the development of `dōn` and the general reduction of older morphological material, which keeps the `do` witness historically legitimate without by itself yielding a broad textbook law for weak-tail nasal loss [@RingeTaylor2014].
- conditioning: back umlaut/back mutation before following back vowels; palatal-vowel and front-mutation developments around `miht` / `niht`; inherited verbal/morphological reduction in the `dōn` pathway
- chronology: this source strongly supports SC059 as the row's center, gives SC060 a real leftward tie to the promoted umlaut core, and leaves SC061 as historically plausible but much more witness-concentrated than chapter-central
- examples: `geofad`; `giefan`; `weofan`; `wefan`; `mieht`; `miht`; `nieht`; `niht`; `dōn`
- cautions: Ringe and Taylor do not imply that SC059, SC060, and SC061 form one classical three-step chapter. Their evidence instead supports one source-backed center plus two asymmetrical flanks.

### Fulk 2018
- source_key: Fulk2018
- locator: §4.7; §4.8; §4.13; §5.6
- terminology: front umlaut; back mutation; diphthongization by initial palatal consonants; later preliterary changes of medial and final vowels
- short quotation: "diphthongization by initial palatal consonants (which precedes front umlaut but not breaking)"
- paraphrase: Fulk is particularly useful as a comparative caution. He keeps back mutation and front umlaut as distinct historical phenomena and explicitly treats palatal-triggered diphthongization as chronologically tricky relative to front umlaut, which reinforces the dossier's claim that SC060 should not be folded backward into the promoted SC055-SC056 chapter even though its one real boundary is `SC055 < SC060` [@Fulk2018, §§4.7, 4.13]. His later-preliterary discussion of medial and final vowel changes also supports the broad weak-tail setting into which SC061 fits, but again without supplying a standalone law of the exact CAPR shape [@Fulk2018, §5.6]. On weighting, Fulk therefore pushes in the same direction as Campbell and Ringe & Taylor: SC059 is the most source-backed center, while SC060 and SC061 are better treated as narrower, chapter-shape-sensitive material.
- conditioning: front umlaut under following high front vocalic material; back mutation as a separate back-vowel-conditioned development; later medial and final vowel loss in the late weak-tail zone
- chronology: Fulk supports the larger sequence that makes SC059-SC061 a real bridge, but he does not flatten the three rules into one standard textbook chapter
- examples: `giefan`; `giest`; `sceap`; `wudu`; `deofol`; `wine`
- cautions: Fulk is strongest for comparative framing and chronology caution, not for the exact SC059 or SC061 witness set.

## CAPR formulation versus literature

The literature supports the row, but very unevenly.

`SC059` is the strongest match. Campbell, Hogg, Ringe and Taylor, and Fulk all
make back mutation or back umlaut historically real as a later Old English
vowel-development zone. CAPR sharpens that into one explicit rule with two real
boundaries, and the chronology cards show that both its left edge at SC048 and
its right edge at SC078 are historically interpretable. This is the clearest
case in the row where both handbook support and current card evidence point in
the same direction.

`SC060` is historically real too, but in a narrower and more awkward way. The
sources support the palatal / umlautal environment behind `miht` and `niht`, yet
they generally frame that material as a dependent right-edge or chronology
complication within the larger i-umlaut / palatal-vowel region rather than as a
chapter center in its own right. CAPR's `SC055 < SC060` result is therefore best
read as a local ordering articulation of material that the handbooks attach more
closely to the earlier promoted umlaut chapter.

`SC061` is the weakest direct match. The `do` / `dōn` witness is historically
legitimate, and the weak-tail setting is well supported in the handbooks, but
the sources do not present a textbook law called "weak-tail nasal loss" that
would obviously deserve equal billing beside back mutation. SC061 therefore
looks more like a structurally useful one-sided note than like a second or third
chapter center.

The grouped row is consequently best understood as an **architectural bridge**
between the promoted umlaut/back-mutation region and the later weak-tail
clusters, not as a stable three-way local core.

## Chronology implications

The cards and the sources line up on three practical conclusions.

1. `SC059` has real two-sided chronology: `SC048 < SC059 < SC078`.
2. `SC060` has one real earlier boundary, `SC055 < SC060`, and no detected later historical boundary before the current runner limit.
3. `SC061` has one real earlier boundary, `SC023 < SC061`, and likewise no detected later historical boundary before the current runner limit.

That means the row contains one true center and two asymmetrical flanks.

- **For SC059:** both boundaries are historically meaningful, but the rightward SC078 relation is broad/far and should remain a cross-reference to the later weak-tail region rather than a reason to stretch the chapter rightward.
- **For SC060:** the leftward SC055 relation is historically real, but it should remain a cross-reference to the already promoted umlaut core rather than a reason to merge chapters backward.
- **For SC061:** the leftward SC023 relation is historically real, but it should remain a cross-reference to the already promoted singleton note rather than a reason to turn the `do` pathway into non-local chapter architecture.

The row therefore does **not** behave like a reciprocal three-change corridor of
the `SC055-SC056` or `SC066-SC068` type. It behaves like one source-backed
bridge center with two one-sided notes attached to the same seam.

## Chapter-shape options

### Option 1: keep SC059-SC061 as one cautious grouped bridge

This remains viable.

- **Why it works:** the rules are adjacent, all three are ordinary FST changes, and together they make a real seam visible between the promoted umlaut core and the later weak-tail reductions.
- **How it should be narrated:** SC059 as the clear back-mutation center, SC060 as a narrower leftward-facing umlaut-side note, and SC061 as a narrow weak-tail/`do` note on the right edge of the bridge.
- **Main caution:** the prose would need to state openly that SC060 and SC061 are one-sided and should not be granted equal historical weight with SC059.

### Option 2: split later into SC059 plus SC060-SC061

This is structurally possible, but the current source pass only partly favors it.

- **Why it works:** SC059 is plainly stronger than the other two members and could carry its own report.
- **Why it is only partly convincing:** SC060 and SC061 do not yet form a clear local pair of their own. They are adjacent, but their strongest ties are outward to SC055 and SC023 rather than inward to each other.
- **Current judgment:** keep this as a live structural option rather than the leading recommendation.

### Option 3: promote SC059 as a singleton later and leave SC060 / SC061 scaffolded

This is the strongest alternative to the grouped bridge.

- **Why it works:** it matches the evidence profile most closely. SC059 is the only member with both robust source support and two real chronology edges, while SC060 and SC061 still read like one-sided notes whose main job is to remain visible in strict order.
- **Possible shape:** a short SC059 back-mutation note, followed by retained scaffold notes for SC060 and SC061 until a later decision clarifies whether either deserves separate promotion.
- **Why it is not mandatory yet:** the present task is dossier preparation only, and the grouped scaffold still serves the assembled half well while that human chapter-shape decision remains open.

### Option 4: do more source work before deciding

This is now weaker than it was.

- **Why it was plausible earlier:** the grouped row had no dedicated literature or book dossier, so the asymmetry could have reflected missing source coverage rather than real chapter-shape differences.
- **Why it is weaker now:** the main historical picture is clear. SC059 is the real center; SC060 and SC061 are real but weaker one-sided members.
- **Current judgment:** more source work could still enrich SC060 or the `do` pathway, but the blocking issue is now chapter architecture, not lack of basic historical support.

## Working recommendation

The row can remain scaffolded for now, but it is now dossier-prepared enough for
a human chapter-shape decision.

1. The next decision should be **architectural**, not a hunt for basic missing sources.
2. The strongest live options are now **Option 1** (one cautious bridge with SC059 carrying the prose) and **Option 3** (promote SC059 as a singleton later while SC060 and SC061 remain scaffolded).
3. **Option 2** remains possible if the project wants an intermediate split step, but the dossier does not find a strong internal `SC060-SC061` core.
4. **Option 4** is no longer the leading need: further source work is optional rather than required.
