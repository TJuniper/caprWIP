# SC003 West Germanic rhotacism — literature dossier

## Historical phenomenon

This dossier concerns the change of medial `*z` to `*r`, best treated historically as a **post-PWGmc West Germanic rhotacism** and represented in the inventory by `deer`, `hoard`, `learn`, `berry`, and `learn (3sg)`.

## CAPR rule

- change_id: `SC003`
- display_name: `Rhotacism`
- rule_name: `PGmcRhotacism`
- FOMA definition: `*z -> *r || EnglishStarVocalic _ ?`

5. `learn (3sg)`

## Source support found so far

1. Bammesberger states that Germanic `/z/` yielded `/r/` in intervocalic position in Old English, though it was generally lost finally [@Bammesberger1992, p. 39].
2. Ringe and Taylor treat the change as independent in Norse and WGmc, non-uniform within WGmc, and best assigned to the post-PWGmc period [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 52, 98, 102].
3. Crist distinguishes final `*z` deletion from rhotacism, places the deletion rules first, and argues that the merger cannot be inherited from Proto-Northwest Germanic [@Crist2001, pp. 104-106; @Crist2002, pp. 1, 4].

## Missing source support

1. The historical stage can now be described cautiously as post-PWGmc West Germanic rather than Proto-Germanic proper, but a more fine-grained account of how uniformly the change spread across WGmc daughters would still benefit from fuller source discussion.
2. Additional explicit evidence for exactly how broad the modeled vocalic environment should be would still be useful.
