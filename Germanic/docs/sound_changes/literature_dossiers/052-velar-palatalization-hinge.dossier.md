## Historical problem

`SC052` now stands alone as the clearest unresolved hinge inside the
palatalization/fronting region. The `SC051` singleton has already been promoted,
so `SC052` is no longer buried inside a mixed four-change scaffold. Its card is
better than ordinary residual scaffold evidence: it has a real earlier boundary
across `SC050` and a real later boundary across the already promoted `SC055`
chapter.

That still leaves the chapter shape open.

1. `SC052` may become a standalone velar-palatalization hinge report.
2. `SC050-SC052` may later become a narrow paired report if the source
   tradition supports that left-edge feeder strongly enough.
3. `SC052` may instead work best as a hinge note between the promoted `SC051`
   **sk-palatalization** report and the promoted `SC055-SC056`
   **umlaut-core** report.

The source task is therefore not just to confirm that velar palatalization is
real. It is to decide whether the handbooks support `SC052` itself as a
chapter-level unit, whether they naturally connect it to `SC051`, and whether
they give any real reason to treat `SC050` as more than a model-visible feeder.

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959

- source_key: Campbell1959
- locator: §170; §§190--197
- witness_used: `docs/references/campbell_old_english_grammar.txt`
- locator_confidence: mixed_section_safe
- terminology: fronting and palatalization of velars; `sk` as a special case; i-mutation
- short quotation: "`[sk]` is more prone to palatalization and assibilation than `[k]`."
- paraphrase: Campbell is useful here because he keeps the whole palatalization-and-mutation neighborhood visible at once. He treats initial `k`, `g`, and `sk` together in the palatal-consonant discussion, but he explicitly marks `sk` as the special and wider case. That is exactly the historical relation the dossier needs: `SC052` belongs to the same handbook zone as the already promoted `SC051`, but it is not identical with it. Later, Campbell treats i-mutation as a separate major vowel process, which supports reading `SC052` as a hinge between palatalization and umlaut rather than as a stray residual note. [@Campbell1959, §170; §§190--197]
- conditioning: palatalization is tied to front-vocalic and palatal environments; `sk` extends the same tendency more widely than plain velars; i-mutation is triggered by following `i/j` and affects the vowel system broadly. [@Campbell1959, §170; §§190--197]
- chronology: Campbell's organization strongly favors a broad sequence in which palatal consonant effects belong to an earlier consonantal zone and i-mutation is a later large-scale vowel development. That is compatible with `SC052 < SC055`, even if the card relation is narrower than the handbook chronology itself. [@Campbell1959, §170; §§190--197]
- examples: `cild`; `giefan`; `dæg`; `sceal`; `giest`; `ieldra`. [@Campbell1959, §170; §§190--197]
- cautions: Campbell supports the reality of the hinge region, but he does not naturally yield a ready-made `SC050-SC052` chapter. His evidence is best for `SC052` as part of the broader palatal-to-umlaut transition.

### Hogg 1992

- source_key: HoggPhonology1992
- locator: pp. 107--108; pp. 111--114
- witness_used: `docs/references/hogg_vol1.txt`
- locator_confidence: page_safe
- terminology: palatalisation of velars; `sk` as a parallel change; i-mutation / i-umlaut
- short quotation: "called /-mutation or i-umlaut"
- paraphrase: Hogg gives the clearest structural prose for the shape question. He treats plain velar palatalization and `sk` palatalization inside the same larger chapter, but still distinguishes the cluster as a parallel and broader change. He then moves to i-umlaut as a separate major vowel development. That is exactly the source pattern the dossier needs: `SC052` is historically real and chapter-capable, but any eventual report must avoid duplicating the already promoted `SC051` singleton on the left or the umlaut-core report on the right. [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 106--108, 113--114]
- conditioning: velars are palatalized in front-vocalic environments; `sk` behaves more broadly because the alveolar element reinforces palatalization; i-umlaut fronts back vowels and raises parts of the front-vowel system under following `i/j`. [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 106--108, 113--114]
- chronology: Hogg's organization places the palatalization material before the main i-umlaut discussion. That supports the general direction of `SC052 < SC055`, while still leaving room for CAPR's local boundary to be narrower and more witness-driven than the textbook chapter sequence. [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 106--108, 113--114]
- examples: `ceosan`; `disc`; `ceaster`; `giefan`; `giest`; `bryd`; `trymman`. [@HoggPhonology1992, pp. 106--108, 113--114]
- cautions: Hogg is strong support for `SC052` as a real handbook process, but he does not give any special reason to yoke it directly to Sievers-law material as a coequal pair.

### Ringe and Taylor 2014

- source_key: RingeTaylor2014
- locator: §6.4.1; §6.5.1; §§6.6.1--6.6.4
- witness_used: `docs/references/ringe_taylor_linguistic_history_vol2.txt`
- locator_confidence: section_safe
- terminology: palatalization of velars; phonologization of palatal consonants; front mutation
- short quotation: "After initial velars and *sk had been palatalized ..."
- paraphrase: Ringe and Taylor are the strongest single source for the central claim of this dossier. They treat plain velar palatalization as a distinct stage, keep `*sk` close to it but not identical with it, and then move on to later palatal-triggered vowel effects and front mutation. Most important of all, they state that the new palatals became surface-contrastive when i-umlaut created new cases after non-palatalized velars, and they reject the idea that medial velar palatalization happened later than i-umlaut. That gives powerful historical backing to the right edge of `SC052`: the card relation `SC052 < SC055` is narrow, but it is not arbitrary. [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.4.1; §6.5.1; §§6.6.1--6.6.4]
- conditioning: `k` and `g` are palatalized in front-vocalic and `j`-adjacent environments; `sk` follows a related but partly broader pattern; front mutation is a following-high-front-vocalic change in the vowel system. [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.4.1; §6.5.1; §§6.6.1--6.6.4]
- chronology: this source gives the dossier its clearest historical spine. Velar palatalization belongs to the earlier palatalization zone, while i-umlaut is later enough to create contrasts with non-palatalized velars. That is the strongest source-based support for narrating `SC052` as a hinge before `SC055`. [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.4.1; §6.5.1; §§6.6.1--6.6.4]
- examples: `weccan`; `licgan`; `lecgan`; `secg`; `ecg`; `wicg`; `brycg`; `giefan`; `giest`; `bryd`; `wyrm`. [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.4.1; §6.5.1; §§6.6.1--6.6.4]
- cautions: Ringe and Taylor support the hinge strongly, but they still discuss the region as a broad palatalization-and-mutation system rather than as a self-evident modern chapter with exactly CAPR's present rule borders.

### Fulk 2018

- terminology: palatal or velar consonant contrast; front mutation; diphthongization by initial palatal consonants

- paraphrase: Fulk is the best short cautionary source for the right edge. He gives compact comparative support for front mutation as a major historical process, but he also reminds us that palatal-triggered vowel effects stand in a different chronology. - chronology: Fulk supports a real structural separation between palatalization-side developments and front mutation. That does not by itself prove the exact local CAPR ordering, but it strongly supports the idea that `SC052` belongs on the left side of the umlaut core rather than inside it. [@Fulk2018, §4.7; §4.13]

### Adamczyk 2001

- source_key: Adamczyk2001
- locator: pp. 61--62; pp. 66--67
- witness_used: `docs/references/adamczyk_2001_oe_reflexes_sievers.txt`
- locator_confidence: article_page_approx
- terminology: Sievers' law; heavy vs. light stems; relation to apocope and syncope
- short quotation: "Sievers' law is dated to early Proto-Germanic ... whereas apocope (presumably followed by syncope) somewhat later"
- paraphrase: Adamczyk is the clearest source for keeping `SC050` in proportion. She treats Old English reflexes of Sievers' law as part of an older prosodic and morphological alternation whose interaction with apocope and syncope is already historically complicated before the specifically Old English palatalization zone comes into view. That supports using `SC050` as a real left-edge feeder in CAPR, but it does **not** support treating Sievers-law syncope and velar palatalization as a traditional textbook pair. [@Adamczyk2001]
- conditioning: the alternation depends on heavy versus light stem structure and on whether `*-iV-` or `*-iiV-` survives into later Old English reflexes. [@Adamczyk2001]
- chronology: Adamczyk is valuable precisely because her chronology is older and more prosodic than the palatalization chapters. It argues against folding `SC050` automatically into the same historical chapter as `SC052` just because `stretch` shows a local ordering relation. [@Adamczyk2001]
- examples: `fremman`; `hrissan`; `trymman`; `secan`; `fedan`; `cypan`; `nerian`. [@Adamczyk2001]
- cautions: this is a specialist article on Sievers-law reflexes, not a direct source on plain velar palatalization. It should be used to limit `SC050`'s chapter claims, not to inflate them.

## CAPR formulation versus literature

CAPR isolates `OEVelarPalatalization` more sharply than most handbook
presentations do.

In the live FOMA rule, `SC052` palatalizes `*k` and `*g` before front vowels,
before `*j`, and in a few front-vowel-adjacent positions that preserve the
historical contrast system CAPR wants to test. That segmentation is useful for
order testing because it lets the model separate:

1. `SC050` **Sievers Law Syncope** as a left-edge feeder;
2. `SC051` **OE Sk Palatalization** as the already promoted cluster rule;
3. `SC052` **OE Velar Palatalization** as the plain-velar hinge;
4. `SC055` **OE I Umlaut** as the next major vowel chapter.

The literature broadly supports that analytical split, but unevenly.

1. The handbooks strongly support a real plain-velar palatalization process corresponding to `SC052`.
2. They also usually discuss `SC051` and `SC052` together inside a larger palatalization complex rather than as totally separate textbook chapters.
3. They treat i-umlaut separately and later enough to make `SC052`'s right-hand hinge role historically legible.
4. They do **not** naturally pair Sievers-law material with velar palatalization as one historical chapter.

That makes the most literature-aligned reading of CAPR straightforward:
`SC052` is chapter-capable, `SC051` is its nearest promoted neighbor, `SC055` is
its promoted right context, and `SC050` is mainly a model-order feeder unless
future book architecture decides the local `stretch` relation is worth a paired
note.

## Chronology implications

The cards and the sources point in the same general direction.

1. `SC050 < SC052`, with `stretch`, is a real local dependency.
2. `SC052 < SC055`, with `cow` and `lung`, is also a real local dependency.
3. `SC050`'s earlier side is runner-limited and must not be overread as a positive historical boundary.
4. `SC052`'s boundaries are narrow but historically interpretable, because the sources broadly place palatalization before umlaut and do not treat the right edge as arbitrary.
5. `SC057` remains an unresolved later palatalization-side question: its card depends on `SC052`, but that does not yet settle whether it belongs in any future SC052 chapter or only as a cross-reference.

The most important restraint is on the left edge. `stretch` is enough to justify
keeping `SC050` visible next to `SC052`, but not enough by itself to turn the
pair into a settled historical chapter.
