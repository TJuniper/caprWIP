# SC021 retirement: alleged Northwest Germanic unstressed \emph{*o}-raising

## SC021. Retired PNWGmc unstressed \emph{*o}-raising (`RetiredPNWGmcUnstressedORaising`) {#rule-RetiredPNWGmcUnstressedORaising}

SC021 formerly modeled an unstressed short \emph{*o} > \emph{*u} before a
following \emph{*u} as a Proto-Northwest-Germanic sound change. It is
**retired** and no longer has an executable Foma definition or a place in the
historical cascade.

Fulk presents the \emph{*-onų > *-unų} analysis as a traditional account, but
explicitly notes its contested status [@Fulk2018, pp. 88--89, §5.5; p. 170,
§7.31]. Ringe and Taylor reject a general PNWGmc change: early Runic
\emph{-on-} and OS/OHG genitive plural \emph{-ōno} retain the vowel, while the
Northumbrian relics allow analogical alternatives [@RingeTaylor2014,
pp. 62--65, 164]. In particular, *galgu* cannot securely establish the
alleged rule [@Bammesberger1990, p. 169; @Kroonen2013, p. 165].

Traditional van Helten raising instead concerns **long** unstressed \emph{*ō}
before following \emph{*u}, a different claim [@StauslandJohnsen2015,
pp. 22--23]. Stausland Johnsen's examination of the Hatton 20 ō-verb pasts
rejects that conditioning and establishes a different late pre-Old-English
distribution: shortened \emph{*o} from original \emph{*ō} gives \emph{u} in
medial syllables and \emph{a} in final syllables [@StauslandJohnsen2015,
pp. 24--25, 28--31]. That demonstrated West Saxon ō-verb development is
implemented separately as SC099 and SC100; its scope is not extended to the
Northumbrian nominal relics [@StauslandJohnsen2015, p. 36].

The full evidence review, the treatment of *galgu*, *foldu*, *eorðu*, and OHG
*zungūn*, and the successor-rule chronology are recorded in
`sc021-adjudication.md`.
