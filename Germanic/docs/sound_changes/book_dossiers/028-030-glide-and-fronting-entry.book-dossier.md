> Four assumptions about SC028 in the text below are now known to be wrong.

> 1. **It is not a "kind of `x`-loss" in Campbell's broad sense.** The rule is the
>    narrow simplification of *xs* before a further consonant (Campbell §417
>    p. 170). The §461 citation with `flēam` and `hēla` conflated it with a
>    different change, the loss of *x* between a vowel and a voiced consonant
>    with compensatory lengthening (Campbell §461 p. 186, Bülbring §528 p. 215).
> 2. **It is not Northwest Germanic.** Gothic retains the *h* of the cluster
>    (`bi-niuhsjan`, `saihsta`), so the change is post-Proto-Germanic; Ringe and
>    Taylor pp. 157-158 read it as northern West Germanic and allow that the
>    North Germanic agreement is partly parallel drift.
> 3. **Its chronology is not merely "negative" for want of searching.** The
>    negative card is now explained: the sole witness `fist` is overdetermined,
>    reaching the same surface form under either order relative to SC103, so no
>    displacement probe can discriminate. The chronology is carried by
>    comparative evidence instead, which bounds the change after the PWGmc
>    *-CijV-* syncope and before breaking.
> 4. **It no longer belongs to this editorial row.** SC028 has been restaged and
>    moved out of the Ingvaeonic corridor into the northern West Germanic region
>    of the cascade. Its former adjacency to SC029 and SC030 was an artifact of
>    the old executable placement, not a historical grouping, so the "entry
>    bridge" question below should be reopened for SC029-SC030 alone.

> 5. **SC029 is not "glide formation".** It is the reversal of the West Germanic
>    gemination of *w* before *j*, restoring the diphthong: PNWGmc \*awj > PWGmc
>    \*[aw'w'] > pre-OE \*[auj] (Ringe and Taylor p. 53 §3.1.3, "it was
>    reversible"; Campbell §120.2 p. 46).
> 6. **SC030 is not a separate Old English fronting.** It is Anglo-Frisian
>    brightening applied to the first element of *au*, restricted to English
>    (Campbell §132 p. 52 lists "West Gmc. a > OE æ; West Gmc. au > OE æu" as one

>    raised below is answered: the adjacency of SC029 and SC030 to SC031-SC034 is
>    editorial, and their historical home is the brightening stage.

## 1. Role in the book

This row is the compact entry into the early Old English fronting and
diphthongal zone. It sits immediately after the promoted `SC026-SC027` nasal
spirant corridor and immediately before the promoted `SC031-SC034` West Saxon
diphthong chain, so the book needs some local prose here even if the final
chapter shape remains unsettled.

The internal hierarchy is no longer mysterious. SC028 is historically legible
as a kind of `x`-loss, but the chronology card remains negative on both sides.
SC029 and SC030 are stronger: the reciprocal `hay` / `strew` evidence gives the
row one real local core, and SC030 then hands directly forward into SC032. The
editorial question is therefore whether the future book should keep one cautious
three-change entry bridge, or later split SC028 off as a short boundary note
while SC029-SC030 carries most of the prose.

## 3. Traditional description and literature

The literature supports the row unevenly rather than uniformly. Campbell gives
direct support for both sides of the architecture: he cites forms such as
`fléam` and `héla` as examples of loss of `x`, which keeps SC028 historically
intelligible, and he also treats the `awj` developments that yield forms such as
`hég / hig`, `ieg`, and `strégan` [@Campbell1959, §461; @Campbell1959, §120].
Ringe and Taylor sharpen the same picture comparatively by deriving `hieg` and
`strégan` through restored `auj` sequences, which matches the exact witnesses
that define the SC029 / SC030 reciprocal boundary [@RingeTaylor2014, §3.1.3;
@RingeTaylor2014, §6.1.2].

That source distribution already suggests the book's hierarchy. SC028 is
source-backed, but thinly bounded. SC029-SC030 is the real source-backed local
core. Luick's broader diphthongal discussion reinforces that the `awj` /
fronting material belongs to a genuine historical region rather than a model
artifact, but it does not force SC028 to remain a coequal chapter member
[@Luick1914, §116].

## 4. Formal implementation

In CAPR terms the row is a short three-step setup:

1. **SC028** removes preconsonantal `x` in a narrow earlier zone.
2. **SC029** reshapes `awj` into the glide sequence that feeds the later fronted outcomes.
3. **SC030** fronts the surviving `au` material and hands the derivations into the later diphthongal corridor.

That formulation is useful precisely because it is sharper than the handbooks.
The grammars describe a broader neighborhood of `x`-loss, glide behavior, and
fronted diphthongal outcomes; CAPR makes those tendencies explicit as adjacent
rule stages.

## 5. Place in the cascade

`SC028-SC030` sits immediately after `SC026-SC027` and immediately before the
promoted `SC031-SC034` report. That is the main reason the row matters
editorially: it keeps the passage from the nasal-spirant corridor into the
diphthongal chain explicit rather than letting the assembled half jump across

The outward links should remain restrained:

## 6. Order-testing evidence

- SC028 has no positive earlier boundary; the search stops at bundled `PWGmcChanges`.
- SC028 has no positive later boundary before the current search limit.
- SC029 has no positive earlier boundary; that side is also runner-limited.
- SC029 must precede SC030, with `hay` and `strew`.
- SC030 must follow SC029, reciprocally with `hay` and `strew`.
- SC030 must also precede SC032, but that later boundary is a broader no-output handoff rather than a second local reciprocal pair.

The practical consequence is that SC028 is a negative or boundary-limited card,
while SC029-SC030 is a real local chronology core. If SC029 is moved later than
SC030, or SC030 earlier than SC029, `*xáwwją` yields `hauġ` instead of expected
`hīeġ`, and `*stráwjaną` yields `strauian` instead of `strīeġan`. SC030 then
hands forward to SC032 through a broader failure set that collapses derivations
rather than producing competing surface forms.

## 7. Interpretation for the book

The safest reading is that this row is an entry seam with one clear internal
center.

1. **SC028** should be treated as a short source-backed but chronology-negative preface.
2. **SC029-SC030** should carry the row if it is ever promoted, because they have both handbook support and the only local reciprocal chronology.
3. **The handoff to `SC031-SC034`** should be explicit, so the entry bridge does not duplicate the fuller diphthong-chain report.
4. **The bundled `PWGmcChanges` limitation** should remain a methodological note, not a historical opening boundary.

That means the next decision is not whether the row has any historical basis. It
does. The real question is whether the finished prose should expose the internal
asymmetry as one grouped bridge or as a later split.

### Literature and historical analysis

Campbell explicitly connects the first-element fronting to ordinary English
fronting; the early aeodbaldum/aeanberhti spellings support a front element
and a distinct offglide, not an exact absolute date
[@Campbell1939, p. 91 and n. 1; @Campbell1959, p. 52, §132].
Nielsen describes contemporaneity as traditional
[@Nielsen2001, pp. 514–515]. Luick connects the two while placing an
earlier offglide lowering in his own reconstruction
[@Luick1914, pp. 130–131, §119]. These support the working English episode
identity; choosing CAPR's atomic `aeu` is a modelling realization.

English identity is separate from the comparative stem claim. Under
daughter-English completed ai contraction before ordinary fronting, ordinary
fronting belongs in the daughter history. Campbell's Frisian au-contraction
mechanism and fronted-ai intermediate are qualified, not directly observed
[@Campbell1939, pp. 90–91, 104]. Goblirsch's proposed shared fronted au
with later Frisian continuation is compatible with a tree, but the Frisian
intermediate is not demonstrated [@Goblirsch1991, pp. 17, 20–21].
Kortlandt's restricted early fronting/later extension is a rival component
history, not a ready restriction inferred from cēapian/kāpia alone
[@Kortlandt2008, pp. 267–268]. Repanšek's separated diphthong and short-a
events remain an explicit rival [@Repansek2012, p. 82].

The proposed English co-occurrence is a weak identity/episode constraint,
not a strict chronology edge. SC029 < SC030 < au completion is an executable
feeding requirement for current inputs. No source-backed common-stem verdict
or new canonical edge follows from these firings.
