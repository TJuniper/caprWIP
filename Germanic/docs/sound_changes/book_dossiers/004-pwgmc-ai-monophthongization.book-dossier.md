### Historical target and alternative premises

Prefer the conventional English secondary-ā account: inherited-long
restructuring precedes availability of completed ai-derived ā. Serializing
the two is a computational realization, not proof that their durations
never overlapped ([@Campbell1939, p. 90; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 170]).
Campbell additionally places completed contraction before ordinary
short-fronting; the diphthong-nucleus premise remains explicitly contested
([@Campbell1939, pp. 90–91; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 170]).

The preferred comparative recommendation puts completed all-context
English contraction on the daughter branch. It is conditional on the
runic readings and interpretation, not on final-output success
([@Versloot2017, pp. 295–297, 318]).
It does not disprove a narrower ancestral onset or remove the AF node.
The inherited-front alternative and Luick's conjectural fronted-ai path
change the input/state premises, rather than merely moving this same
rewrite ([@Fulk2018, pp. 60–61, 72–73; @Luick1914, pp. 132–133]).
Nielsen's weak/co-occurring chain remains a separate alternative
([@Nielsen2001, pp. 514–516, 521]).
