## 3. Traditional description and literature

The literature dossier points in a consistent direction.

Campbell, Hogg, Luick, Ringe and Taylor, and Fulk all support the centrality of
i-umlaut. That is the chapter's secure historical core. The same sources also
recognize West Saxon palatal diphthongization, but usually as narrower,
dialect-specific, or embedded in a broader palatal chronology rather than as a
second coequal law with the same chapter weight as i-umlaut.

This means the eventual report should be confident about `SC055` and more
careful about `SC056`. The handbooks support a companion section, but they do
not automatically support giving both rules equal narrative billing.

## 4. Formal implementation

In CAPR prose, the pair is straightforward.

1. `OEIUmlaut` fronts and raises vowels under a following `i/j` trigger and
   supplies the main historical relation for outputs such as `cȳ` and other
   later umlaut-sensitive forms.
2. `OEWsPalatalDiphthongization` applies West Saxon palatal-triggered
   diphthongization on the right edge of that zone, yielding the local card
   relation seen in `gift` and `sheath`.

That is useful formal material for the book, but it needs one explicit caution:
CAPR's local `SC055 < SC056` order is not identical to the broad textbook
chronology for all palatal-diphthongization material.

**Evidence-led correction (research, not a production verdict).** Direct
checking now explains why the local card cannot date the entire historical
process. Fulk explicitly gives ordinary palatal diphthongization before front
umlaut (p. 74), and Ringe and Taylor give the same ordinary ordering but a
separate, later sc treatment of the ai-derived mutation product in sheath
([@Fulk2018, p. 74; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 215–217, 222, 235];
[@Campbell1959, pp. 68–69, §§184–185]). The preferred historical target
distinguishes those two layers; its executable realization remains unapproved.

The stored gift input is `*géftiz`, but the held second-edition Ringe explicitly
reconstructs PGmc `*giftiz` and places the relevant e-raising much earlier than

### Literature and preferred historical specification

Ordinary WS initial-palatal diphthongization follows breaking and precedes
front mutation [@Fulk2018, p. 74]. Ringe–Taylor explicitly derive
guest, kettle and cheese through PD before mutation and start the mutation
account after the earlier changes with a Mercian-second-fronting
qualification [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 215–217, 222].
Image-verified Luick independently states the immunity of existing
diphthongs and PD before mutation, while denying that similar Frisian
outcomes must be the same event [@Luick1914, pp. 162–163, §§174–176].

The ordinary target is:

```text
original stressed æ/ǣ/e/ē after initial qualifying palatal or inherited j
    > ea/ēa/ie/īe
breaking < ordinary PD < OE mutation
```

This target concerns vowel origin, quantity, stress and WS dialect.
It is not a claim that every contemporary palatal marker is already a
historical j phoneme.

Campbell separates a small sc glide class involving mutation products
from ordinary front-vowel PD, including sċēaþ beside sċǣþ;
Ringe–Taylor give later sc diphthongization of the ai-derived product and
surviving nondiphthongized examples
[@Campbell1959, pp. 68–69, §§184–185;
@RingeTaylor2014, p. 235]. Exact supported order:

```text
ai-derived ā --mutation--> ǣ --later sc treatment--> ēa (in its domain)
```

Alternate attestations do **not** specify optional lexical rewrites,
a blanket sc+ǣ rule, or an exceptionless conditioner separating every
matching/nonmatching case. That missing conditioner is a localized
**DEFER**, and blocks claiming a ready whole production decomposition.

[@Fulk2018, pp. 58–59, 61]. Ringe–Taylor warn that it occurred
hundreds of years earlier and that later repetitions are rare/doubtful
[@RingeTaylor2014, p. 220]. Current e-raising at SC055 catches up
the selected input; it does not refute ordinary PD-before-mutation.
