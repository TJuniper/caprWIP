# SC042-SC048 Post-brightening Region

## 1. Role in the book

This region sits immediately after the promoted SC043 Anglo-Frisian Brightening report and immediately before the promoted SC049-SC050 bridge. Its editorial problem is not whether the material matters, but how to keep strict chronology while converting scaffold bands into adjacent production units.

The answer is no longer a single chapter. SC043 already occupies the center of the region, so the old `042-044-045` scaffold cannot be promoted unchanged. The book instead needs a short sequence of adjacent treatments: a narrow SC042 context note, a likely SC044-SC045 pair, and then either a cautious SC046-SC048 corridor or a later split between SC046 and SC047-SC048.

## 2. Hard structural constraint

Grouped production reports may cover adjacent changes only. Non-adjacent links around SC043 must therefore be handled by cross-reference.

That means:

1. `042-044-045` is acceptable as a scaffold context band but not as a final production unit.
2. SC042 must not be bundled non-contiguously with SC044-SC045.
3. SC045's later relation to SC060 and SC048's later relation to SC059 must stay as cross-references rather than chapter-expansion triggers.

## 3. Region-by-region book reading

### SC042

SC042 is a narrow context rule rather than a chapter center. The `rest` derivation gives it a real local window, `SC020 < SC042 < SC043`, but both sides depend on the same witness. In book terms that makes SC042 useful as a brief prefatory note explaining one model-specific left edge of the brightening corridor, not as the center of a larger chapter.

### SC044-SC045

This is the strongest prose-ready pair in the region. SC044 OE Breaking is standard handbook material, and the chronology cards show that it must stand between SC043 and SC045. SC045 OE Velar Fricative Palatalization is narrower in naming, but the local evidence is excellent: the `fee`, `fight`, `flax`, `knight`, and `laugh` set makes `SC044 <-> SC045` the cleanest reciprocal pair in the post-brightening zone.

If one adjacent unit is promoted next, this should probably be it.

### SC046-SC048

This corridor is more mixed but still viable. SC046 has strong historical clarity as restoration/retraction after brightening. SC047 and SC048 are less standard as a named textbook pair, yet they form a broad reciprocal computational core through the large `-en` failure set. Book treatment therefore depends on tone:

- a cautious corridor chapter can keep the whole stretch visible in order;
- a later split could separate the more source-legible SC046 from a tighter SC047-SC048 nasal-tail pair.

The current dossier review does not force that choice yet.

### Literature and historical analysis

The working conventional sequence is ordinary fronting < breaking <
restoration < mutation. Campbell's slēan argument and restoration
environments are explicit [@Campbell1959, pp. 60–62, §§157–161];
Ringe–Taylor distinguish the stressed domain and later retraction
[@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 189–192]. Hogg's calan/galan constrain
restoration before effective palatalization under the conventional
conditioning, not uniquely an earlier fronting event
[@Hogg1979, pp. 90–91, 96].

Versloot's Standard Theory table distinguishes dialects; his rival includes
`a > [aˑ] > ea / _rC` and selective fronting with mutation before
particular breaking. Following back vowels, labials, lC, voiced nonvelar
rC, and excluded rn/rw cannot be discarded
[@Versloot2025, pp. 104, 107, 123, 126–128, 134–135].
The farmjan- > ferm- > feorm- analysis of feormat is a potentially
discriminating mutation-created-e case only if its etymology and reading
are accepted; it was excluded from the main tally. The cited
uuerci/smeruui/teru unbroken controls limit extension to rk/rw.
Compound/scribal alternatives also remain
[@Versloot2025, pp. 131–132]. These are narrow Anglian hypotheses,
not a mandate to move the whole WS A/E/I network.

Frisian e/i breaking to rising iu in closed syllables before tautosyllabic
χχ/χs/χt is not English rC breaking. Bremmer's weak medial-h-loss ordering
and separate intervocalic χ > h chronology must not be conflated
[@Bremmer2009, pp. 33–35, 37]. Hogg and Laker distinguish
voiced-fricative and h evidence [@Hogg1979, pp. 91, 96, 103–105, 111;
@Laker2007, pp. 176–177]. SC045's zero voiced census supplies no
experimental demonstration of their merger chronology.

For the h component specifically, Luick distinguishes early initial and
intervocalic weakening to h, whose coloring follows its surroundings, from
the retained preconsonantal/final fricative and its palatal treatment
[@Luick1914, pp. 835–836, §637]. CAPR still uses x for many initial
h inputs, so its 24 x-token applications cannot be read as 24 independent
historical retentions of velar [x] followed by one dated palatalization.
The bounded source target keeps (i) breaking while the relevant fricative
is an effective velar trigger, (ii) local palatal coloring/articulation,
and (iii) weakening/loss in their own position-specific domains. There is
no established single productive-cutoff or merger date for every h position.
**RETAIN the represented control; DEFER a changed h-layer law or chronology**
until the source-backed positional input and breaking-trigger assays are ready.
Neither the voiced-g merger nor SC057 supplies that missing cutoff.
