By A. CAMPBELL
ALTHOUGH I shall deal almost as much with Old English as
Old Frisian, I have entitled my paper 1 'Some Old Frisian
Sound-changes', because I shall have little to say that is
new about Old English. Since Siebs and others wrote what
are still the authoritative works on Old Frisian phonology,
knowledge of Old English has greatly advanced, and my
purpose is to see if this enables new light to be thrown on
Old Frisian. My paper falls into two parts. In the first,
I attempt to decide whether the results of certain early
Germanic developments, which were not uniform in all the
prehistoric West Germanic dialects, and which accordingly
occasioned diversity in the forms of the same word found in
the historic West Germanic tongues, are so similar in Old
English and Old Frisian that a very early Anglo-Frisian
linguistic unity can be assumed. In the second, I attempt to
discover whether the nature and the chronology of the
principal early Frisian sound-changes can be illuminated by
the undoubted tendency of Old English and Old Frisian to
develop their phonological systems on similar lines. I confine
